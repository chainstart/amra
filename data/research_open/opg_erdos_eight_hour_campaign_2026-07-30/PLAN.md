# Eight-hour proof plan

## Success contracts

### OPG-1757

The original conjecture says that for every finite graph \(G\), distinct
edges \(e,f\), and a uniformly chosen forest \(F\subseteq E(G)\),
\[
\Pr(e\in F\mid f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F).
\]

A full success is a proof of this statement.  A publication-grade
intermediate success must be an unbounded theorem with a complete human
proof, such as:

- a uniform Hall/injection theorem closing the first complete-split
  coefficient for all parameters;
- the entire \(\alpha^2\) layer for all complete-split graphs; or
- a new graph operation preserving the forest Rayleigh inequality and
  covering an infinite class not subsumed by existing
  series-parallel/symmetry results.

A bounded matching, another fixed-\(k\) polynomial, or an
outside-unstable local rule is not a success.

### Erdős #1083

For fixed \(d\ge3\), let \(f_d(n)\) be the minimum number of distances
determined by \(n\) points in \(\mathbb R^d\).  The conjecture is
\[
f_d(n)=n^{2/d-o(1)}.
\]
For \(d=3\), the current target is to improve the inherited
\(n^{3/5-o(1)}\) lower bound.

A full success proves the conjectured exponent.  A publication-grade
intermediate success must be an unconditional structural theorem which,
when inserted into the inherited proof tree, gives
\[
f_3(n)\ge n^{3/5+\varepsilon-o(1)}
\]
for some explicit \(\varepsilon>0\), or an independently substantial
inverse theorem whose hypotheses are actually forced by that proof tree.

A fixed-field obstruction, a conditional rank calculation, or a
finite algebraic-unit search is not an exponent improvement.

## Kill criteria

1. A local OPG replacement that merges a source boundary component
   without conditioning on the external partition is abandoned
   immediately: it cannot be universally outside-stable.
2. A proposed OPG proof preserving every fixed union/profile layer is
   abandoned if the existing \(4>2\) local deficit or the saved negative
   TP2 minor applies.
3. An Erdős construction in one fixed number field or a fixed-rank
   multiplicative group is abandoned once its parameter count is only
   polylogarithmic.
4. An Erdős incidence estimate is not retained unless the exponent
   ledger shows a strict gain beyond \(3/5\) after every inherited
   branch is reattached.
5. No result is called publication-grade before a primary-source
   novelty comparison and an independent proof audit.

### Erdős #1083

- The critical codegree was converted into a parameterized
  matching-or-hub theorem, and the Euclidean reverse-circle incidence
  theorem excludes hubs for every fixed \(\kappa<1/5\).
- A weighted dyadic treatment of merged reverse-circle classes
  strengthens the surviving-hub multiplicity to
  \(\mu\ge t^{(5-15\kappa)/2-o(1)}\) for \(\kappa<1/3\).
- One repeated circle-axis chart, and even any fixed number of
  concentric common-axis charts with different radii, has an exact
  linear-distance saturation model.
- Mathialagan--Sheffer's known two-circle classification was translated
  into the retained axial-chart coordinates.  Its perpendicular
  exception is impossible for retained off-axis reverse circles, so
  every nonaligned pair obeys the known bipartite expansion bound.
- The resulting high-rich concentration corollary proves that circles
  with at least \(t^{9/4+\eta}\) source incidences contribute at most
  \(MQ=t^{4+o(1)}=o(LH)\) weighted hub mass for fixed
  \(\kappa<1\).  Hence the difficult mass is forced onto many
  moderately rich circles.
- An exact feasible exponent ledger shows that the saved
  moderate-rich, pairwise two-circle, incidence, and plane-capacity
  constraints can all be saturated simultaneously on
  \(1/5\le\kappa<1/3\).  This abstract barrier identifies
  reverse-circle algebra or cross-plane slot correlation as the next
  genuinely new input.
