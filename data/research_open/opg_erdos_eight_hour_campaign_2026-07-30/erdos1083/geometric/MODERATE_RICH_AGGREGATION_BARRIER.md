# Erdős #1083: moderate-rich circle aggregation ledger barrier

Date: 2026-07-30

## 0. Result

The current moderate-rich constraints do not by themselves contradict
the critical hub ledger.  For every fixed
\[
\frac15\le\kappa<\frac13,
\tag{1}
\]
there is an exact exponent assignment that simultaneously saturates:

- the total triple capacity \(\mathsf T=MQL\);
- the strengthened multiplicity lower bound from
  `WEIGHTED_REVERSE_CIRCLE_DYADIC_REFINEMENT.md`;
- the weighted \(6/11,9/11\) point--circle term;
- the hub mass \(LH\);
- the high-rich cutoff \(s<t^{9/4+\eta}\);
- every pairwise Mathialagan--Sheffer product constraint; and
- the fixed-plane injective cap \(\mu\le M\).

This is an abstract exponent-ledger barrier, not a realization by an
actual Euclidean point set.  It proves that one more inequality or
geometric compatibility input is necessary.

## 1. The critical exponent assignment

Put
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
a&=1-\kappa
&&\text{(source incidences per circle)},\\
b&=\frac{7+11\kappa}{2}
&&\text{(number of merged circle classes)},\\
m&=\frac{5-15\kappa}{2}
&&\text{(multiplicity per circle)}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the model ledger has
\[
s=t^{a+o(1)},\qquad
N=t^{b+o(1)},\qquad
\mu=t^{m+o(1)}.
\tag{3}
\]

The total merged triple weight is exactly
\[
N\mu=t^{b+m+o(1)}
=t^{6-2\kappa+o(1)}
=MQL.
\tag{4}
\]
The weighted incidence mass is
\[
N\mu s
=t^{a+b+m+o(1)}
=t^{7-3\kappa+o(1)}
=LH.
\tag{5}
\]

The multiplicity exponent in (2) is precisely the new forced lower
bound.  On the range (1),
\[
0<m\le1,
\tag{6}
\]
so it also respects \(\mu\le M=t^{1+o(1)}\).  At
\(\kappa=1/5\) it saturates \(m=1\), and it tends to zero as
\(\kappa\uparrow1/3\).

## 2. Exact saturation of the point--circle term

Ignoring weights, the incidence exponent of (3) is
\[
a+b
=\frac92+\frac{9\kappa}{2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The \(6/11,9/11\) point--circle term for \(Q=t^3\) and
\(N=t^b\) has exponent
\[
\frac{18}{11}+\frac{9b}{11}
=
\frac{18}{11}
+\frac9{11}\frac{7+11\kappa}{2}
=\frac92+\frac{9\kappa}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the unweighted ledger sits exactly on that incidence term.
Multiplying dyadically by \(\mu=t^m\) gives
\[
\frac{18}{11}+\frac{9b}{11}+m
=7-3\kappa,
\tag{9}
\]
which is the hub exponent.  Equivalently, substituting \(m\) into the
second term of the weighted bound gives equality:
\[
\frac{72}{11}-\frac{18\kappa}{11}+\frac{2m}{11}
=7-3\kappa.
\tag{10}
\]

## 3. The other constraints

### Moderate richness

For (1),
\[
a=1-\kappa<1<\frac94+\eta.
\tag{11}
\]
Every model circle lies far below the high-rich cutoff.

### Pairwise two-circle bound

Place every abstract circle in its own alignment class.  Then every
pair is treated as nonaligned.  Equal richness \(t^a\) gives the
Mathialagan--Sheffer lower exponent
\[
\min\left\{\frac{4a}{3},2a\right\}
=\frac{4(1-\kappa)}3<3.
\tag{12}
\]
This is compatible with the critical distance budget.  The
same-centre disjointness condition is vacuous because each alignment
class has only one member.

### Target-plane capacity

There are \(M=t^{1+o(1)}\) retained target planes.  Give every circle
\(\mu=t^m\) distinct plane neighbours in a biregular abstract
circle--plane graph.  The average number of circle triples on one
plane is
\[
\frac{N\mu}{M}
=t^{5-2\kappa+o(1)}
=QL,
\tag{13}
\]
exactly the available \((q,d)\) slot count.  Assigning a distinct
slot to every incident circle respects fixed-plane injectivity.

For rational exponents, a genuinely finite biregular ledger is
obtained by taking \(t\) through suitable perfect powers and applying
the standard cyclic bipartite construction.  For arbitrary fixed
\(\kappa\), floors and ceilings alter only \(t^{o(1)}\) factors.

## 4. What this barrier proves

Equations (2)--(13) form a feasible point in the complete exponent
linear program generated by the currently saved constraints.  Hence
those constraints cannot force either:

1. two circles above the \(t^{9/4+\eta}\) threshold; or
2. a contradiction to the critical hub mass.

This does not construct a Euclidean point--circle configuration
saturating the incidence theorem.  A successful next step must use
information absent from the exponent ledger, for example:

- special algebraic restrictions on reverse-circle centres and
  radii;
- correlations among the \((q,d)\) slots across target planes;
- an incidence saving for this reverse-circle family; or
- aggregation of actual distance sets beyond pairwise
  Mathialagan--Sheffer lower bounds.

No distinct-distance exponent gain is claimed.
