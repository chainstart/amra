# Fixed-number-field terminal theorem for weighted chord charts

Date: 2026-07-30

## 0. Outcome

The rational-chart terminal argument extends to one fixed number field.
The infinite unit group does not create a polynomial fibre once every
archimedean conjugate of the scaled coordinates has polynomial height.

This closes the local arithmetic obstruction exhibited by the regular
pentagonal prism: its chord lengths lie in one fixed finite extension of
\(\mathbb Q\), even though they do not admit one rational scaling.
It does not prove that a general critical configuration can be extracted
into one common bounded-degree field.

## 1. A two-square representation theorem over a fixed field

Let \(K\) be a fixed number field, let \(\mathcal O_K\) be its ring of
integers, and put
\[
F=K(i),\qquad D=[F:\mathbb Q].
\]
Let \(r_F\) be the unit rank of \(F\).  For \(B\ge2\) and
\(0\ne n\in\mathcal O_K\), define
\[
\mathcal R_K(n;B)
=
\left\{
(x,y)\in\mathcal O_K^2:
x^2+y^2=n,\quad
\max_{\tau:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
\max(|\tau x|,|\tau y|)\le B
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

### Theorem 1 (fixed-field two-square fibre)

There is a constant \(C_F>0\), depending only on the unit group of
\(F\) (its logarithmic unit lattice and its finite torsion subgroup),
such that
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal R_K(n;B)|
\le
C_F(1+\log(2B))^{r_F}
\tau\!\left(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(n)|\right)^D.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, whenever \(\mathcal R_K(n;B)\ne\varnothing\),
\[
1\le |N_{F/\mathbb Q}(n)|
\le(2B^2)^D.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, uniformly in nonzero \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal R_K(n;t^C)|=t^{o(1)}
}
\tag{4}
\]
for every fixed \(K\) and fixed \(C\).

### Proof

For a representation \((x,y)\), put
\[
\alpha=x+iy,\qquad \beta=x-iy.
\]
Both lie in \(\mathcal O_F\), and
\[
\alpha\beta=n.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(n\ne0\), both factors are nonzero.  The map
\((x,y)\mapsto\alpha\) is injective on the solution set: from
\(\alpha\) and \(n\), recover
\[
\beta=\frac n\alpha,\qquad
x=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2},\qquad
y=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2i}.
\tag{6}
\]
This remains valid if \(i\in K\); no relative-norm interpretation is
needed.

Equation (5) gives an integral-ideal factorization
\[
(\alpha)(\beta)=(n).
\]
Hence \((\alpha)\) is an integral ideal divisor of \((n)\).  If
\[
(n)=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\mathfrak p^{e_{\mathfrak p}},
\]
the number of its integral ideal divisors is
\[
d_F((n))=\prod_{\mathfrak p}(e_{\mathfrak p}+1).
\tag{7}
\]
For a rational prime \(p\), at most \(D\) prime ideals of \(F\) lie
above \(p\), and every corresponding exponent is at most
\(v_p(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}n|)\).  Therefore
\[
\boxed{
d_F((n))
\le
\tau\!\left(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}n|\right)^D.
}
\tag{8}
\]
No class-number factor is needed: principal ideals generated by
solutions form only a subset of all ideal divisors.

It remains to count generators of one fixed ideal divisor.  Every
embedding \(\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) restricts to an
embedding of \(K\), and \(\sigma(i)=\pm i\).  Thus
\[
|\sigma(\alpha)|\le2B.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(\alpha\) is a nonzero algebraic integer,
\[
|N_{F/\mathbb Q}\alpha|\ge1.
\]
Applying (9) to the other \(D-1\) embeddings gives the missing lower
bound
\[
\boxed{
(2B)^{-(D-1)}
\le|\sigma(\alpha)|\le2B
\quad\text{for every }\sigma.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Suppose \(\alpha_0\) is one solution generator of the same principal
ideal.  Every other one is
\[
\alpha=\varepsilon\alpha_0,
\qquad\varepsilon\in\mathcal O_F^\times.
\]
Equation (10) for both generators yields
\[
(2B)^{-D}
\le|\sigma(\varepsilon)|
\le(2B)^D
\quad\text{for every }\sigma.
\tag{11}
\]
Under the Dirichlet logarithmic embedding,
\(\mathcal O_F^\times/\mu_F\) is a fixed lattice of rank \(r_F\).
The box in (11) has side length \(O_F(1+\log(2B))\), so it contains at
most
\[
C_F(1+\log(2B))^{r_F}
\tag{12}
\]
units, including the finite torsion group.  Multiplying (12) by (8)
proves (2).

Finally, for every embedding,
\[
|\sigma(n)|
=|\sigma(x)^2+\sigma(y)^2|
\le2B^2.
\]
Taking the product proves (3).  The classical divisor estimate
\[
\max_{m\le X}\tau(m)
=\exp\left(O\left(\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}\right)\right)
=X^{o(1)}
\]
combined with fixed \(D,r_F,C_F\) proves (4).
\(\square\)

## 2. Weighted number-field chord charts

Use the cylindrical notation of
`CROSS_PLANE_TO_WEIGHTED_CHART_EXTRACTION_ATTACK.md`.  Thus
\(\mathcal J\) is a finite ray set, \(\rho\) ranges over ordinary
radii, and
\[
\mathcal Z_{j,\rho}\subset\mathbb R
\]
is the finite height set on the corresponding column.  Write
\[
h_{j,\rho}=|\mathcal Z_{j,\rho}|,\qquad
U=\max\{1,\max_{j,\rho}h_{j,\rho}\},
\]
\[
\Omega_{\rm cyl}
=\sum_\rho\sum_{j\ne k}
\min(h_{j,\rho},h_{k,\rho}),
\qquad
L_U=1+\lfloor\log_2U\rfloor.
\tag{13}
\]

Fix one positive real scale \(q\), one number field \(K\), and one
distinguished embedding through which the following algebraic numbers
have their displayed real values.  Assume:

1. for every height in the chart,
   \[
   \widetilde z=qz\in\mathcal O_K,\qquad
   \max_{\tau:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
   |\tau(\widetilde z)|\le B;
   \tag{14}
   \]
2. whenever two relevant columns at the same radius are nonempty,
   their scaled chord
   \[
   x_{j,k,\rho}
   =q\rho\|v_j-v_k\|\in\mathcal O_K
   \tag{15}
   \]
   has positive distinguished real value and satisfies
   \[
   \max_\tau|\tau(x_{j,k,\rho})|\le B;
   \tag{16}
   \]
3. the anchored algebraic chord multiplicity
   \[
   K_{\rm chord}
   =
   \max_{k,x}
   |\{(j,\rho):
   j\ne k,\ h_{j,\rho}h_{k,\rho}>0,\
   x_{j,k,\rho}=x\}|
   \tag{17}
   \]
   is finite.

Define
\[
\mathfrak F_K(B)
=
C_F(1+\log(2B))^{r_F}
\max_{1\le m\le\lfloor(2B^2)^D\rfloor}\tau(m)^D.
\tag{18}
\]

### Theorem 2 (weighted fixed-number-field terminal bound)

If \(\Omega_{\rm cyl}>0\), then
\[
\boxed{
|\Delta^2(P)|
\ge
\frac{\Omega_{\rm cyl}}
{|\mathcal J|L_UK_{\rm chord}\mathfrak F_K(2B)}.
}
\tag{19}
\]
In particular, if \(K\) and \(C\) are fixed and \(B\le t^C\), then
\[
\boxed{
|\Delta^2(P)|
\ge
\frac{\Omega_{\rm cyl}}
{|\mathcal J|L_UK_{\rm chord}\,t^{o(1)}}.
}
\tag{20}
\]

### Proof

Weighted-star averaging and the dyadic layer cake give a base ray
\(k_0\), a power of two \(H\), and a superlevel set
\(\mathcal E_H\) such that
\[
H|\mathcal E_H|
\ge
\frac{\Omega_{\rm cyl}}{|\mathcal J|L_U}.
\tag{21}
\]
Mapping \((j,\rho)\) to the algebraic chord
\(x_{j,k_0,\rho}\) and using (17) gives at least
\[
\frac{|\mathcal E_H|}{K_{\rm chord}}
\tag{22}
\]
different chord values.

For one representative of each chord value, choose one base-column
anchor \(z_x^0\) and any \(H\) distinct heights \(z\) from the other
column.  Put
\[
y=q(z-z_x^0)\in\mathcal O_K.
\]
For fixed \(x\), these \(H\) values are distinct, and (14) gives
\[
\max_\tau|\tau y|\le2B.
\tag{23}
\]
The genuine squared distance, after multiplying by the common factor
\(q^2\), is
\[
n=q^2d^2=x^2+y^2\in\mathcal O_K.
\tag{24}
\]
The distinguished value of \(x\) is positive, so \(n>0\) at that
embedding and in particular \(n\ne0\).  Theorem 1 with coordinate bound
\(2B\) shows that each label receives at most
\(\mathfrak F_K(2B)\) selected input pairs.

Dividing the input count by this fibre and using (21)--(22) proves
(19).  Since multiplication by the one fixed positive real number
\(q^2\) preserves the number of distance labels, the count applies to
\(\Delta^2(P)\).  Equation (20) follows from (4).
\(\square\)

## 3. Dependence on the field

For one fixed \(K\), every field-dependent quantity in (2) and (19) is
a constant.  The same conclusion is uniform over any fixed finite
family of fields.  In particular, bounded degree and bounded absolute
discriminant give only finitely many possibilities by Hermite's
theorem.

For a varying family \(K=K_t\), the exact uniform dependence is given
in `GROWING_NUMBER_FIELD_WEIGHTED_CHART_THEOREM.md`.  In particular,
polynomial all-conjugate coordinate height and
\[
[K_t(i):\mathbb Q]=o(\sqrt{\log\log t})
\tag{25}
\]
already suffice for a \(t^{o(1)}\) fibre, uniformly over the fields;
no discriminant bound is required.  The proof uses a unit-lattice
packing bound together with Dobrowolski's height theorem.  The more
general exact criterion also permits larger degree when the unit rank,
the shortest non-torsion unit-log vector, and the ideal-divisor factor
are better controlled.

Neither the class number nor an unrecorded choice of ideal generators
is being suppressed.

## 4. Why the height and nonzero assumptions are necessary

### 4.1 Infinite units with only one embedding controlled

Consider the Salem polynomial
\[
p(X)=X^4-X^3-X^2-X+1.
\tag{26}
\]
It has a real unit root \(\theta>1\), its reciprocal, and two
non-torsion conjugates on the unit circle.  In the compositum with
\(\mathbb Q(i)\), define
\[
x_m=\theta^m+\theta^{-m},
\qquad
y_m=-i(\theta^m-\theta^{-m}).
\tag{27}
\]
These are algebraic integers and
\[
x_m^2+y_m^2=4.
\tag{28}
\]
At an embedding sending \(\theta\) to a unit-circle conjugate, the
displayed values of both \(x_m,y_m\) are real and bounded by \(2\) for
every \(m\).  At the real embedding, they grow exponentially.
Therefore a bound at only the distinguished Euclidean embedding permits
infinitely many representations of the fixed nonzero label \(4\).

A polynomial bound for the absolute multiplicative Weil heights of
\(x,y\) is an equivalent sufficient replacement for (1), up to a
constant change in the exponent at fixed degree.  What is essential is
an \(O(\log t)\) window in every unit-log direction.

### 4.2 The zero label

If \(K\) contains \(i\), then for every \(u\in\mathcal O_K\),
\[
(iu)^2+u^2=0.
\tag{29}
\]
Even an all-conjugate box of polynomial size may contain polynomially
many such pairs.  Thus \(n\ne0\) is essential in Theorem 1.

In the Euclidean terminal application, (15) has positive distinguished
value, so every selected label in (24) is automatically nonzero.  The
zero-label obstruction never occurs there.

## 5. Pentagonal prism and remaining extraction gap

For the regular pentagonal prism, the two squared chord values are
\[
\frac{5-\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
\frac{5+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
Their positive square roots lie in one fixed finite extension of
\(\mathbb Q\).  Hence the prism is covered by Theorem 2 after choosing
that fixed field: its irrational chord ratio defeats only the rational
common-denominator theorem, not the fixed-number-field theorem.

This is a terminal theorem, not an extraction theorem.  The unresolved
bridge is still to prove that a critical few-distance configuration
places a sufficiently large portion of \(\Omega_{\rm cyl}\) in:

- one common field \(K\) of controlled arithmetic complexity;
- one common scale \(q\);
- polynomial absolute height at every embedding; and
- subpolynomial anchored chord multiplicity.

The five-column prism shows that insisting on \(K=\mathbb Q\) is too
strong.  It does not show that one bounded-degree \(K\) can be extracted
from a growing critical configuration.

## 6. Verification

`verify_fixed_number_field_weighted_chart.py` checks:

1. the rational specialization \(r_2(n)\le4\tau(n)\) for 500 labels;
2. the local ideal-divisor envelope
   \((v_p(N)+1)^D\) over exhaustive synthetic splitting patterns;
3. the \(O((\log B)^{r_F})\) fixed-rank unit-lattice count;
4. exclusion of \(n=0\); and
5. the Salem-unit obstruction when conjugate heights are removed.

Run:
```bash
pytest -q test_verify_fixed_number_field_weighted_chart.py
python3 verify_fixed_number_field_weighted_chart.py \
  > fixed_number_field_weighted_chart_certificate.json
```

The executable checks finite algebra and boundary cases.  The
arbitrary-field proof is Theorem 1.
