# Growing cyclotomic no-go for low-complexity chart extraction

Date: 2026-07-30

## 0. Outcome

The fixed-number-field terminal theorem cannot be activated from the
number of distance labels, weighted radial overlap, or distance
representation energy alone.

A growing family of regular prime prisms has the local critical ledger
\[
|\Delta^2|=\Theta(t^3),\qquad
\Omega_{\rm cyl}=\Theta(t^4),\qquad
|\mathcal J|=\Theta(t),
\tag{1}
\]
but every nonzero horizontal chord has algebraic degree
\(\Theta(t)\).  Moreover, the chord ratios have multiplicative rank
\(\Theta(t)\), and the high representation energy remains supported on
labels of degree \(\Theta(t)\).

This is a rigorous **local extraction no-go**.  It is not a global
counterexample to Erdős #1083: the family has \(t^3\), rather than
\(t^5\), points.  It shows that a proof at the \(N=t^5\) critical node
must use additional global Euclidean structure to extract one
low-complexity field; a pigeonhole based only on
\(D=t^{3+o(1)}\) labels and \(\Omega=t^{4+o(1)}\) cannot do so.

## 1. The prime-prism family

Let \(p\ge5\) be an odd prime and \(H\ge3\).  Define
\[
P_{p,H}
=
\left\{
\left(
\cos\frac{2\pi j}{p},
\sin\frac{2\pi j}{p},
h
\right):
0\le j<p,\ 1\le h\le H
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
It has
\[
|P_{p,H}|=pH.
\tag{3}
\]
Every column has ordinary radius one and height \(H\).  Consequently
the ordered weighted radial overlap is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\Omega_{\rm cyl}=p(p-1)H.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Put
\[
\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/p),
\qquad
a_d=2-\zeta^d-\zeta^{-d}
=4\sin^2\frac{\pi d}{p},
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
1\le d\le\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]
There are exactly \((p-1)/2\) horizontal squared-chord types.  Every
squared distance belongs to
\[
\{u^2:1\le u<H\}
\cup
\left\{
a_d+u^2:
1\le d\le\frac{p-1}{2},\
0\le u<H
\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Hence
\[
|\Delta^2(P_{p,H})|
\le
\frac{p-1}{2}H+H-1.
\tag{7}
\]

For \(u\ge2\),
\[
a_d+u^2\in(u^2,u^2+4).
\]
The intervals for two different integers \(u\ge2\) are disjoint,
because
\[
(u+1)^2-(u^2+4)=2u-3>0.
\]
Within one layer the \(a_d\)'s are distinct.  Therefore
\[
|\Delta^2(P_{p,H})|
\ge
\frac{p-1}{2}(H-2).
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (7)--(8) give
\[
\boxed{
|\Delta^2(P_{p,H})|=\Theta(pH).
}
\tag{9}
\]

At the local critical choice \(H=p^2\), with \(t=p\),
\[
|P_{p,p^2}|=t^3,\qquad
|\Delta^2(P_{p,p^2})|=\Theta(t^3),
\qquad
\Omega_{\rm cyl}=t^4-t^3.
\tag{10}
\]

## 2. Every cross label has growing field degree

Let
\[
L_p=\mathbb Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})
\]
be the maximal real subfield of the \(p\)-th cyclotomic field.  It has
\[
[L_p:\mathbb Q]=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\tag{11}
\]
For every \(1\le d\le(p-1)/2\),
\[
\zeta^d+\zeta^{-d}=2-a_d.
\]
Since \(d\) is invertible modulo \(p\),
\(\zeta^d+\zeta^{-d}\) is a Galois conjugate generator of \(L_p\).
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb Q(a_d)=L_p,\qquad
[\mathbb Q(a_d):\mathbb Q]=\frac{p-1}{2}.
}
\tag{12}
\]
The chord length \(c_d=\sqrt{a_d}\) has degree at least
\((p-1)/2\).

More strongly, every separated cross label in (8) satisfies
\[
\mathbb Q(a_d+u^2)=\mathbb Q(a_d)=L_p,
\tag{13}
\]
because \(u^2\in\mathbb Q\).  Thus passing to a heavily represented
distance label does not lower the field degree.

The field generated by the complete squared-distance set is exactly
\[
\mathbb Q(\Delta^2(P_{p,H}))=L_p:
\tag{14}
\]
all labels lie in \(L_p\), and the \(u=0,d=1\) label is \(a_1\), which
generates \(L_p\).

## 3. Energy pigeonholing also fails

For one chord type \(d\), there are \(2p\) ordered angular pairs.
For a fixed positive vertical difference \(u\), there are
\[
2(H-u)
\]
ordered height pairs.  On the separated layers \(2\le u<H\), the
ordered representation multiplicity of \(a_d+u^2\) is therefore
\[
4p(H-u).
\]
The corresponding exact energy is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak E_{\rm sep}
&=
\frac{p-1}{2}
\sum_{u=2}^{H-1}
\bigl(4p(H-u)\bigr)^2\\
&=
\frac{8p^2(p-1)}6
(H-2)(H-1)(2H-3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mathfrak E_{\rm sep}
=\left(\frac83+o(1)\right)p^3H^3.
\tag{16}
\]
Every label counted in (15) generates the same degree-\((p-1)/2\)
field \(L_p\).  Hence even energy-weighted pigeonholing can remain
entirely on growing-degree labels.

## 4. No bounded-degree common-scale chart

The choice of heights \(1,\ldots,H\) makes the common scale interact
rigidly with the coefficient field.  Suppose a nonempty subchart uses
one scale \(q>0\) and one number field \(K\), with
\[
qh\in\mathcal O_K
\]
for a selected height \(h\in\{1,\ldots,H\}\).  Since \(h\) is a
nonzero rational integer,
\[
q=\frac{qh}{h}\in K.
\tag{17}
\]
If the same chart contains one horizontal chord type and
\[
qc_d\in\mathcal O_K,
\]
then
\[
c_d=\frac{qc_d}{q}\in K.
\tag{18}
\]
Equation (12) now forces
\[
[K:\mathbb Q]\ge\frac{p-1}{2}.
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore:

### Proposition 1 (bounded-degree extraction no-go)

If
\[
[K:\mathbb Q]<\frac{p-1}{2},
\]
no nonempty common-scale number-field chord chart can contain even one
cross-column pair of \(P_{p,H}\).

This is stronger than saying that no single fixed field captures a
positive proportion: it captures zero weighted cross overlap under the
absolute-height formulation of the fixed-field terminal theorem.

The same obstruction survives if the chart uses height differences
rather than absolute heights, provided it retains two distinct integer
heights.  Their nonzero integer difference again forces \(q\in K\).

## 5. Multiplicative-rank no-go

There is also a scale-free obstruction.  Define the real cyclotomic
units
\[
\xi_d
=
\frac{\sin(\pi d/p)}{\sin(\pi/p)},
\qquad
2\le d\le\frac{p-1}{2}.
\tag{20}
\]
The standard cyclotomic-unit theorem says that these
\((p-3)/2\) units are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion;
equivalently, they generate a finite-index subgroup of the circular
units of the maximal real field.  Squaring does not change the rank,
and
\[
\xi_d^2=\frac{a_d}{a_1}.
\tag{21}
\]

Consequently, any set of \(s\) distinct chord types has chord-ratio
multiplicative rank at least \(s-1\).  A common scale cancels from all
ratios:
\[
\frac{qc_d}{qc_e}=\frac{c_d}{c_e}.
\]
Thus a chart whose scaled chord set has multiplicative rank at most
\(r\) can contain at most \(r+1\) chord types.

Each type accounts for exactly \(2pH\) of the global ordered overlap.
Since there are \((p-1)/2\) types,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Omega_{\rm captured}}{\Omega_{\rm cyl}}
\le
\frac{2(r+1)}{p-1}.
}
\tag{22}
\]
In particular, \(r=p^{o(1)}\) captures at most
\[
p^{-1+o(1)}\Omega_{\rm cyl}.
\tag{23}
\]
It cannot give the \(\chi<1\) overlap fraction required by the current
critical terminal interface.

The field-degree no-go in Proposition 1 is elementary.  The rank
statement uses the classical circular-unit rank theorem; the verifier
checks its regulator matrix for small primes but does not substitute
finite numerics for that theorem.

## 6. A conditional finite-palette extraction lemma

There is one useful weak positive statement.  It does not require all
overlap to lie in a single field.

Suppose a collection of \(F\) admissible number-field charts covers
weighted overlap of total mass at least \(\Omega_*\).  Each chart must
satisfy the common-scale, algebraic-integrality, all-conjugate height,
and nonzero-label hypotheses of
`FIXED_NUMBER_FIELD_WEIGHTED_CHART_THEOREM.md`.  Assume their fields
belong to a fixed finite family, or otherwise have a uniform
\(t^{o(1)}\) representation-fibre constant.  Let their anchored chord
multiplicities be at most \(K_*\).

Assign each covered weighted token to one chart containing it.  Some
chart receives at least
\[
\frac{\Omega_*}{F}
\tag{24}
\]
mass.  Applying the fixed-number-field terminal theorem to that chart
gives:

### Lemma 2 (conditional field-palette extraction)

\[
\boxed{
|\Delta^2(P)|
\ge
\frac{\Omega_*}
{F|\mathcal J|L_UK_*\,t^{o(1)}}.
}
\tag{25}
\]

For
\[
\Omega_*=t^{\omega-o(1)},\quad
F=t^{\phi+o(1)},\quad
|\mathcal J|=t^{1+o(1)},\quad
K_*=t^{\kappa+o(1)},
\]
this becomes
\[
|\Delta^2(P)|
\ge t^{\omega-1-\phi-\kappa-o(1)}.
\tag{26}
\]
At the natural overlap exponent \(\omega=5\), it beats the critical
\(t^3\) scale whenever
\[
\boxed{\phi+\kappa<1.}
\tag{27}
\]

Lemma 2 is a rigorous weakening of “one common field”: a
sub-\(t\)-sized palette suffices when chord multiplicity is small.  It
is conditional because no current argument derives such a palette from
the global distance bound.

## 7. What can and cannot be extracted

The following implication is false if its premises consist only of
distance-label count, weighted radial overlap, and representation
energy:
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\Delta^2|&\le t^{3+o(1)},\\
\Omega_{\rm cyl}&\ge t^{4-o(1)}
\end{aligned}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{matrix}
\text{one degree-}O(1)\text{ field, or}\\
\text{one }t^{o(1)}\text{-rank chart capturing }
t^{-\chi}\Omega,\ \chi<1.
\end{matrix}
\tag{28}
\]
The prime prisms satisfy the two displayed local exponents and violate
the conclusion.

What remains possible is a theorem using the full global critical
hypotheses
\[
N=t^5,\qquad
D=t^{3+o(1)},\qquad
\mathfrak C_{\rm plane}\ge t^{13-o(1)}.
\tag{29}
\]
The prism family has only \(N=t^3\) points and does not refute such a
theorem.  In particular, this note does not construct a global
few-distance configuration at the Erdős critical scale.

The minimum honest additional hypothesis for the current terminal route
is one of:

1. a bounded-degree field chart capturing
   \(t^{-\chi+o(1)}\Omega_{\rm cyl}\) with \(\chi<1\);
2. a chord-ratio group of rank \(t^{\sigma+o(1)}\) together with a
   capture loss satisfying
   \[
   \sigma+\chi<1;
   \tag{30}
   \]
3. a new global Euclidean theorem showing that the \(N=t^5\) mass and
   \(t^{13}\) cross-plane energy rule out the growing cyclotomic
   alternative.

Items 1--2 are assumptions/interfaces, not consequences proved here.
Item 3 is the narrowed extraction problem.

## 9. Full-critical-energy update

The later note
`CROSS_PLANE_GALOIS_ORBIT_TRICHOTOMY.md` now uses the previously
unused input
\[
\mathfrak C_{\rm plane}\ge t^{13-o(1)}
\]
to prove two exact statements:

1. a positive proportion of this energy lies on labels with
   \(t^{5-o(1)}\) representations supported on \(t^{1-o(1)}\)
   ordered plane pairs;
2. if complete algebraic orbits of size at least \(R\) carry energy
   \(\mathfrak C_R\), and one orbit carries at most \(H_R\), then
   \[
   |\Delta^2(P)|\ge \frac{R\mathfrak C_R}{H_R}.
   \]

For growing prime-cyclotomic degree \(R=t^{1-o(1)}\), a fixed distance
gain would follow from a fixed saving over the one-orbit barrier
\[
H_R=t^{11-o(1)}.
\]
However, `GROWING_CYCLOTOMIC_CRITICAL_ENERGY_ATTACK.md` constructs an
abstract critical representation tensor with
\[
D=\Theta(t^3),\qquad
\mathfrak C_{\rm plane}=\Theta(t^{13}),\qquad
H_R=\Theta(t^{11})
\]
that attains equality in the orbit inequality.  Its labels form complete
degree-\(\Theta(t)\) cyclotomic orbits.

This sharpens Item 3 of Section 7: the missing global Euclidean theorem
must disperse the energy of every growing-field orbit below \(t^{11}\),
extract a terminal low-complexity chart, or turn the remaining
concentrated orbit into ruled distance expansion.  The abstract tensor is
not a Euclidean realization, so the global Erdős problem remains open.
