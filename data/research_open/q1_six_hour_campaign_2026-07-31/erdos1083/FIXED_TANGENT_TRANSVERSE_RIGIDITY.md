# Erdős #1083: fixed-tangent transverse rigidity

Date: 2026-08-01

## 0. Result and boundary

The transverse-heavy branch of the tangent-overlap dichotomy has one
exact geometric consequence.

For one fixed tangent square \(\tau\), the associated \(S\)-element
distance cells from two rationally transverse height rows intersect
in at most one distance label.  Two common labels would immediately
produce a nonzero vector in the intersection of the two rational
dilation spaces.

Thus the fixed-\(\tau\) cells form a linear set system along every
transverse edge.  A second-moment argument gives an exact union bound.
At the frozen exponents, however, the guaranteed fixed-tangent row
support is only \(t^{5/9}\), while the capacity threshold is
\(U=t^{5/6}\).  The theorem therefore does not yet contradict the
\(SU\)-element common spectrum.

## 1. Same-tangent cells

Fix \(\rho>0\), a finite \(S\)-element set \(X\subset\mathbb R\), and
a tangent square \(\tau\).  For every nonzero height \(z\), define

\[
 C_{z,\tau}
 =\rho^2+z^2+\tau+2\rho zX.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This is exactly the anchor squared-distance cell generated by the
target

\[
 q_{z,\tau}=(A,\sqrt\tau,-z)
\]

in the reverse-circle chart.  Since \(z\ne0\), every cell has
\(|C_{z,\tau}|=S\).

Put

\[
 W_z=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\bigl(2\rho z(X-X)\bigr).
\tag{1.2}
\]

### Theorem 1 (two-label rigidity)

If

\[
 W_z\cap W_{z'}=\{0\},
\tag{1.3}
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{|C_{z,\tau}\cap C_{z',\tau}|\le1.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Equivalently, if the two same-tangent cells share two distinct
distance labels, then the two rational dilation spaces intersect
nontrivially.

#### Proof

Suppose distinct labels \(d_1,d_2\) lie in both cells.  There are
\(x_1,x_2,x'_1,x'_2\in X\) such that

\[
 d_\ell
 =\rho^2+z^2+\tau+2\rho zx_\ell
 =\rho^2+z'^2+\tau+2\rho z'x'_\ell
\qquad(\ell=1,2).
\]

Subtract the two identities:

\[
 2\rho z(x_1-x_2)
 =2\rho z'(x'_1-x'_2).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The common value is nonzero, because \(d_1\ne d_2\) and each affine
cell map is injective.  The left side belongs to \(W_z\), and the
right side belongs to \(W_{z'}\), contradicting (1.3).  \(\square\)

### Sharpness

The constant one cannot be replaced by zero.  Take

\[
 \rho=1,\qquad X=\{0,\tfrac12\},\qquad
 z=1,\qquad z'=\sqrt2.
\]

Then

\[
 W_z=\mathbb Q,\qquad W_{z'}=\sqrt2\,\mathbb Q
\]

are transverse, while

\[
 C_{1,\tau}=\{2+\tau,3+\tau\},
\]

\[
 C_{\sqrt2,\tau}
 =\{3+\tau,3+\sqrt2+\tau\}.
\]

Their intersection is exactly \(\{3+\tau\}\).

## 2. A fixed-tangent union theorem

Let \(Z_\tau\) be a finite set of nonzero rows containing the same
tangent square \(\tau\), and put \(n=|Z_\tau|\).  Define ordered pair
counts

\[
 e_\perp
 =|\{(z,z'):z\ne z',\
 W_z\cap W_{z'}=\{0\}\}|
\tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
 e_\parallel=n(n-1)-e_\perp.
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem 2 (linear-edge packing)

The union of the same-tangent cells satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\bigcup_{z\in Z_\tau}C_{z,\tau}\right|
 \ge
 \frac{n^2S^2}
 {nS+e_\perp+Se_\parallel}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

In particular, if the \(n\) spaces are pairwise transverse, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|\bigcup_{z\in Z_\tau}C_{z,\tau}\right|
 \ge
 \frac{nS^2}{S+n-1}.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

For a distance label \(d\), let

\[
 m(d)=|\{z\in Z_\tau:d\in C_{z,\tau}\}|.
\]

Then

\[
 \sum_dm(d)=nS.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The diagonal contribution to \(\sum_dm(d)^2\) is \(nS\).
By Theorem 1, every ordered transverse pair contributes at most one
common label.  An ordered nontransverse pair contributes at most
\(S\), the full cell size.  Hence

\[
 \sum_dm(d)^2
 \le nS+e_\perp+Se_\parallel.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz applied to (2.5) gives (2.3).  In the pairwise
transverse case,

\[
 e_\perp=n(n-1),\qquad e_\parallel=0,
\]

which reduces (2.3) to (2.4).  \(\square\)

## 3. Endpoint audit

The tangent-transversality dichotomy gives, in its transverse-heavy
branch, one \(\tau\) supporting

\[
 t^{10/9+o(1)}
\]

ordered transverse row pairs.  Therefore its support has at least

\[
 n\ge t^{5/9+o(1)}
\tag{3.1}
\]

rows.

If one could further extract \(n=t^{5/9+o(1)}\) pairwise transverse
rows, (2.4), with \(S=t^{7/9+o(1)}\), would give

\[
 \left|\bigcup_z C_{z,\tau}\right|
 \ge t^{4/3-o(1)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is a genuine distance lower bound, but it is smaller than the
common spectrum capacity

\[
 SU=t^{29/18+o(1)}
\]

by the factor

\[
 t^{29/18-4/3}=t^{5/18}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

More conceptually, when \(n<S\), formula (2.4) is of order \(nS\).
It reaches the \(SU\) capacity only when \(n\) is of order \(U\).
The frozen tangent pigeonhole gives only

\[
 n\ge\frac{qU}{R}=t^{5/9+o(1)},
\]

whereas

\[
 U=t^{5/6+o(1)}.
\]

The missing row-support exponent is again

\[
 \frac56-\frac59=\frac5{18}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

This exact gap explains why fixed-tangent incidence geometry alone
does not close the transverse-heavy branch.

## 4. What the theorem changes

The transverse-heavy branch is no longer an unstructured collection
of common tangent labels.  For every fixed \(\tau\):

- every transverse row pair has at most one common anchor label in
  its \(\tau\)-cells;
- any pair with two common labels is automatically nontransverse;
- the full cell union obeys the exact graph-sensitive bound (2.3).

The next missing step must use more than pairwise cell intersections.
Plausible additional inputs are:

1. a labelled cycle involving different tangent squares;
2. a lower bound forcing the same distance labels to recur across
   several \(\tau\)-fibres; or
3. an extraction showing that a large part of the fixed-\(\tau\)
   transverse graph is pairwise transverse and has support closer to
   \(U\).

No such extraction is claimed here.

## 5. Geometry firewall

The cells in (1.1) are actual anchor-to-target squared distances.
The theorem does not control target--target distances or all
translated-source cross distances, and it does not turn the local
bundle into a global few-distance construction.
