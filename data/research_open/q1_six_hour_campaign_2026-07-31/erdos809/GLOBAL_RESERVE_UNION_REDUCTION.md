# Erdős #809 — global reserve-union reduction

Date: 2026-08-01

Status: `PROVED__HALL_FREE_GLOBAL_DICHOTOMY_AND_ZERO_SHORE_MASS_BOUND`

## 1. The accounting simplification

Retain the maximum-degree setup and the reserves
\[
\mathcal R(e)=
\begin{cases}
\{e\},&e\text{ has a nonempty shore},\\
\mathcal K(e),&e\text{ is zero-shore}.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
Every \(\mathcal R(e)\) consists of missing edges of \(G[B]\), and it
contains its base pair \(e\).

Let
\[
 \mathcal E_*
 =\bigcup_\gamma\binom{Y_\gamma}{2}
\tag{2}
\]
be the set of distinct active base pairs, and define the **global forced
reserve**
\[
 \mathcal Q
 =\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal E_*}\mathcal R(e).
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
 \mathcal E_*\subseteq\mathcal Q
 \subseteq\overline E(G[B]).
\tag{4}
\]

The previous token-by-token Hall and adaptive-tree constructions are
valid, but stronger than the numerical goal requires.  To prove
\(D_B\le M_B\), the forced missing edges do not need to retain an
association with individual defect tokens.  Their cardinality alone is
enough.

### Theorem 1.1 (global reserve-union dichotomy)

Exactly one of the following holds:

1. \(|\mathcal Q|\ge D_B\), in which case
   \[
   \boxed{D_B\le M_B};
   \tag{5}
   \]
2. the single global obstruction
   \[
   \boxed{|\mathcal Q|<D_B}
   \tag{6}
   \]
   holds.

#### Proof

By (4), \(|\mathcal Q|\le M_B\).  Thus the first alternative gives
\(D_B\le|\mathcal Q|\le M_B\); the second is its negation. \(\square\)

This is a strictly stronger closure test than either fixed-root Hall or
adaptive forest-reserve feasibility: either matching produces
\(D_B\) distinct elements of \(\mathcal Q\), whereas (5) only asks that
such elements exist globally.

The strictness is real even in an abstract four-vertex reserve system.
Give pair \(12\) the reserve \(\{b,c\}\) and every other pair the
reserve \(\{a\}\).  The global union has three charges, exactly the
defect of a four-point colour.  Yet the partition
\(\{\{1,2\},\{3\},\{4\}\}\) exposes only charge \(a\), so no adaptive
reserve-labelled spanning tree exists.  This is an accounting example,
not a claimed BCM realization.

## 2. Exact mass forced by failure

For each active base pair \(e\), let
\[
 h_e=|\{\gamma:e\in\tbinom{Y_\gamma}{2}\}|.
\tag{7}
\]
Put
\[
 X=\sum_\gamma\binom{t_\gamma}{2}
   =\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_*}h_e.
\tag{8}
\]
The nonempty-shore fixed-pair theorem gives \(h_e\le1\) unless \(e\)
is zero-shore. Hence the repeated zero-shore mass is exactly
\[
 E_0
 =\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal E_*:\ e\text{ zero-shore}}}
 (h_e-1)
 =X-|\mathcal E_*|.
\tag{9}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (global zero-shore surplus theorem)

If the global reserve-union test fails, then
\[
\boxed{
 E_0
 \ge X-D_B+1
 =1+\sum_{\gamma:\,t_\gamma\ge1}\binom{t_\gamma-1}{2}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

#### Proof

The quantities are integral, so (6) gives
\[
 |\mathcal E_*|\le|\mathcal Q|\le D_B-1.
\]
Substitute this into (9).  Finally,
\[
 \binom{t}{2}-(t-1)=\binom{t-1}{2}
\]
for every integer \(t\ge1\); colours with \(t=0\) contribute zero to
both \(X\) and \(D_B\). Sum over the colours. \(\square\)

Thus any failure pays not only one repeated zero-shore incidence, but
also the full quadratic surplus generated by every colour with three or
more outer endpoints.  The hardest remaining configuration is forced
toward colours with \(t_\gamma=2\), rather than arbitrary large endpoint
sets.

### Corollary 2.2 (congestion or many exceptional pairs)

Let \(z\) be the number of distinct active zero-shore pairs.  Under
(6), necessarily \(D_B\ge2\), and
\(z\le|\mathcal E_*|\le D_B-1\). Some zero-shore pair has
multiplicity at least
\[
\boxed{
 1+\left\lceil
 \frac{1+\sum_{\gamma:\,t_\gamma\ge1}\binom{t_\gamma-1}{2}}
      {D_B-1}
 \right\rceil.
}
\tag{11}
\]
The fifth-attack colour-rectangle theorem then forces
\[
 M_A\ge h^2
\tag{12}
\]
for that multiplicity \(h\).

## 3. Global synchronization certificate

Let \(W\subseteq B\) be the union of the endpoints of all active
zero-shore pairs.  Failure (6) has the simultaneous consequences
\[
\boxed{
 \left|
 \{f\in\overline E(G[B]): f\cap W\ne\varnothing\}
 \right|<D_B,
}
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
 \left|
 \bigcup_{bc\text{ active zero-shore}}
 N_B(b)\widehat\times N_B(c)
 \right|<D_B.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Indeed, every missing edge incident to an endpoint of an active
zero-shore pair belongs to that pair's \(\mathcal K\)-reserve, and so
the set in (13) is contained in \(\mathcal Q\).  The same is true of
every forced neighbourhood rectangle in (14).  This global form has no
deleted charge set \(C_+\) and no choice of roots or partitions.

In particular, if \(\overline d_B(w)\) denotes missing degree inside
\(B\), then
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{w\in W}\overline d_B(w)<2D_B.
}
\tag{15}
\]
Every missing edge counted in (13) contributes at most two to the sum.

## 4. Zero-endpoint localization

The global certificate can be sharpened by removing everything already
paid outside the zero-shore endpoint set. Put
\[
 U_\gamma=Y_\gamma\setminus W,
 \qquad u_\gamma=|U_\gamma|,
\tag{16}
\]
and let \(I_B(W)\) be the number of missing \(B\)-edges incident to
\(W\). Every active pair contained in \(U_\gamma\) has a nonempty shore;
otherwise its endpoints would belong to \(W\). Such a pair occurs in
at most one colour by the fixed-pair theorem. Consequently the
\[
 P_{\rm out}=\sum_\gamma\binom{u_\gamma}{2}
\tag{17}
\]
active pairs wholly outside \(W\) are all distinct. They are disjoint
from the edges counted by \(I_B(W)\), and both sets lie in
\(\mathcal Q\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal Q|\ge I_B(W)+P_{\rm out}.}
\tag{18}
\]

Define the residual zero-endpoint burden
\[
 \mathfrak B_W
 =D_B-P_{\rm out}
 =\sum_\gamma
 \left((t_\gamma-1)_+-\binom{u_\gamma}{2}\right).
\tag{19}
\]
If the global reserve test fails, integrality and (18) give the sharper
certificate
\[
\boxed{I_B(W)\le\mathfrak B_W-1.}
\tag{20}
\]
In particular \(\mathfrak B_W\ge1\) and
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{w\in W}\overline d_B(w)<2\mathfrak B_W.
}
\tag{21}
\]

More precisely, if \(M_B[W]\) denotes the number of missing edges with
both endpoints in \(W\), then
\[
 I_B(W)=
 \sum_{w\in W}\overline d_B(w)-M_B[W].
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently
\[
 I_B(W)\ge
 \max\left\{
 \frac12\sum_{w\in W}\overline d_B(w),
 \sum_{w\in W}\overline d_B(w)-\binom{|W|}{2}
 \right\}.
\tag{23}
\]

There is also an incidence-only consequence. Let
\[
 c_w=|\{\gamma:w\in Y_\gamma\}|.
\tag{24}
\]
Every \(A\)--\(B\) edge has its forced outer endpoint in \(B\), and
same-colour good edges form a matching. Thus the colours counted by
\(c_w\) are in bijection with the \(A\)-neighbours of \(w\), giving the
exact identity
\[
 c_w=d_A(w).
\]
Since
\(v\) has maximum degree \(m-1\),
\[
 d_B(w)\le m-1-d_A(w),
\]
whence
\[
 \overline d_B(w)=|B|-1-d_B(w)
 \ge |B|-m+c_w.
\]
Moreover \(\overline d_B(w)\ge1\) because \(w\) is an endpoint of a
zero-shore missing pair. Thus global failure forces
\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{w\in W}
 \max\{1,\ |B|-m+c_w\}
 <2\mathfrak B_W.
}
\tag{25}
\]

The burden itself has a useful exact incidence form. Put
\[
 C_W=\sum_{w\in W}c_w=\sum_\gamma|Y_\gamma\cap W|,
\]
let \(N_0\) count the colours with \(t_\gamma\ge1\) and
\(Y_\gamma\subseteq W\), and put
\[
 P_{\ge3}=\sum_{\gamma:\,u_\gamma\ge3}
 \binom{u_\gamma-1}{2}.
\]
Then direct expansion of (19), separating \(u_\gamma=0\) from
\(u_\gamma\ge1\), gives
\[
\boxed{\mathfrak B_W=C_W-N_0-P_{\ge3}.}
\tag{26}
\]
Thus the residual burden is no larger than the total colour incidence
on \(W\); one root is saved for each colour wholly supported on \(W\),
and three or more outside endpoints create an additional quadratic
credit.

Let \(g=m-|B|\) be the closed-neighbourhood imbalance. From
\(\overline d_B(w)\ge c_w-g\), (22), and
\(M_B[W]\le\binom{|W|}{2}\),
\[
 I_B(W)\ge C_W-g|W|-\binom{|W|}{2}.
\]
Together with (20) and (26), every global obstruction must therefore
satisfy
\[
\boxed{
 N_0+P_{\ge3}
 \le g|W|+\binom{|W|}{2}-1.
}
\tag{27}
\]
This bounds the total quadratic credit from colours having at least
three endpoints outside the exceptional set, plus the number of colours
wholly inside it, by the size and imbalance of the exceptional endpoint
set.

Combining (18), (23), and the lower bound used in (25) gives the
following fully numerical exit. Define
\[
 \ell_w=\max\{1,\ |B|-m+c_w\}.
\]
If
\[
\boxed{
 P_{\rm out}+
 \max\left\{
 \frac12\sum_{w\in W}\ell_w,
 \sum_{w\in W}\ell_w-\binom{|W|}{2}
 \right\}
 \ge D_B,
}
\tag{28}
\]
then \(|\mathcal Q|\ge D_B\) and hence \(D_B\le M_B\). This exit uses
only endpoint-set incidences, the maximum-degree witness, and the
zero-shore endpoint set; it does not require choosing roots, trees, or
matchings.

Equations (19)--(28) isolate the remaining difficulty: colours with
many endpoints outside \(W\) are paid quadratically by their distinct
base pairs, while the residual incidence on \(W\) must coexist with an
abnormally small incident missing-edge boundary.

## 5. Low-degree vertex cover for all exceptional pairs

Assume the global test fails and define
\[
 q_D=\max\left\{q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
 \binom q2\le D_B-1\right\}.
\tag{29}
\]
For every active zero-shore pair \(bc\), its reserve contains a missing
neighbourhood rectangle of size at least
\[
 \binom{\min\{d_B(b),d_B(c)\}}2.
\]
Since \(\mathcal K(bc)\subseteq\mathcal Q\) and
\(|\mathcal Q|\le D_B-1\),
\[
 \min\{d_B(b),d_B(c)\}\le q_D.
\tag{30}
\]
Consequently
\[
 L=\{w\in W:d_B(w)\le q_D\}
\tag{31}
\]
is a vertex cover of the graph of all active zero-shore pairs.

Put \(l=|L|\),
\[
 u^L_\gamma=|Y_\gamma\setminus L|,
 \qquad
 P_L=\sum_\gamma\binom{u^L_\gamma}{2}.
\tag{32}
\]
Every active pair wholly outside \(L\) has a nonempty shore, by the
vertex-cover property, and therefore occurs in at most one colour.
All \(P_L\) such pairs are distinct missing edges outside the incident
missing boundary \(I_B(L)\). Since \(L\subseteq W\), both sets belong
to \(\mathcal Q\), and
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal Q|\ge I_B(L)+P_L.}
\tag{33}
\]

Every vertex of \(L\) has missing \(B\)-degree at least
\(r-1-q_D\), where \(r=|B|\). Therefore
\[
 I_B(L)\ge
 \max\left\{
 \frac{l(r-1-q_D)}2,
 l(r-1-q_D)-\binom l2
 \right\}.
\tag{34}
\]
We obtain the following exact closure criterion:
\[
\boxed{
 P_L+
 \max\left\{
 \frac{l(r-1-q_D)}2,
 l(r-1-q_D)-\binom l2
 \right\}
 \ge D_B
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_B\le M_B.
}
\tag{35}
\]
Conversely, every global obstruction satisfies the strict reverse of
the inequality in (35). In particular, whenever \(q_D<r-1\),
\[
\boxed{
 l<\frac{2D_B}{r-1-q_D}.
}
\tag{36}
\]
Thus a subquadratic obstruction \(D_B=o(r^2)\) has
\(q_D=O(\sqrt{D_B})=o(r)\) and all active zero-shore pairs are covered
by
\[
 |L|=O(D_B/r)=o(r)
\]
low-\(B\)-degree vertices. This is an all-parameter reduction, not a
claim that the remaining low-degree-star configuration is impossible.

## 6. Interface to the full target

Whenever (5) holds and the separately defined outer-\(A\) residue obeys
\[
 R_A\le S_m,
\tag{37}
\]
one has
\[
 D_A=R_A+D_B\le S_m+M_B,
\tag{38}
\]
which closes the maximum-degree branch.  If (5) fails, (10), (13), and
(14) give a root-free global exceptional certificate on which the next
structural argument may focus.

## 7. Scope firewall

Theorems 1.1 and 2.1 are exact all-parameter results and strictly
improve the sufficient reserve test.  They do not prove that (6) is
impossible under the full BCM contract, and they do not control
\(R_A\).  Erdős #809 remains open.

`verify_global_reserve_union.py` independently reconstructs the
\(B\)-side reserves and audits every displayed consequence on 45
deterministic systems and 4,000 seeded random systems satisfying
independence and nonempty-shore uniqueness. Of these, 747 are genuine
global-union obstructions. These are finite falsification guards, not
full BCM witnesses and not the proof of the universal statements.
