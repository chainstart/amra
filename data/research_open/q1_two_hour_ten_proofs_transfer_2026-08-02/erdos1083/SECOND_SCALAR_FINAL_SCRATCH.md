# Final second-scalar scratch: an unbounded coprimality theorem for the fixed five-point mask

Date: 2026-08-02

Status: **PROVED FOR THIS FIXED SOURCE MASK; ISOLATED SCRATCH RESULT;

Put

\[
 X=\{0,1,3,5,6\},\qquad
 P(x)=1+x+x^3+x^5+x^6.
\]

The finite gcd no-go in `SECOND_SCALAR_SCRATCH_HANDOFF.md` extends to all
scalar ratios.  The only escape is the obvious opposite-sign associate,
caused by the central symmetry of \(X\).

## 1. The theorem

### Theorem 1 (global scalar-substitution coprimality)

For distinct positive integers \(r\ne s\),

\[
 \boxed{\gcd_{\mathbb Z[x]}(P(x^r),P(x^s))=1.}
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently, let \(\lambda,\mu\ne0\) be real scalars with
\(|\lambda|\ne|\mu|\).  In the integral group ring of the additive group
generated by \(\lambda X\cup\mu X\), the two masks

\[
 P_{\lambda X},\qquad P_{\mu X}
\]

are coprime.

### Corollary 2 (no exact-block second row for this \(X\))

There is no integral common complement \(H\) with

\[
 H(1)=5C,\qquad 1\le C<5,
\]

which is divisible by both \(P_{\lambda X}\) and \(P_{\mu X}\) for
\(|\lambda|\ne|\mu|\).  In particular the signed escape
\(M=PQ\), where \(Q(1)=2\), cannot be extended to a second nonassociate
scalar-copy row with the same common mask.

The condition on absolute values is necessary.  Reciprocity gives

\[
 x^6P(x^{-1})=P(x),
\]

so the copies at \(\lambda\) and \(-\lambda\) are Laurent associates.  They
are translations of one another, not a coprime/transverse pair.

## 2. Galois structure of the six roots

Use the reciprocal substitution

\[
 x^{-3}P(x)=f(x+x^{-1}),\qquad
 f(y)=y^3+y^2-3y-1.
\tag{2}
\]

The rational-root test makes \(f\) irreducible, and

\[
 \operatorname{disc}(f)=148=4\cdot37
\]

is not a square.  Moreover

\[
 f(-3)<0<f(-2),\qquad
 f(-\tfrac12)>0>f(0),\qquad
 f(1)<0<f(2).
\]

Thus \(f\) has three real roots \(y_1,y_2,y_3\), one outside
\([-2,2]\) and two inside it.  Its splitting field \(K\) is totally real,
has Galois group \(S_3\), and has the unique quadratic subfield
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{37})\).

Put

\[
 d_i=y_i^2-4,
 \qquad z_i^{\pm}=\frac{y_i\pm\sqrt{d_i}}2.
\]

Then \(z_i^-=(z_i^+)^{-1}\), and the six \(z_i^\pm\) are precisely the
roots of \(P\).  The three square classes \(d_1,d_2,d_3\) are independent
in \(K^\times/K^{\times2}\).  Here is a short exact check of all seven
possible relations.

First,

\[
 \prod_{i=1}^3d_i
 =\prod_i(y_i-2)\prod_i(y_i+2)
 =(-f(2))(-f(-2))=5.
\tag{3}
\]

The element \(5\) is not a square in \(K\), because an \(S_3\)-extension
has only one quadratic subfield and it is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{37})\), not
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).  This rules out the three-factor relation.

For a one-factor relation, an automorphism of \(K\) sends the chosen
\(y_i\) to either root lying in \((-2,2)\); the corresponding image of
\(d_i\) is negative.  Since \(K\) is totally real, a square cannot have a
negative real conjugate.  Thus no \(d_i\) is a square.  For a two-factor
relation, (3) gives

\[
 d_id_j=5/d_k.
\]

Send \(y_k\) to a root in \((-2,2)\).  The displayed conjugate is again
negative, so no two-factor product is a square.  This proves square-class
independence.

Therefore the splitting field of \(P\) is

\[
 L=K(\sqrt{d_1},\sqrt{d_2},\sqrt{d_3}),
 \qquad \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\cong C_2^3.
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, for each \(i\) there is an automorphism \(\tau_i\) which
interchanges \(z_i^+\) and \(z_i^-\) and fixes both roots in the other two
reciprocal pairs.  The full group is \(C_2^3\rtimes S_3\), of order \(48\),
and acts transitively on the six roots.  Hence \(P\) is irreducible.  Since
one reciprocal pair is off the unit circle, no root of \(P\) is a root of
unity.

## 3. Proof of substitution coprimality

Suppose \(P(x^r)\) and \(P(x^s)\) have a common root \(\alpha\).  Then

\[
 z=\alpha^r,\qquad w=\alpha^s
\]

are roots of \(P\), and

\[
 z^s=w^r.
\tag{5}
\]

If \(z,w\) lie in different reciprocal pairs, apply the automorphism which
inverts \(z\)'s pair and fixes \(w\)'s pair.  Equation (5) becomes
\(z^{-s}=w^r\).  Comparing with (5) gives \(z^{2s}=1\), impossible.

If \(z=w\), then (5) gives \(z^{s-r}=1\), so non-torsion forces \(r=s\).
If \(z=w^{-1}\), it gives \(z^{r+s}=1\), again impossible.  Thus no common
root exists when \(r\ne s\), proving (1).

For rationally dependent real scalars, clearing a common unit reduces their
absolute-value ratio to coprime positive integers \(r,s\), so (1) applies.
For rationally independent scalars, their group ring is a Laurent polynomial
ring in two independent variables; the irreducibles \(P(u)\) and \(P(v)\)
are visibly nonassociate and coprime.  This proves the real-scalar statement.

Finally, if both coprime masks divide an integral \(H\), their product divides
\(H\) in the Laurent UFD.  Write

\[
 H=P_{\lambda X}P_{\mu X}R.
\]

Augmentation gives

\[
 5C=H(1)=25R(1).
\]

Since \(R(1)\in\mathbb Z\), this forces \(5\mid C\), contradicting
\(1\le C<5\).  Corollary 2 follows.

## 4. What this does and does not settle

This is an unbounded theorem for the single fixed source
\(X=\{0,1,3,5,6\}\).  It upgrades the earlier finite `r,s<=100` search and
explains structurally why the current signed escape cannot acquire a genuine
second scalar row.  The executable `verify_second_scalar_global_structure.py`
guards the reciprocal identity, cubic discriminant and sign intervals,
product (3), order-48 Galois computation, and additional substitution gcds;
the unbounded conclusion rests on the proof above.

It does not show that an arbitrary source mask has substitution coprimality,
does not control a power-large family whose source \(X\) is different, and
does not provide the Euclidean outer extraction or a distance-exponent
improvement.  Erdős #1083 remains open.
