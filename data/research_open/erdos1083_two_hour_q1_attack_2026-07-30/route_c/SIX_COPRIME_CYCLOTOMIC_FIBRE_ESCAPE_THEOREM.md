# Erdős #1083: six-coprime cyclotomic fibre escape

Date: 2026-07-30

## 0. Outcome

The rational cyclotomic fibre theorem holds for every angular order
\[
\boxed{m>1,\qquad \gcd(m,6)=1.}
\tag{1}
\]
This strictly strengthens
`ROUGH_ORDER_CYCLOTOMIC_FIBRE_ESCAPE_THEOREM.md`: factors of \(5\)
are now allowed.  If \(\ell\ge5\) is the least prime divisor of \(m\),
then arbitrary equal-size angular fibres satisfy the sharp bound
\[
\boxed{
|\Delta^2(P)|\ge\frac{\ell-1}{2\ell}|P|.
}
\tag{2}
\]

The extra observation is sign-sensitive.  Mann's theorem permits a
five-term relation when \(5\mid m\), but an irreducible five-term
relation must be a regular pentagon with all coefficients equal.
A distance collision has two negative chord coefficients and two
positive chord coefficients, so it cannot be that relation.

Moreover, \(\gcd(m,6)=1\) is the exact arithmetic threshold for this
universal selected-label injection: divisibility by \(2\) or \(3\)
creates a rational chord and an explicit cross-radius/height collision.

This is a structured-family theorem, not an unconditional improvement
of the \(N^{3/5}\) distinct-distance exponent in dimension three.

## 1. Signed five-term rigidity

Let \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/m}\).  For a nonzero sign class modulo the odd
integer \(m\), use its representative
\[
1\le d\le\frac{m-1}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

### Lemma 1

Assume (1).  Let \(d,e\) satisfy (3), let \(R,S\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\),
and let \(C\in\mathbb Q\).  If
\[
C-R(\zeta^d+\zeta^{-d})
+S(\zeta^e+\zeta^{-e})=0,
\tag{4}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{d=e,\qquad R=S,\qquad C=0.}
\tag{5}
\]

#### Proof

First suppose \(d=e\).  If either \(R\ne S\) or \(C\ne0\), equation
(4), after collecting coefficients, is a nonzero rational relation
among at most three distinct \(m\)-th roots of unity.  Choose a
minimal vanishing subsum.  Mann's theorem says the ratio of any two
roots in a \(k\)-term minimal relation has order dividing
\[
\prod_{\substack{q\le k\\q\ {\rm prime}}}q,
\]
which divides \(6\) for \(k\le3\).  The ratio is also an \(m\)-th
root, and \(\gcd(m,6)=1\), so the ratio must be one.  This contradicts
distinctness.  Hence \(R=S\) and \(C=0\).

Now suppose \(d\ne e\).  The pairs
\(\{d,-d\}\) and \(\{e,-e\}\) are disjoint modulo \(m\).  If \(C=0\),
(4) has four terms; if \(C\ne0\), it has five.  Choose a minimal
vanishing subsum with \(k\le5\) terms.

For \(k\le4\), Mann's theorem makes every root ratio have order
dividing \(6\), producing the same contradiction as above.  Therefore
\(k=5\), so \(C\ne0\) and the full relation (4) is irreducible.
Mann's theorem now makes every root ratio have order dividing
\[
2\cdot3\cdot5=30.
\]
Because the ratios are also \(m\)-th roots and \(\gcd(m,6)=1\), their
orders divide \(5\).  The five distinct roots in (4) are consequently
one coset of the fifth roots of unity.

The rational relation space among the five roots in such a coset is
one-dimensional, generated by
\[
1+\omega+\omega^2+\omega^3+\omega^4=0.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus all five coefficients in (4) would have to be equal.  This is
impossible because the coefficients on the \(d\)-pair are
\(-R<0\), whereas those on the \(e\)-pair are \(S>0\).  Hence
\(d\ne e\) is impossible, completing the proof. \(\square\)

The Mann input is H. B. Mann, *On linear relations between roots of
unity*, Mathematika **12** (1965), 107--117,
[doi:10.1112/S0025579300005210](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300005210).
Only its standard minimal-relation order bound is used.

### Proposition 2 (exact injection classification)

For \(m>1\), consider the following property:

> For all nonzero angular sign classes \(d,e\), all
> \(R,S\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), and all \(H,K\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\),
> \[
> R(2-\zeta^d-\zeta^{-d})+H
> =
> S(2-\zeta^e-\zeta^{-e})+K
> \tag{7}
> \]
> implies \(d=e\), \(R=S\), and \(H=K\).

This property holds if and only if
\[
\boxed{\gcd(m,6)=1.}
\tag{8}
\]

#### Proof

The implication from (8) to the property is Lemma 1 after collecting
the rational constant in (7).

Conversely, if \(2\mid m\), then with \(d=m/2\),
\[
2-\zeta^d-\zeta^{-d}=4.
\]
Taking \((R,H)=(2,0)\) and \((S,K)=(1,4)\) violates the property.  If
\(3\mid m\), take \(d=m/3\).  Then
\[
2-\zeta^d-\zeta^{-d}=3,
\]
and \((R,H)=(2,0)\), \((S,K)=(1,3)\) gives a violation. \(\square\)

## 2. Fibre theorem

Use \(G=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\).  Let
\({\cal R}\subset\mathbb R_{>0}\) be a finite set of distinct radii
with \(r^2\in\mathbb Q\).  For each \(r\), let
\({\cal Z}_r\subset\mathbb R\) be finite and nonempty, put
\(z_r^-=\min{\cal Z}_r\), and assume
\[
(z-z_r^-)^2\in\mathbb Q.
\tag{9}
\]
At every radius-height pair choose an arbitrary nonempty angular
support \({\cal A}_{r,z}\subseteq G\), and define
\[
P=
\left\{
\left(
r\cos\frac{2\pi j}{m},
r\sin\frac{2\pi j}{m},
z
\right):
r\in{\cal R},\ z\in{\cal Z}_r,\ j\in{\cal A}_{r,z}
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Put
\[
{\cal D}_{r,z}
=
{\cal A}_{r,z}-{\cal A}_{r,z_r^-},
\qquad
H_{r,z}=\operatorname{Stab}({\cal D}_{r,z}),
\tag{11}
\]
let \(q_{r,z}\) be the number of nonzero sign classes
\(\{d,-d\}\) meeting \({\cal D}_{r,z}\), and define
\[
L_{r,z}
=
|{\cal A}_{r,z}+H_{r,z}|
+|{\cal A}_{r,z_r^-}+H_{r,z}|
-|H_{r,z}|.
\tag{12}
\]

### Theorem 3

The nonzero squared-distance set of \(P\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
|\Delta^2(P)|
\ge
\sum_{r,z}q_{r,z}
\ge
\sum_{r,z}
\left\lceil\frac{L_{r,z}-1}{2}\right\rceil.
}
\tag{13}
\]

#### Proof

For every counted sign class, select its representative \(d\) from
(3).  The corresponding anchor-to-fibre squared distance is
\[
\lambda(r,z,d)
=
r^2(2-\zeta^d-\zeta^{-d})+(z-z_r^-)^2.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(\lambda(r,z,d)=\lambda(s,w,e)\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
0={}&
2r^2-2s^2
+(z-z_r^-)^2-(w-z_s^-)^2\\
&-r^2(\zeta^d+\zeta^{-d})
+s^2(\zeta^e+\zeta^{-e}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Lemma 1, with \(R=r^2\) and \(S=s^2\), gives
\[
d=e,\qquad r^2=s^2.
\]
The radii are positive and distinct, so \(r=s\).  The remaining
constant equality in (15) gives equal anchored height squares.  Both
heights lie above the same minimum \(z_r^-\), hence \(z=w\).
Thus all selected labels are distinct.

Kneser's theorem gives
\[
|{\cal D}_{r,z}|\ge L_{r,z}.
\]
After discarding zero, each sign class contains at most two elements,
so
\[
q_{r,z}
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{|{\cal D}_{r,z}|-1}{2}\right\rceil
\ge
\left\lceil\frac{L_{r,z}-1}{2}\right\rceil.
\]
This proves (13). \(\square\)

## 3. Consequences and sharpness

### Corollary 4 (equal fibres)

If every angular fibre has size \(S\ge2\), then (2) holds.

#### Proof

For one anchored difference set, let \(H\) be its stabilizer and
\(h=|H|\).  If \(H\) is trivial, Kneser gives
\[
q_{r,z}\ge S-1\ge\frac{\ell-1}{2\ell}S.
\]
If \(H\) is nontrivial, then \(h\ge\ell\).  With
\(c=\lceil S/h\rceil\), Kneser gives
\[
L_{r,z}\ge(2c-1)h,\qquad S\le ch,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{q_{r,z}}S
\ge
\frac{(2c-1)h-1}{2ch}
\ge
\frac{h-1}{2h}
\ge
\frac{\ell-1}{2\ell}.
\]
Sum over the \(|P|/S\) fibres. \(\square\)

The constant is sharp.  On one radius and one height, take the unique
order-\(\ell\) subgroup of \(G\) as the angular support.  This is a
regular \(\ell\)-gon with exactly \((\ell-1)/2\) nonzero distances.
In particular, when \(5\mid m\), equality gives the constant \(2/5\).

### Corollary 5 (aperiodic equal fibres)

If every angular fibre has size \(S\ge2\) and every
\({\cal D}_{r,z}\) is aperiodic, then
\[
\boxed{
|\Delta^2(P)|\ge\left(1-\frac1S\right)|P|.
}
\tag{16}
\]

### Corollary 6 (complete fibres)

If every angular fibre is all of \(G\), then
\[
\boxed{
|\Delta^2(P)|\ge\frac{m-1}{2m}|P|.
}
\tag{17}
\]

### Corollary 7 (real base-field extension)

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R\) be a characteristic-zero field for which
\(\Phi_m\) is irreducible over \(K\).  Replace the rationality
assumptions by
\[
r^2\in K,\qquad (z-z_r^-)^2\in K.
\]
Then Theorem 3 and Corollaries 4--6 remain valid.

#### Proof

Irreducibility gives
\[
[K(\zeta):K]=\varphi(m)=[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q].
\]
Consequently every \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent subset of
\(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\) remains \(K\)-linearly independent: extend it
to a rational basis and compare with the usual power basis.

In Lemma 1, every support of size at most four is independent over
\(\mathbb Q\), and hence over \(K\).  The only possible five-root
support is a coset of the fifth roots; its relation space has
dimension one over both fields and is generated by the all-ones
pentagon relation.  The coefficients \(-R\) and \(+S\), with
\(R,S>0\) in the ordered real field \(K\), still cannot be equal.
Thus Lemma 1, selected-label injection, and the subsequent
field-free Kneser argument all persist. \(\square\)

## 4. Exact boundary

The condition \(\gcd(m,6)=1\) is used in two places: it eliminates
all minimal relations of length at most four, and it leaves only the
regular-pentagon possibility at length five.  The sign pattern in
(15) eliminates that last possibility.

The signed rigidity and the selected-label injection genuinely fail
at every order divisible by \(2\) or \(3\), as Proposition 2 shows.
This does not by itself disprove the aggregate distance lower bound
(13), because unselected distances might compensate for a selected
collision.  At \(m=8\), the chord values
\(a_d=2-\zeta^d-\zeta^{-d}\) satisfy
\[
a_2=2,\qquad a_4=4,
\]
giving a positive rational cross-radius collision.  At \(m=9\),
\[
a_3=3\in\mathbb Q.
\]
Thus the two selected labels
\[
2a_3+0
\quad\text{and}\quad
1a_3+3
\]
coincide although their squared radii and anchored height squares
differ.

Without irreducibility over the coefficient field, new short
dependencies can appear and no extension is asserted.

## 5. Role in Erdős #1083

The theorem eliminates the full class of anchored coaxial
cyclotomic fibres of angular order prime to six, even with arbitrary
height-dependent deletion and periodicity of the angular supports.
It strictly enlarges the previously audited prime, prime-power, and
\(30\)-coprime terminals.

The unresolved bridge is still
\[
\boxed{
\text{critical cross-plane distance reuse}
\Longrightarrow
\text{a large anchored six-coprime fibre or another expanding chart}.
}
\tag{18}
\]
Without that inverse theorem, no improvement of the global \(3/5\)
exponent follows.
