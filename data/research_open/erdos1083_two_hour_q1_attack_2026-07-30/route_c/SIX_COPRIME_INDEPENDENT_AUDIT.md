No counterexample was found at \(m=5,25,35\), either on a substantially

## 1. Mann's theorem at five terms

Use the standard signed-coefficient form of Mann's theorem.  If
\[
\sum_{j=1}^k c_j\omega_j=0,\qquad c_j\in\mathbb Q^\times,
\]
is irreducible, then every quotient \(\omega_i/\omega_j\) has order
dividing
\[
P_k=\prod_{\substack{p\le k\\p\ {\rm prime}}}p.
\]
Clearing rational denominators does not change the roots or
irreducibility.

For \(k\le4\), \(P_k\mid6\).  Every root in Lemma 1 is an \(m\)-th
root, so under \(\gcd(m,6)=1\) every quotient in such a subsum would
have order one.  This contradicts the distinctness of the collected
roots.

For \(k=5\),
\[
P_5=30.
\]
The quotient order divides both \(m\) and \(30\).  Since
\(\gcd(m,6)=1\), it divides \(5\).  Therefore all roots in the
irreducible subsum lie in one coset of \(\mu_5\).  In the
\(d\ne e\), \(C\ne0\) case there are exactly five distinct roots, so
they exhaust that coset.

After factoring out its common coset representative, a rational
relation on those roots is a relation among
\[
1,\omega,\omega^2,\omega^3,\omega^4,
\qquad \Phi_5(\omega)=0.
\]
Their rational span has dimension four, hence their rational relation
space is one-dimensional and is generated by
\[
1+\omega+\omega^2+\omega^3+\omega^4=0.
\]
Thus all five collected coefficients must be equal.

This is impossible for the distance relation
\[
C-R(\zeta^d+\zeta^{-d})
+S(\zeta^e+\zeta^{-e})=0,
\]
because its \(d\)-pair has coefficient \(-R<0\) and its \(e\)-pair
has coefficient \(S>0\).  The pentagon possibility is therefore
eliminated exactly as claimed.

The primary source is H. B. Mann, *On linear relations between roots
of unity*, Mathematika **12** (1965), 107--117,
doi:10.1112/S0025579300005210.

## 2. The \(d=e\) case

When \(d=e\), equation (4) becomes
\[
C+(S-R)(\zeta^d+\zeta^{-d})=0.
\]
The roots \(1,\zeta^d,\zeta^{-d}\) are distinct: \(d\ne0\), \(m\) is
odd, and hence \(d\not\equiv-d\pmod m\).

If the collected relation were nonzero, a minimal vanishing subsum
would have two or three terms; a one-term sum cannot vanish.  Mann's
bound then divides \(6\), which is coprime to \(m\), and forces two
distinct roots to be equal.  Hence the collected relation is zero:
\[
S-R=0,\qquad C=0.
\]
This verifies the part of Lemma 1 that is also used when the two
selected angular classes agree but their radii or heights differ.

The manuscript leaves the impossibility of a one-term vanishing sum
implicit.  This is elementary and not a logical gap, though the words
“necessarily \(2\le k\le3\)” would make the proof maximally explicit.

## 3. Exact universal-injection threshold

Proposition 2 asserts that, for every choice of nonzero sign classes,
positive rational \(R,S\), and nonnegative rational \(H,K\),
\[
R a_d+H=S a_e+K
\]
is trivial if and only if \(\gcd(m,6)=1\).

The forward direction is precisely Lemma 1 after moving the rational
terms to the constant coefficient.

For the converse:

- if \(2\mid m\), the nonzero class \(d=m/2\) has \(a_d=4\), and
  \[
  2a_d+0=1a_d+4;
  \]
- if \(3\mid m\), the nonzero class \(d=m/3\) has \(a_d=3\), and
  \[
  2a_d+0=1a_d+3.
  \]

Both are allowed positive-radius/nonnegative-height-square data, and
both have equal angular classes but unequal radii and height offsets.
Thus every order divisible by \(2\) or \(3\) fails the universal
property.  The iff statement is exact.

## 4. Injection across fibres

Equality of two selected labels gives Lemma 1 with
\[
R=r^2,\qquad S=s^2,
\]
and a rational constant formed from the two radial and anchored-height
terms.  Lemma 1 gives \(d=e\), \(r^2=s^2\), and equality of that
constant.

Since radii are positive, \(r^2=s^2\) gives \(r=s\).  The two height
offsets are measured from the same minimum \(z_r^-\), and both are
nonnegative, so equality of their squares gives \(z=w\).  Thus selected
labels are injective across angular sign classes, radii, and heights.

If an anchored difference set meets only \(-d\), rather than the
canonical representative \(d\), the same distance is still realized
because \(a_{-d}=a_d\).  No orientation case is missing.

All selected labels are nonzero: \(d\ne0\), \(r>0\), and the height
square is nonnegative.

## 5. Kneser constant and sharpness

Let \(\ell\) be the least prime divisor of \(m\).  Under
\(\gcd(m,6)=1\), \(\ell\ge5\).  If the stabilizer \(H\) is trivial,
Kneser's theorem gives
\[
q\ge S-1\ge\frac{\ell-1}{2\ell}S.
\]

If \(H\) is nontrivial, \(h=|H|\) is a nontrivial divisor of \(m\), so
\(h\ge\ell\).  With \(c=\lceil S/h\rceil\),
\[
L\ge(2c-1)h,\qquad S\le ch.
\]
The sign-class count gives
\[
\frac qS
\ge\frac{(2c-1)h-1}{2ch}
\ge\frac{h-1}{2h}
\ge\frac{\ell-1}{2\ell}.
\]
The middle inequality is equivalent to
\[
(c-1)(h+1)\ge0,
\]
so it contains no hidden parity or ceiling assumption.

The unique order-\(\ell\) subgroup of the cyclic angular group gives a
regular \(\ell\)-gon.  It has \(\ell\) points and exactly
\((\ell-1)/2\) nonzero distances.  Hence the uniform constant is sharp;
for \(5\mid m\), its sharp value is \(2/5\).

The aperiodic and complete-fibre corollaries follow from the same
sign-class count and also pass.

## 6. The \(m=9\) boundary

At \(m=9\),
\[
a_3=2-\zeta^3-\zeta^{-3}=3.
\]
Therefore
\[
2a_3+0=1a_3+3=6
\]
is an exact rational collision.  It is the primitive positive-integer
example in the displayed normalization and correctly disproves Lemma
1 and the universal selected-label property.

It does not by itself disprove the aggregate lower bound in Theorem 3.
For example, realize it with:

- squared radius \(2\), height \(0\), angular support \(\{0,3\}\);
- squared radius \(1\), anchor height \(0\), support \(\{0\}\);
- squared radius \(1\), height \(\sqrt3\), support \(\{3\}\).

Here
\[
\sum_{r,z}q_{r,z}=2,
\]
but the complete point set has five distinct nonzero squared distances,
\[
\{6,\ 3-2\sqrt2,\ 3+\sqrt2,\ 6-2\sqrt2,\ 6+\sqrt2\}.
\]
Thus the current manuscript's careful boundary statement is correct:
the injection mechanism fails, while failure of the aggregate
inequality is not asserted.

An additional exact enumeration of all \(129^3=2{,}146{,}689\)
configurations in this three-fibre template with nonempty angular
supports of size at most three found no violation of the aggregate
bound; the minimum observed gap
\(|\Delta^2(P)|-\sum q_{r,z}\) was \(2\).  This is falsification
evidence only, not a proof for excluded orders.

## 7. Independent collision searches

Two checks stronger than the supplied finite grid were performed.

First, positive rational squared radii were taken from all distinct
fractions \(n/d\) with \(1\le n\le12\), \(1\le d\le6\), and
nonnegative height squares from all \(n/d\) with
\(0\le n\le12\), \(1\le d\le6\).  Exact quotient-ring labels were
hashed for every canonical angular class:

| \(m\) | exact labels checked | nontrivial collisions |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \(5\) | 4,324 | 0 |
| \(25\) | 25,944 | 0 |
| \(35\) | 36,754 | 0 |

Second, for every pair \(d,e\), the nonconstant coordinates of
\(a_d,a_e\) in the exact \(\mathbb Q[X]/(\Phi_m)\) basis were compared.
A positive-rational collision can exist only if those vectors are
positive rational multiples.  At \(m=5,25,35\), this occurs only for
\(d=e\) with ratio one; the constant coordinate then forces equal
height squares.

Equivalently, the only cross-class rational dependencies found were the
pentagon identities
\[
a_1+a_2=5\quad(m=5),\qquad
a_5+a_{10}=5\quad(m=25),\qquad
a_7+a_{14}=5\quad(m=35).
\]
They require opposite signs for the two putative squared radii and
therefore cannot be distance-label collisions with \(R,S>0\).

## 8. Real base-field extension

Let \(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), and suppose \(\Phi_m\) is irreducible over
a characteristic-zero field \(K\subset\mathbb R\).  Then
\[
[K(\zeta):K]=\varphi(m)=[F:\mathbb Q].
\]
The power family
\[
1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{\varphi(m)-1}
\]
is therefore a basis over both \(\mathbb Q\) and \(K\).

Take any \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent subset of \(F\), extend it
to a \(\mathbb Q\)-basis, and express that basis in the power basis.
The change-of-basis matrix has rational entries and nonzero rational
determinant.  The same matrix is invertible over \(K\).  Consequently
the chosen subset remains \(K\)-linearly independent.

This proves the exact independence transfer used in Corollary 7; it is
equivalently the standard linear-disjointness consequence of the
irreducibility hypothesis.

For a support of at most four roots, rational Mann rigidity gives
\(\mathbb Q\)-independence, hence \(K\)-independence.  For five roots,
there are two cases:

1. the roots are \(\mathbb Q\)-independent, and remain
   \(K\)-independent;
2. they form a fifth-root coset.  Four of them are
   \(\mathbb Q\)-independent and hence \(K\)-independent, while the
   rational all-ones relation still holds.  Thus their \(K\)-relation
   space is exactly one-dimensional and is generated by the same
   pentagon relation.

In the second case all five \(K\)-coefficients would still have to be
equal.  Since \(K\subset\mathbb R\), the natural order makes
\(-R<0<S\), so the distance sign pattern remains impossible.

The subsequent equality of anchored height squares again uses only the
real order.  Kneser's theorem is coefficient-field free.  Therefore the
whole theorem and Corollaries 4--6 extend exactly as stated.

Irreducibility is a sufficient and necessary hypothesis for the
independence-transfer argument being used; without it, the degree can
drop and new \(K\)-linear root relations may appear.  The manuscript
correctly makes no broader claim.

## 10. Scope

The theorem is a correct sign-sensitive strengthening of the
\(30\)-coprime result to the exact universal-injection range
\(\gcd(m,6)=1\).  It remains a structured-family terminal: it does not
extract a cyclotomic fibre from an arbitrary critical configuration and
does not improve the unconditional \(N^{3/5}\) exponent for Erdős
#1083.
