# Erdős #1083: a mask-factor sunflower inverse theorem for the high-rank quotient clique

Date: 2026-08-02

## 0. Result

The high-rank residual in
`EXACT_BLOCK_TRANSVERSE_STAR_CLUSTER.md` has a stronger exact
form than pairwise intersection of rational direction spaces.

Let one row \(0\) of an exact block be transverse to a leaf family
\(L\), and assume

\[
 V=A_i\oplus\lambda_iX,\qquad |X|=S,\qquad |A_i|=U<S^2.
\tag{0.1}
\]

In one common Laurent-polynomial UFD, put

\[
 F_i=P_{\lambda_iX}.
\tag{0.2}
\]

Then:

1. every leaf mask \(F_j\) divides the centre complement mask
   \(P_{A_0}\), whose augmentation is only \(U\) (in particular,
   \(S\mid U\));
2. every two leaf masks have a common nonunit irreducible factor;
3. if one leaf mask has \(r\) distinct irreducible factors, one of
   those factors is common to at least

   \[
   1+\frac{|L|-1}{r}
   \tag{0.3}
   \]

   leaf masks.

The common factor has a nonzero Newton direction contained in every
corresponding row space.  Thus it gives a genuine common rational
direction, not merely pair-dependent intersections.

For the common-tangent leaf family

\[
 |L|=t^{5/9+o(1)}.
\tag{0.4}
\]

Consequently, unless a row mask has polynomially many irreducible
factors of augmentation \(\pm1\), a power-large subfamily shares one
actual mask factor and one common Newton direction.  The exact
surviving obstruction is therefore an **augmentation-unit
factor-rich source mask**, not arbitrary high-dimensional linear
algebra.

This is an inverse theorem inside the exact block.  It does not prove
that the exact block is extracted from the original near-extremal
\(N\)-point set, and a common mask factor is not yet a
\(t^3\)-distance contradiction.

## 1. Common complement divisibility

Let \(\Gamma\) be the finitely generated additive subgroup of
\(\mathbb R\) generated by every element appearing in the finite
block.  It is torsion free, so

\[
 \mathbb Z[\Gamma]
 \cong\mathbb Z[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_d^{\pm1}]
\tag{1.1}
\]

is a UFD.  Exact directness gives

\[
 P_V=P_{A_i}F_i
 \qquad(i\in\{0\}\cup L).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The centre is transverse to a leaf \(j\), so the Newton-direction
argument in the torsion-free direct-tiling theorem gives

\[
 \gcd(F_0,F_j)=1.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Since \(F_j\mid P_V=P_{A_0}F_0\), Euclid's lemma in the UFD yields

\[
 \boxed{F_j\mid P_{A_0}}
 \qquad(j\in L).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Applying augmentation to (1.4) also gives the exact arithmetic
condition

\[
 \boxed{S\mid U}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

This is the first place where the unused \(U-1\) tangent cells enter:
all leaf copies of \(X\) must factor the one \(U\)-term complement
mask belonging to the centre row.

## 2. Pairwise common irreducible factors

Suppose distinct leaves \(j,k\) had coprime masks.  Equations (1.4)
and unique factorization would imply

\[
 F_jF_k\mid P_{A_0}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Apply augmentation, which maps every monomial to one.  There would be
an integer \(c\) with

\[
 U=P_{A_0}(1)=F_j(1)F_k(1)c=S^2c.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Because \(0<U<S^2\), this is impossible.  Hence:

### Lemma 1 (factor-intersection clique)

Every pair of leaf masks has a common nonunit irreducible Laurent
factor.

No constant prime can be such a factor: every mask polynomial has
content one.  Therefore a common nonunit factor \(G\) has a
positive-dimensional Newton polytope.  If \(G\mid F_j\), then

\[
 \operatorname{dir}(\operatorname{Newt}G)
 \subseteq W_j\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R.
\tag{2.3}
\]

A factor shared by several leaves supplies one common nonzero
direction space across all of them.

## 3. Factor sunflower

Fix a leaf \(j_*\).  Let

\[
 \mathcal I_*=
 \{\text{distinct nonunit irreducible divisors of }F_{j_*}\},
 \qquad r_*=|\mathcal I_*|.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For every other leaf \(j\), Lemma 1 lets us choose one factor in

\[
 \mathcal I_*\cap\operatorname{Irr}(F_j).
\]

Pigeonholing these \(|L|-1\) choices over \(r_*\) factors proves:

### Theorem 2 (mask-factor sunflower)

Some nonunit irreducible factor divides at least

\[
 \boxed{
 1+\left\lceil\frac{|L|-1}{r_*}\right\rceil}
\tag{3.2}
\]

leaf masks.  Its Newton direction is common to all row spaces in
that subfamily.

One may choose \(j_*\) to minimize \(r_*\) over the leaves.

## 4. The exact augmentation-unit residual

Every irreducible divisor \(G\) of a leaf mask has

\[
 G(1)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},
\]

because the product of all factor augmentations is
\(F_j(1)=S\ne0\).  Count factors with multiplicity.  At most

\[
 \lfloor\log_2S\rfloor
\tag{4.1}
\]

of them can satisfy \(|G(1)|\ge2\); otherwise the absolute value of
their product already exceeds \(S\).

Let \(a_j\) be the number of distinct irreducible divisors of \(F_j\)
with

\[
 |G(1)|=1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
 r_j\le a_j+\lfloor\log_2S\rfloor.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (3.2)--(4.3) gives the quantitative inverse theorem

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{common-factor subfamily size}
 \ \ge\
 1+\frac{|L|-1}{a_*+\lfloor\log_2S\rfloor},}
\tag{4.4}
\]

where \(a_*=\min_{j\in L}a_j\).

At \(|L|=t^{5/9+o(1)}\), for every fixed \(\eta>0\) one obtains the
exact dichotomy:

1. a common irreducible mask factor and common Newton direction on
   \(t^{5/9-\eta+o(1)}\) leaves; or
2. every leaf mask has at least \(t^{\eta+o(1)}\) distinct
   augmentation-unit irreducible factors.

In particular, if \(a_*=t^{o(1)}\), an almost-full
\(t^{5/9-o(1)}\) leaf subfamily shares one factor.

## 5. Why the factor-rich branch is a real quantifier obstruction

There is no bound for \(a_j\) in terms of \(|X|\) alone when the
ambient lattice \(\Gamma\) may be highly saturated.  In one Laurent
variable, view the two-term mask

\[
 1+x^m
\tag{5.1}
\]

inside \(\mathbb Z[x^{\pm1}]\).  If \(m\) is the product of \(k\)
distinct odd primes, then

\[
 1+x^m=\prod_{d\mid m}\Phi_{2d}(x).
\tag{5.2}
\]

It has \(2^k\) distinct irreducible factors although its support has
only two monomials.  Exactly one factor, \(x+1\), has augmentation
two; the other \(2^k-1\) factors have augmentation one.

In the subgroup generated by the two exponents alone, (5.1) becomes
\(1+y\).  The proliferation appears when other block data refine the
common ambient lattice by a factor \(m\).  The inherited Euclidean
problem imposes no denominator/ambient-saturation bound, so this
exception cannot be discarded.

This example is not an exact #1083 block and not a counterexample to
the public problem.  It proves that the final alternative in Section
4 is logically necessary unless new geometric information controls
ambient-lattice saturation or augmentation-unit factors.

## 6. Original-problem interface

The exact chain is

```text
literal exact common-spectrum block
    + transverse-heavy common-tangent windmill
        -> t^(5/9) leaf masks divide one U-term complement mask
        -> pairwise common irreducible factors
        -> common-factor sunflower OR augmentation-unit factor richness.
```

Two arrows remain before an improvement over
\(f_3(N)\gg N^{3/5-o(1)}\):

1. a stability theorem extracting the literal block from the original
   near-direct endpoint; and
2. a denominator-free Euclidean escape theorem for the common-factor
   chart, or an exclusion of the augmentation-unit-rich branch using
   tangent geometry.

Neither arrow is claimed here.
