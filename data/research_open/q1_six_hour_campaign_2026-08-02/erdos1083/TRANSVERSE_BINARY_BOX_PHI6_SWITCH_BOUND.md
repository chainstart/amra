# Erdős #1083: the signed transverse binary-box \(\Phi _6\) switch bound

Date: 2026-08-02

Status: **PROVED — INDEPENDENT CROSS-AUDIT PASSED AFTER NUMERIC REPAIR**

## 0. Outcome

The mixed \(1/3\)-step tensor construction cannot be repaired to the
exact-block interface by merely replacing its centre with a transverse
scalar copy and allowing a signed regularizer.

Let \(u_1,\ldots,u_k\) be \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent leaf
directions and put

\[
 A_i=1+z_i,\qquad T_i=1-z_i+z_i^2,\qquad
 A_iT_i=1+z_i^3.
\tag{0.1}
\]

For \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^k\), define the mixed binary-box source mask

\[
 F_\epsilon=\prod_{i=1}^k A_iT_i^{\epsilon_i}.
\tag{0.2}
\]

Every \(F_\epsilon\) has

\[
 S=2^k
\tag{0.3}
\]

terms. Let \(v_1,\ldots,v_k\) be \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent
centre directions, and let the centre be the \(S\)-term binary box

\[
 F_0=\prod_{i=1}^k(1+y_i),
\tag{0.4}
\]

whose rational direction space is transverse to
\(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{u_i\}\).  Suppose a family
\(\mathcal E\subseteq\{0,1\}^k\) occurs inside a literal common-spectrum
block:

\[
 F_0P_{A_0}=F_\epsilon P_{A_\epsilon}=P_V,
 \qquad
 P_{A_0},P_{A_\epsilon}\text{ are masks},
\tag{0.5}
\]

with

\[
 P_{A_\epsilon}(1)=U=SC,\qquad 1\le C<S.
\tag{0.6}
\]

The complementary quotients are allowed to be signed.  Then every pair
\(\epsilon,\eta\in\mathcal E\) obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 |\epsilon\setminus\eta|\le\lfloor\log_2C\rfloor,\qquad
 |\eta\setminus\epsilon|\le\lfloor\log_2C\rfloor.}
\tag{0.7}
\]

Consequently, with \(L=\lfloor\log_2C\rfloor\),

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathcal E|
 \le\sum_{r=0}^{2L}\binom kr.}
\tag{0.8}
\]

At the exact tensor calibration \(k=14\ell\), \(C=2^\ell\),
\(S=2^{14\ell}\), and \(t=2^{18\ell}\), this gives

\[
 |\mathcal E|
 \le 2^{\,kH_2(1/7)}
 =S^{H_2(1/7)}
 =t^{(7/9)H_2(1/7)},
\tag{0.9}
\]

where

\[
 H_2(1/7)=0.5916727785823273\ldots
\tag{0.10}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 (7/9)H_2(1/7)
 =0.4601899388973657\ldots
 <5/9.}
\tag{0.11}
\]

The exponent margin is

\[
 5/9-(7/9)H_2(1/7)
 =0.0953656166581898\ldots.
\tag{0.12}
\]

There is an even cleaner common-\(X\) corollary.  The two endpoint masks

\[
 F_{\mathbf0}=P_X,\qquad
 F_{\mathbf1}=P_{3X}
\tag{0.13}
\]

are genuine scalar copies of the same binary box.  Their one-sided
Hamming difference is \(k\), so (0.7) forces

\[
 C\ge2^k=S,
\tag{0.14}
\]

contrary to the exact-block inequality \(C=U/S<S\).  Thus even these two
uniform scalar-copy endpoints cannot coexist with a transverse
binary-box centre in the strict block.

The proof has two independent ingredients:

1. a binary-box centre always has a finite quotient tiling which can be
   chosen to annihilate the transverse leaf span; and
2. a nonnegative integral multiple of \(d\) independent \(\Phi_6\)
   factors has mass at least \(2^d\), by its Newton zonotope.

No nonnegativity of the original complementary quotient, nor of its
ordinary projection, is assumed.

## 1. The centre quotient separates the leaf span

Let \(\Gamma\) be the finitely generated torsion-free exponent group
generated by all masks in (0.5), and put
\(V=\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\).  Write

\[
 W_0=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\},
 \qquad
 W_1=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{u_1,\ldots,u_k\},
\tag{1.1}
\]

where \(y_i=[v_i]\).  The transverse hypothesis is

\[
 W_0\cap W_1=\{0\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Choose rational linear functionals
\(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k:V\to\mathbb Q\) such that

\[
 \ell_i(v_j)=\delta_{ij},\qquad
 \ell_i(W_1)=0.
\tag{1.3}
\]

They exist by (1.2).  Since \(\Gamma\) is finitely generated, there is a
positive integer \(D\) with

\[
 D\ell_i(\Gamma)\subseteq\mathbb Z
 \qquad(1\le i\le k).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Define

\[
 G_D=(\mathbb Z/2D\mathbb Z)^k,\qquad
 \pi(\gamma)=
 \bigl(D\ell_1(\gamma),\ldots,D\ell_k(\gamma)\bigr)\pmod{2D}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Then

\[
 \pi(v_i)=De_i,\qquad \pi(W_1\cap\Gamma)=0.
\tag{1.6}
\]

The image of the centre is the subgroup mask

\[
 \pi(F_0)=P_H,\qquad H=\{0,D\}^k\le G_D.
\tag{1.7}
\]

Taking

\[
 Y=\{0,1,\ldots,D-1\}^k
\tag{1.8}
\]

gives the exact tiling

\[
 \boxed{P_HP_Y=P_{G_D}.}
\tag{1.9}
\]

To retain the leaf factors rather than collapse them, let
\(\psi:\Gamma\to\overline\Gamma\) be the natural image of \(\Gamma\) in
\(V/W_0\), and use the combined homomorphism

\[
 \Phi=(\pi,\psi):
 \Gamma\longrightarrow G_D\oplus\overline\Gamma.
\tag{1.10}
\]

The restriction of \(\psi\) to \(W_1\cap\Gamma\) is injective by
(1.2), while \(\psi(v_i)=0\).  Therefore

\[
 \Phi(F_0)=P_H,\qquad
 \Phi(z_i)=(0,\overline z_i),
\tag{1.11}
\]

and the \(\overline z_i\)'s remain \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent.

### Lemma 1.1 (separating binary-box shadow)

Let \(B=F_0Q\) be a nonzero \(0/1\) mask and \(Q(1)=C>0\). Then there is a
nonnegative integral
\(K\in\mathbb Z[\overline\Gamma]\) such that

\[
 \boxed{P_Y\Phi(B)=P_{G_D}K,\qquad K(1)=C.}
\tag{1.12}
\]

If \(R\in\mathbb Z[W_1\cap\Gamma]\) divides \(B\), then

\[
 \boxed{\Phi(R)\mid K.}
\tag{1.13}
\]

#### Proof

By (1.9)--(1.11),

\[
 P_Y\Phi(B)
 =P_Y\Phi(F_0)\Phi(Q)
 =P_{G_D}\Phi(Q).
\tag{1.14}
\]

The left side has nonnegative integer coefficients: \(\Phi\) can only
merge coefficients of the mask \(B\), and multiplication by \(P_Y\)
preserves nonnegativity.  Multiplication by \(P_{G_D}\) makes the
coefficient constant in the \(G_D\)-coordinate.  Hence (1.14) has the
unique form \(P_{G_D}K\) with \(K\ge0\).

Here \(|G_D|=(2D)^k\), \(|Y|=D^k\), and \(B(1)=SC=2^kC\).  Taking
augmentations in (1.12) gives

\[
 D^k2^kC=(2D)^kK(1),
\tag{1.15}
\]

so \(K(1)=C\) exactly.

If \(B=RT\), then \(\Phi(R)\) is independent of the \(G_D\)-coordinate
by (1.6).  Equation (1.12) becomes

\[
 P_{G_D}K=\Phi(R)P_Y\Phi(T).
\tag{1.16}
\]

Comparing the coefficient of any one element of \(G_D\) proves
\(K=\Phi(R)K'\) for an integral \(K'\).  This proves (1.13). QED.

Thus the signed quotient has been replaced by a positive shadow without
losing any factor supported in the transverse leaf span.  No direct-sum
or primitivity assumption on the ambient lattice was hidden: the
denominator \(D\) in (1.4) absorbs its finite-index defects.

## 2. Newton-zonotope mass

### Lemma 2.1

Let \(w_1,\ldots,w_d\) be \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent directions,
write \(x_i=[w_i]\), and put

\[
 R_D=\prod_{i=1}^d(1-x_i+x_i^2).
\tag{2.1}
\]

If a nonzero Laurent polynomial \(K\) has nonnegative integer
coefficients and \(R_D\mid K\), then

\[
 \boxed{K(1)\ge2^d.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

Write \(K=R_DL\).  Newton polytopes are exactly additive under
multiplication in a torsion-free Laurent domain, even if the interior
coefficients cancel:

\[
 \operatorname{Newt}(K)
 =\operatorname{Newt}(L)
  +\sum_{i=1}^d[0,2w_i].
\tag{2.3}
\]

The second summand is a \(d\)-dimensional parallelotope with \(2^d\)
vertices.  The normal fan of a Minkowski sum is the common refinement of
the two normal fans. Each of the \(2^d\) full-dimensional vertex cones of
the parallelotope is covered by cones of the refinement, and a finite
union of lower-dimensional cones cannot cover its interior. It therefore
contains a full-dimensional refined cone; different vertex-cone interiors
give different refined cones. Hence Minkowski addition cannot decrease
the number of full-dimensional vertex cones of this parallelotope, and
\(\operatorname{Newt}(K)\) has at least \(2^d\) vertices.

Every Newton vertex is an actual support exponent of \(K\).  Because the
coefficients of \(K\) are nonnegative integers, each contributes at
least one to \(K(1)\).  This proves (2.2).

The bound is sharp:

\[
 R_D\prod_{i=1}^d(1+x_i)
 =\prod_{i=1}^d(1+x_i^3),
\tag{2.4}
\]

whose right side has exactly \(2^d\) terms. QED.

Nonnegativity is used only in the final conversion from Newton vertices
to coefficient mass.  The other multiplier \(L\) may be signed, and no
positivity or factorization of \(L\) is required.

## 3. Pairwise Hamming rigidity

First note that \(F_0\) is coprime to every \(F_\epsilon\).  Any common
nonunit factor would have a nonzero Newton direction lying in both
\(W_0\) and \(W_1\), contrary to (1.2); constant prime factors are
excluded because masks are primitive.

Comparing the centre row with the \(\epsilon\)-row in (0.5), Euclid's
lemma therefore gives

\[
 P_{A_\epsilon}=F_0Q_\epsilon,\qquad
 Q_\epsilon(1)=C.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Fix \(\epsilon,\eta\in\mathcal E\), and put

\[
 I_+=\{i:\epsilon_i=1,\ \eta_i=0\},\qquad
 I_-=\{i:\epsilon_i=0,\ \eta_i=1\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Cancel the coordinatewise gcd of \(F_\epsilon\) and \(F_\eta\) from

\[
 F_\epsilon P_{A_\epsilon}
 =F_\eta P_{A_\eta}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The residual identity is

\[
 \left(\prod_{i\in I_+}T_i\right)P_{A_\epsilon}
 =
 \left(\prod_{i\in I_-}T_i\right)P_{A_\eta}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The two residual products are coprime: different \(T_i\)'s have
independent Newton directions, and \(1+z_i\) is coprime to
\(1-z_i+z_i^2\).  Euclid's lemma gives

\[
 \prod_{i\in I_+}T_i\mid P_{A_\eta},\qquad
 \prod_{i\in I_-}T_i\mid P_{A_\epsilon}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Apply Lemma 1.1 to the first positive mask in (3.5).  It gives a
nonnegative shadow \(K_\eta\) of mass \(C\), still divisible by the
\(|I_+|\) independent \(\Phi_6\) factors.  Lemma 2.1 yields

\[
 C\ge2^{|I_+|}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Interchanging \(\epsilon,\eta\) gives

\[
 C\ge2^{|I_-|}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Equations (3.6)--(3.7) prove (0.7).  Notice that the original
\(Q_\epsilon,Q_\eta\) may remain signed throughout.

## 4. Family count and endpoint

Fix \(\eta\in\mathcal E\).  Every other pattern is obtained by deleting
at most \(L\) ones of \(\eta\) and inserting at most \(L\) zeros, where
\(L=\lfloor\log_2C\rfloor\).  In particular its symmetric difference
from \(\eta\) has size at most \(2L\).  Therefore

\[
 |\mathcal E|
 \le\sum_{r=0}^{2L}\binom kr,
\tag{4.1}
\]

which proves (0.8).  This deliberately discards the stronger two-sided
restriction in (0.7), so it is a safe upper bound for every base weight.

At \(k=14\ell\), \(C=2^\ell\), the radius is exactly

\[
 2L=2\ell=k/7.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The standard binary entropy bound gives

\[
 \sum_{r=0}^{k/7}\binom kr
 \le2^{kH_2(1/7)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equations (0.9)--(0.12) follow.  The demanded family has
\(t^{5/9-o(1)}\) rows, while (4.3) is only
\(t^{0.4601899388\ldots}\).

For the uniform endpoints \(\epsilon=\mathbf0\) and
\(\eta=\mathbf1\), one of \(I_+,I_-\) has size \(k\).  Thus (3.6) or
(3.7) gives \(C\ge2^k=S\).  These two leaf masks are exactly \(X\) and
\(3X\); if \(F_0\) is any transverse scalar copy \(\lambda_0X\), all
three source rows satisfy the common-\(X\) interface.  The contradiction
therefore uses, rather than omits, both scalar-copy and centre--leaf
transversality.

## 5. Sharpness and exact boundary

The local Hamming cost \(2^d\) is sharp.  For a prescribed set \(D\) of
\(d\) coordinates, the two positive complements

\[
 Q_{\rm small}=\prod_{i\in D}(1+z_i),\qquad
 Q_{\rm large}=\prod_{i\in D}(1+z_i^3)
\tag{5.1}
\]

have mass \(2^d\) and satisfy

\[
 \left(\prod_{i\in D}T_i\right)Q_{\rm small}
 =Q_{\rm large}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Multiplying both by an independent transverse binary-box centre preserves
directness. More explicitly, put \(G=\prod_{i=1}^k(1+z_i)\). The two leaf
rows

\[
 G\,[F_0Q_{\rm large}]
 = [G\prod_{i\in D}T_i]\,[F_0Q_{\rm small}]
\tag{5.3}
\]

have a common positive spectrum, and the corresponding centre complement

\[
 G\left(\prod_{i\in D}T_i\right)Q_{\rm small}
 =\prod_{i\in D}(1+z_i)(1+z_i^3)
  \prod_{i\notin D}(1+z_i)
\tag{5.4}
\]

is also a direct mask. Thus neither Lemma 2.1 nor the pairwise exponent
in (0.7) can be improved without further input. When \(d=k\), this sharp
model has \(C=S\) and lies exactly on, rather than inside, the strict
block boundary.

This theorem closes the signed transverse repair of the explicit
independent-\(\Phi_6\) binary-box tensor model.  It does **not** prove
that an arbitrary exact-block source set \(X\) is a binary box, that an
arbitrary residual divisor family contains independent \(\Phi_6\)
factors, or that every aperiodic centre has a finite shadow.  The theorem
is conditional on the literal exact block and supplies no stability back
to the original near-extremal point set.  Erdős #1083 remains open.
