# Erdős #1083: heavy-factor synchronization and a reciprocal ruled chart

Date: 2026-08-02

## 0. Main result

Continue with the common-tangent transverse windmill from
`EXACT_BLOCK_TRANSVERSE_STAR_CLUSTER.md`.  Thus one centre row \(0\)
is transverse to a leaf family \(L\), all leaves use one literal
tangent \(\tau_0\), and

\[
 |L|=t^{5/9+o(1)}.
\tag{0.1}
\]

Write the exact partitions and source masks as

\[
 V=A_i\oplus\lambda_iX,\qquad
 F_i=P_{\lambda_iX},\qquad
 |X|=S,\quad |A_i|=U<S^2.
\tag{0.2}
\]

The mask-factor theorem gives \(F_j\mid P_{A_0}\) for every leaf.
The new observation is that the small quotient

\[
 C:=\frac US=t^{1/18+o(1)}
\tag{0.3}
\]

forces a power-large set of leaves to choose exactly the same
irreducible factors of augmentation magnitude at least two.

Let

\[
 H_2(p)=-p\log_2p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)
\tag{0.4}
\]

be binary entropy, and put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \beta
 &:=\frac59-\frac56H_2\left(\frac1{15}\right)\\
 &=0.2610894430\ldots>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{0.5}
\]

Then there is a subfamily \(L'\subseteq L\) with

\[
 \boxed{|L'|\ge t^{\beta+o(1)}}
\tag{0.6}
\]

and one common nonconstant Laurent polynomial \(H\) such that

\[
 F_j=H E_j,\qquad
 |H(1)|=S,\qquad |E_j(1)|=1
 \quad(j\in L').
\tag{0.7}
\]

Every irreducible factor of every \(E_j\) has augmentation magnitude
one.  Thus \(H\) is the complete common **heavy-factor skeleton** and
the residual row-to-row variability is purely augmentation-unit.

Moreover, a nonzero difference \(h\) between exponents in the Newton
polytope of \(H\) is common to all row direction spaces.  With
\(\lambda_j=2\rho z_j\), there are \(0\ne w_j\in
W:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}(X-X)\) for which

\[
 \lambda_j=\frac h{w_j},
 \qquad
 z_j=\frac h{2\rho w_j}.
\tag{0.8}
\]

The selected common-tangent distance cells therefore lie in the
explicit reciprocal parabolic chart

\[
 \boxed{
 \rho^2+\tau_0+
 \frac{h^2}{4\rho^2w_j^2}
 +\frac h{w_j}X
 \qquad(j\in L').}
\tag{0.9}
\]

This is a genuine ruled/commensurate alternative inside the literal
exact block.  It is not yet a global distance contradiction and it
does not supply the missing stability theorem back to the original
near-extremal point set.

## 1. Heavy factors and omitted-factor patterns

Work in the common Laurent UFD

\[
 R=\mathbb Z[\Gamma]
 \cong\mathbb Z[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_d^{\pm1}]
\tag{1.1}
\]

generated by all finite block data.  Factor

\[
 P_{A_0}=\varepsilon\prod_{\nu=1}^mG_\nu,
\tag{1.2}
\]

where occurrences are listed with multiplicity and \(\varepsilon\)
is a Laurent unit.  No \(G_\nu(1)\) vanishes, since
\(P_{A_0}(1)=U\ne0\).

Call an occurrence **heavy** if

\[
 |G_\nu(1)|\ge2.
\tag{1.3}
\]

If there are \(\ell\) heavy occurrences, then

\[
 2^\ell
 \le\prod_{\nu\ {\rm heavy}}|G_\nu(1)|
 =U,
\qquad
 \ell\le\lfloor\log_2U\rfloor.
\tag{1.4}
\]

For a leaf \(j\), write

\[
 P_{A_0}=F_jQ_j.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Augmentation gives \(Q_j(1)=C=U/S\), so in particular \(S\mid U\).
Every heavy occurrence of \(P_{A_0}\) is assigned either to \(F_j\)
or to \(Q_j\).  If \(R_j\) is the multiset assigned to \(Q_j\), then

\[
 2^{|R_j|}
 \le\prod_{\nu\in R_j}|G_\nu(1)|
 =|Q_j(1)|=C.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Hence

\[
 |R_j|\le r:=\lfloor\log_2C\rfloor.
\tag{1.7}
\]

After artificially labelling repeated occurrences, the number of
possible omitted-heavy-factor patterns is at most

\[
 B(\ell,r):=\sum_{a=0}^r{\ell\choose a}
 \le
 \sum_{a=0}^r{\lfloor\log_2U\rfloor\choose a}.
\tag{1.8}
\]

Pigeonholing proves an exact finite-parameter statement.

### Theorem 1 (heavy-factor synchronization)

There is \(L'\subseteq L\) with

\[
 |L'|\ge
 \frac{|L|}
 {\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{\lfloor\log_2(U/S)\rfloor}
 {\lfloor\log_2U\rfloor\choose a}}
\tag{1.9}
\]

such that every \(F_j\), \(j\in L'\), contains precisely the same
heavy-factor multiset.

Let \(H\) be the product of that multiset.  The total absolute
augmentation of the heavy factors of \(P_{A_0}\) is \(U\); that of
the omitted heavy factors is \(C\).  All other factors have
augmentation magnitude one.  Consequently

\[
 |H(1)|=\frac UC=S.
\tag{1.10}
\]

The polynomial \(P_{A_0}\) is a \(0/1\) set mask and therefore has
content one.  No integer prime is one of its irreducible factors.
Since \(|H(1)|=S\ge2\), the skeleton \(H\) is consequently
nonconstant and nonunit.  We have \(F_j=HE_j\) and
\(|E_j(1)|=1\), and by construction every irreducible factor of
\(E_j\) has augmentation magnitude one.

This is stronger than merely finding one common irreducible factor:
the whole non-unit-augmentation part of the leaf masks is synchronized.

## 2. Endpoint entropy calculation

At the endpoint,

\[
 \log_2U=\left(\frac56+o(1)\right)\log_2t,\qquad
 \log_2C=\left(\frac1{18}+o(1)\right)\log_2t.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Their ratio tends to

\[
 \frac{1/18}{5/6}=\frac1{15}<\frac12.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The standard binomial-tail bound

\[
 \sum_{a=0}^{pn}{n\choose a}
 \le 2^{nH_2(p)}
\qquad(0\le p\le1/2)
\tag{2.3}
\]

therefore gives

\[
 B(\lfloor\log_2U\rfloor,\lfloor\log_2C\rfloor)
 \le
 t^{(5/6)H_2(1/15)+o(1)}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Combining (0.1), (1.9), and (2.4) proves (0.6).  The positive margin
is

\[
 \frac59-\frac56H_2(1/15)
 =0.2610894430\ldots.
\tag{2.5}
\]

## 3. From the common skeleton to a reciprocal ruled chart

The mask \(F_j\) has Newton direction space

\[
 \operatorname{dir}(\operatorname{Newt}F_j)
 =\lambda_jW\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since \(H\mid F_j\), Newton-polytopal additivity under multiplication
implies

\[
 \operatorname{dir}(\operatorname{Newt}H)
 \subseteq
 \lambda_jW\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Because \(H\) is nonconstant (constant prime factors were excluded by
the \(0/1\)-mask content-one property), its Newton polytope has a
nonzero direction.  Choose a nonzero exponent
difference \(h\) in that rational direction space.  Saturation may
make \(h\) a rational fraction of an original support difference, but
it still belongs to the \(\mathbb Q\)-space \(\lambda_jW\).  Hence

\[
 h=\lambda_jw_j
\quad\text{for some }0\ne w_j\in W,
\tag{3.3}
\]

which proves (0.8).

For the common tangent \(\tau_0\), the \(j\)-th row cell is

\[
 \rho^2+\tau_0+z_j^2+2\rho z_jX.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Substitution of (0.8) gives exactly (0.9).  The corresponding target
points

\[
 q_{j,\tau_0}=(A,\sqrt{\tau_0},-z_j)
\tag{3.5}
\]

are collinear and their heights are parametrized by the reciprocal
slice \(h/(2\rho W)\).

No denominator bound for the \(w_j\) is asserted.  The conclusion is
a denominator-free ruled chart, not an invocation of the inherited
polynomial-coordinate escape theorem.

## 4. What exact positivity does and does not force

The quotient \(Q_j\) in (1.5) need not be a mask, even though both
exact complements \(A_0,A_j\) are masks.  For example, with two
transverse variables,

\[
 Q=x+y-xy+xy^2+x^2y
\tag{4.1}
\]

has a negative coefficient, while both

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (1+x)Q
 &=x^3y+x^2y^2+x^2+xy^2+x+y,\\
 (1+y)Q
 &=x^2y^2+x^2y+xy^3+x+y^2+y
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

are \(0/1\) masks.  Thus algebraic divisibility cannot be promoted to
a third direct-sum factor without an additional theorem.

There is nevertheless a sharp support consequence of (0.7).  Suppose
the leaf scalars \(\lambda_j\) are distinct, as they are for distinct
height rows.  If \(E_j\) were only a Laurent unit, then \(F_j\) would
be associate to \(H\).  Two such leaves would have

\[
 \lambda_jX=c+\lambda_kX
\tag{4.3}
\]

for some translation \(c\).  Taking difference sets gives

\[
 \lambda_j(X-X)=\lambda_k(X-X).
\tag{4.4}
\]

For a finite nonzero subset of \(\mathbb R\), a scalar preserving it
is \(+1\) or \(-1\): compare the maximum absolute value.  Hence one
associate class contains at most the two scalars
\(\lambda,-\lambda\).

More generally, if \(E_j\) and \(E_k\) are associates, then
\(F_j=HE_j\) and \(F_k=HE_k\) are associates.  The same support
argument gives
\(\lambda_j(X-X)=\lambda_k(X-X)\) and hence
\(\lambda_j/\lambda_k=\pm1\).  Thus **every** residual associate class
contains at most two leaves.  The Laurent units constitute one such
class, so at most two \(E_j\) are units.  After deleting them, the
number of nonunit residual associate classes is at least

\[
 \frac{|L'|-2}{2}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

In particular, among the \(t^{\beta+o(1)}\) synchronized leaves, all
but at most two \(E_j\) are nonunit augmentation-unit polynomials,
and there are

\[
 \frac12t^{\beta+o(1)}
\tag{4.6}
\]

distinct associate classes of such residuals.  Exact positivity and
the scalar-copy support condition therefore force a power-large
**augmentation-unit switching atlas** around one fixed heavy
skeleton.

This does not exclude the residual: cyclotomic refinements show that
augmentation-unit switches genuinely exist.  It identifies the exact
object that a final geometric or sparse-factor theorem must bound.

## 5. Claim firewall

The proved implication is

```text
literal exact common-spectrum block
 + transverse-heavy common-tangent windmill
   -> t^(0.261089...+o(1)) leaves share the full heavy skeleton
   -> reciprocal ruled chart
   -> power-large augmentation-unit switching atlas.
```

It does **not** show:

- that pairwise nontransverse leaf spaces have a total common
  intersection before factor synchronization;
- that \(Q_j\) is nonnegative or a set mask;
- that the reciprocal parameters have bounded denominators;
- that the ruled chart alone determines more than \(t^3\) distances;
- or that a literal exact block follows from the original
  near-extremal configuration.

The rank-two three-dimensional-top example remains a valid firewall
for the first point.  The common direction here comes from a shared
polynomial factor, not from pairwise linear intersection.
