## 1. Heavy-factor hub: PASS

Let \(R=\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\), with \(\Gamma\) the finitely generated
subgroup of \((\mathbb R,+)\) generated by the finite masks.  Then
\(\Gamma\) is free abelian and \(R\) is a Laurent-polynomial UFD.
Transversality implies \(\gcd(F_0,F_j)=1\): a common nonunit irreducible
would have a nonzero Newton direction in both transverse spaces; a
direction-zero irreducible would be a constant prime up to a monomial,
which cannot divide a primitive \(0/1\) mask.  Euclid's lemma therefore
does give

\[
F_j\mid P_{A_0}.
\]

The augmentation count is also sound.  In a factorization of the primitive
mask \(P_{A_0}\), every irreducible has nonzero integral augmentation and

\[
U=|P_{A_0}(1)|
  =\prod_H |H(1)|^{m_H}.
\]

Hence at most \(\lfloor\log_2U\rfloor\) factor occurrences have
\(|H(1)|\ge2\).  Since \(F_j(1)=S>1\), every leaf contains at least one
such occurrence type.  Pigeonholing types, rather than occurrences, only
improves the bound, so the common factor occurs on at least
\(\lceil |L|/\lfloor\log_2U\rfloor\rceil\) leaves.  Content one rules out
a constant prime and ensures that the chosen factor has a nonzero Newton
direction.

Finally, Newton-polytope additivity gives

\[
\operatorname{dir}(\operatorname{Newt}G)
 \subseteq (\lambda_jW)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R.
\]

Both sides are rational subspaces of the common exponent group.  A fixed
nonzero rational direction \(h\) of \(G\) therefore lies in
\(\lambda_jW\), so \(h=\lambda_jw_j\) with \(w_j\in W\setminus\{0\}\).
The displayed reciprocal cell follows by literal substitution.  No
denominator or height bound is silently used.

## 2. Simultaneous normal form and clean quotient: PASS

With \(F_j=GR_j\) and \(P_{A_0}=GB\), the already proved divisibility
\(F_j\mid P_{A_0}\) gives \(B=R_jQ_j\).  Cancellation in the Laurent
domain gives

\[
P_{A_j}=F_0Q_j,
\qquad
Q_j(1)=\frac{U}{S}=C\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]

No positivity of \(G,R_j,Q_j\) is inferred.  Positivity is asserted only
for the products already equal to the masks \(P_{A_0}\) and \(P_{A_j}\),
which is correct.

The associate-class bound also passes.  If \(R_j\sim R_k\), then
\(F_j\sim F_k\).  Associates of two \(0/1\) masks differ by a translation,
so their difference sets agree.  Numerical width then gives
\(|\lambda_j|=|\lambda_k|\); distinct real row scalars leave at most the
two choices \(\lambda,-\lambda\).  Thus at least \(\lceil K/2\rceil\)
divisor-associate classes occur, and the UFD divisor count yields

\[
\Omega(B)\ge\log_2\lceil K/2\rceil.
\]

For the clean-quotient lemma, a collision \(f+q=f'+q'\) lies in
\(W_0\cap W(Q_j)\).  If that intersection is zero, the support-sum map is
injective.  The coefficient of \(F_0Q_j\) at \(f+q\) is then exactly the
nonzero coefficient \(c_q\).  Since the product is a \(0/1\) mask,
\(c_q=1\) for every \(q\).  Therefore \(Q_j\) is a \(C\)-term mask and
both mask products are direct.  This argument correctly uses quotient
directions, not merely centre--leaf transversality.

### R2. The large-prime conclusion needs prime-power valuations

The proved text treats only

\[
p\mid M,\qquad p\nmid m,
\]

and then says that *all* row variation is confined to primes at most \(C\).
For unrestricted \(M\), mere containment of each large prime in \(m\) does
not freeze its exponent.  For example, with

\[
S=5,\qquad C=2,\qquad M=3^2,\qquad m=3,
\]

the selected divisor \(m\) contains the only prime \(3>C\), but it still
differs from \(M\) in that large prime's valuation.  Thus the stated
inference does not follow from the displayed case.

The conclusion is repairable.  Put \(a=v_p(m)\) and \(e=v_p(M)\).  If
\(a<e\), then \(p^{a+1}\mid M\) but \(p^{a+1}\nmid m\), and the exact
factorization (4.2) gives

\[
\boxed{\Phi_{S p^{a+1}}(y)\mid F_M/F_m.}
\]

A nonnegative multiple evaluated at a primitive root of exact order
\(Sp^{a+1}\) gives a nonempty vanishing sum of roots of that order.  Since
\(S\ne p\) are prime, the least prime divisor of \(Sp^{a+1}\) is
\(\min\{S,p\}\), and the coefficient mass is at least this value.  Hence
\(C\ge\min\{S,p\}\) again.  When \(C<S\), every \(p>C\) must satisfy

\[
\boxed{v_p(m)=v_p(M).}
\]

This is the exact replacement for “must contain every prime divisor” and
does justify that all support and valuation variation is confined to
\(p\le C\).  Alternatively the section may add the explicit hypothesis
that \(M\) is squarefree.  The verifier currently checks only the
first-power case \(\Phi_{Sp}\), so it does not cover the published
quantifier.  The smallest numerical witness to the gap in the written
inference is \(S=3,C=1,M=4,m=2\): the large prime \(2>C\) divides \(m\),
but its valuation still varies.  The repair detects the missing factor
\(\Phi_{12}\); a positive multiple has coefficient mass at least two and
therefore cannot have augmentation \(C=1\).

### R3. Formula (2.7) must be restricted to the same-sign subfamily

The width identity

\[
|\lambda_j|=(a+b_j)/D
\]

determines only absolute values.  Therefore

\[
\|q_{j,\tau_0}-q_{k,\tau_0}\|^2
=\frac{\bigl(\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_j)(a+b_j)
 -\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_k)(a+b_k)\bigr)^2}
 {4\rho^2D^2}
\]

in general.  The simpler formula

\[
\frac{(b_j-b_k)^2}{4\rho^2D^2}
\]

is valid only after \(j,k\) are restricted to the same-sign subfamily
introduced immediately before it.  The prose appears to intend that
restriction but the displayed statement should say it explicitly.  A
publication-ready replacement for (2.6)--(2.7) is: choose

\[
J_\sigma=\{j:\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_j)=\sigma\},
\qquad |J_\sigma|\ge\lceil K/2\rceil.
\]

Then, for \(j\in J_\sigma\),

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_j=\frac{\sigma(a+b_j)}D,\qquad
z_j=\frac{\sigma(a+b_j)}{2\rho D},\qquad
w_j=\frac{\sigma hD}{a+b_j},}
\tag{R3.1}
\]

and, only for \(j,k\in J_\sigma\),

\[
\boxed{
\|q_{j,\tau_0}-q_{k,\tau_0}\|^2
=\frac{(b_j-b_k)^2}{4\rho^2D^2}.}
\tag{R3.2}
\]

A minimal witness exists inside the exact-block/common-tangent interface,
not merely at the level of formal widths.  Take

\[
X=\{-1/2,1/2\},\qquad \lambda_+=1,\qquad\lambda_-=-1.
\]

Then the two scalar-copy masks coincide:
\(F_+=P_X=P_{-X}=F_-=:G\).  To include the centre's literal common
tangent, choose

\[
\rho^2=\sqrt2,\qquad \beta=1+2\sqrt2,
\]

and take the centre scalar to be \(\beta\).  Thus
\(F_0=P_{\beta X}\) is transverse to \(G\).  The untranslated masks are

\[
P_V=F_0G,\qquad P_{A_0}=G,
\qquad P_{A_+}=P_{A_-}=F_0.
\]

They are exact direct masks with \(S=U=2<S^2\).  Translate the common
spectrum and every complement by the same \(R\); then, as actual sets,

\[
\widetilde V=R+(\beta X\oplus X),\qquad
A_0=R+X,\qquad A_+=A_-=R+\beta X.
\]

With \(T_i=A_i-\rho^2-z_i^2\), all three direct row spectra are
literally \(\widetilde V\).

Both leaf residuals are
units, hence \(b_+=b_-=0\).  The centre height is
\(z_0=\beta/(2\rho)\), while the leaf heights are
\(z_\pm=\pm1/(2\rho)\).  The identity

\[
\frac12-\frac{\beta^2}{4\rho^2}
=-\frac\beta2-\frac1{4\rho^2}
\]

follows from
\(\beta^2-2\rho^2\beta-(2\rho^2+1)=0\).  It makes the tangent arising
from \(1/2\in X=A_0-R\) equal to the tangent arising from
\(-\beta/2\in\beta X=A_\pm-R\).  Taking \(R\) large makes this literal
centre--leaf common tangent and every other tangent positive.  Moreover
\(z_+^2=z_-^2\) and the leaf complements coincide, so the two entire leaf
tangent sets coincide.  Formula (2.7) without the same-sign restriction
predicts zero, whereas the actual squared distance between the two targets
on the centre's common tangent is

\[
(z_+-z_-)^2=1/\rho^2.
\]

### R4. Make the branch pigeonhole explicit

The clean/contaminated dichotomy is row-by-row.  Define

\[
J_{\rm clean}=\{j:W(Q_j)\cap W_0=\{0\}\},\qquad
J_{\rm cont}=J\setminus J_{\rm clean}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\max\{|J_{\rm clean}|,|J_{\rm cont}|\}
\ge\left\lceil\frac K2\right\rceil
=t^{5/9-o(1)}.}
\tag{R4.1}
\]

On \(J_{\rm clean}\), every \(Q_j\) is a \(C\)-term mask and both tilings
are direct.  On \(J_{\rm cont}\), every quotient has
\(W(Q_j)\cap W_0\ne\{0\}\), but it need not be signed; the rigorous
alternative is therefore “contaminated quotients,” not “signed quotients.”
The asymptotic exponent is unchanged.  Section 5 should explicitly take
the subfamily furnished by (R4.1) before stating its two alternatives.

The broader “honest or signed” sentence in Section 0 is also valid after a
different explicit pigeonhole.  Let \(J_{\rm mask}\) be the rows for which
\(Q_j\) is a mask and let \(J_{\rm signed}=J\setminus J_{\rm mask}\).  If
an integral \(Q_j\) had no negative coefficient while \(F_0Q_j\) were a
\(0/1\) mask, every coefficient of \(Q_j\) would be one and every support
sum would be unique; hence it would already lie in \(J_{\rm mask}\).
Thus every row of \(J_{\rm signed}\) genuinely has a negative coefficient,
and the clean-quotient lemma puts it in \(J_{\rm cont}\).  One of
\(J_{\rm mask},J_{\rm signed}\) again has size at least \(\lceil K/2\rceil\).

## 4. Signed-switch no-go: PASS

The identity is exact for every \(S\ge4\):

\[
Q_S=x+y-xy+xy^S+x^Sy,
\qquad Q_S(1,1)=3<S.
\]

Using

\[
(1-y+y^S)P_S(y)=1+y^{S+1}+\cdots+y^{2S-1}
\]

splits \(P_S(y)Q_S\) into three disjoint \(S\)-term masks.  Multiplication
by \(P_S(x)\) splits the total product into three disjoint \(S^2\)-term
rectangles.  Hence both complements and the common spectrum are honest
\(0/1\) masks, and both source-mask products are genuinely direct.  The
negative coefficient of \(Q_S\) is not hidden by a non-injective exponent
map.

Indeed, the real embedding uses \(\alpha/\beta=\sqrt2\), so
\((m,n)\mapsto m\alpha+n\beta\) is injective on \(\mathbb Z^2\).  The
chosen elements satisfy

\[
a_0-a_1=z_0^2-z_1^2,
\]

and an arbitrarily large common translation makes every tangent square
positive while preserving the literal common tangent.  With

\[
p_x=(A+\sqrt{1-x^2},0,x),\qquad
q_{i,\tau}=(A,\sqrt\tau,-z_i),
\]

one has exactly

\[
\|p_x-q_{i,\tau}\|^2=1+z_i^2+\tau+2z_ix.
\]

All points used in these cells are real and distinct.  The construction
correctly claims only two exact rows.  It neither supplies a power-large
leaf family nor bounds all pairwise distances of an endpoint-sized point
set, and the manuscript explicitly preserves both firewalls.

## 5. Cyclotomic vanishing-sum check

For prime \(S\nmid M\),

\[
P_S(y^m)=\prod_{d\mid m}\Phi_{Sd}(y)
\]

is correct.  In the explicit two-variable candidate, divisibility by a
monic polynomial in \(y\) holds fibrewise after collecting coefficients in
the other variable.  A nonzero nonnegative fibre divisible by
\(\Phi_n(y)\) evaluates to a nonempty vanishing sum of \(n\)-th roots.  Its
coefficient mass is at least the least prime divisor of \(n\).  Thus the
bound \(C\ge\min\{S,p\}\) is valid; only the valuation quantifier in R2 was
missing.
