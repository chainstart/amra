# Human cube-commutativity lemmas for all dimension-nine survivors

Date: 2026-07-30

## Result

The iteration-zero SMT exclusions of
\[
 (2,2,1,1,1,1,1),\qquad
 (3,1,1,1,1,1,1),\qquad
 (2,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)
\]
can be replaced by short human arguments.  In every case, all cubes in
the associative algebra commute.  No raw-cube closure assumption, finite
field enumeration, or solver result is needed.

The formerly closure-aware profile
\[
(2,2,2,1,1,1)
\]
also has a human exclusion.  Here closure is used only to make the
degree-three cube map bijective in the historical solver model.  The hand
proof is stronger: one-dimensional layers in degrees five and six
force the sixfold multiplication tensor to be symmetric, without using
closure or bijectivity.

Write
\[
 A_i=J^i/J^{i+1}.
\]
For every ordered split \(i+j=k\), multiplication
\(A_i\otimes A_j\to A_k\) spans \(A_k\), because
\(J^iJ^j=J^{i+j}\).

## Lemma 1: the seven-layer profiles

**Lemma.** Let \(J\) be an associative algebra with \(J^8=0\).  Suppose
\[
 \dim A_3=\dim A_4=\dim A_6=1.
\]
Then all cubes commute.

**Proof.** If every cube belongs to \(J^4\), then their pairwise products
belong to \(J^8=0\), so there is nothing to prove.

Otherwise choose \(a\in J\) with \(a^3\notin J^4\).  Its image spans
\(A_3\).  The product map \(A_3\otimes A_3\to A_6\) is onto, so
\[
 a^6=(a^3)^2\notin J^7.                         \tag{1}
\]
If \(a^4\) belonged to \(J^5\), then
\[
 a^6=a^4a^2\in J^5J^2=J^7,
\]
contrary to (1).  Hence the image of \(a^4\) spans \(A_4\).

Now take arbitrary cubes \(c=x^3\) and \(d=y^3\).  Since \(A_3\) is
one-dimensional, for some scalars \(\alpha,\beta\) one may write
\[
 c=\alpha a^3+r,\qquad d=\beta a^3+s,
 \qquad r,s\in J^4.
\]
Modulo \(J^5\), both \(r\) and \(s\) are scalar multiples of \(a^4\).
Consequently, modulo \(J^8\), every term in
\[
 [c,d]=\alpha[a^3,s]+\beta[r,a^3]+[r,s]
\]
is zero: \([r,s]\in J^8\), while the degree-seven terms are scalar
multiples of
\[
 [a^3,a^4]=a^7-a^7=0.
\]
Thus \([c,d]=0\). \(\square\)

This proves the two length-seven profiles
\[
 (2,2,1,1,1,1,1),\qquad (3,1,1,1,1,1,1),
\]
and also replaces the old dimension-eight SMT step for
\((2,1,1,1,1,1,1)\).

## Lemma 2: the eight-layer profile

**Lemma.** Let \(J^9=0\), and suppose every layer \(A_i\) is
one-dimensional for \(2\le i\le8\).  Then all cubes commute.

**Proof.** First suppose every cube belongs to \(J^4\).  For cubes
\(c,d\), their images in the one-dimensional space \(A_4\) are
proportional.  If both images vanish, then \(c,d\in J^5\) and their
products already lie in \(J^{10}=0\).  Otherwise, after interchanging
\(c,d\) if necessary, choose the one with nonzero image as \(c\).  Then
\(d-\lambda c\in J^5\) for a scalar \(\lambda\), and
\[
 [c,d]=[c,d-\lambda c]\in J^4J^5+J^5J^4
 \subseteq J^9=0.
\]

Otherwise choose \(a\) with \(a^3\notin J^4\).  Then
\(a^2+J^3\) and \(a^3+J^4\) generate \(A_2\) and \(A_3\),
respectively.  Inductively, if \(a^k+J^{k+1}\) generates \(A_k\), the
surjective product
\[
 A_2A_k=A_{k+2}
\]
shows that \(a^{k+2}+J^{k+3}\) generates \(A_{k+2}\).  Starting with
\(k=2,3\), all the elements
\[
 a^2,a^3,\ldots,a^8
\]
have nonzero successive leading terms.  They therefore form a
filtration-adapted basis of \(J^2\).  Since they are powers of one
element, they commute pairwise.  Hence \(J^2\), and in particular the
set of cubes, is commutative. \(\square\)

This proves the last profile
\[
 (2,1,1,1,1,1,1,1).
\]

## Lemma 3: one-dimensional consecutive tail layers

**Lemma.** Let \(J\) be an associative algebra with \(J^7=0\), and put
\(A=\operatorname{gr}J\).  If
\[
\dim A_5=\dim A_6=1,
\]
then all cubes in \(J\) commute.

We use the following elementary tensor fact.  If
\[
0\ne T\in(V^*)^{\otimes m},\qquad 0\ne\ell,r\in V^*,
\qquad T\otimes r=\ell\otimes T,                 \tag{2}
\]
then
\[
T=c\,\ell^{\otimes m},\qquad r\in\langle\ell\rangle.
\]
Indeed, view (2) by its first tensor factor.  The first-mode span of its
right side is \(\langle\ell\rangle\); because \(r\ne0\), the first-mode
span of its left side is the first-mode span of \(T\).  Hence
\(T=\ell\otimes T_{m-1}\).  Substitute this into (2) and cancel the
nonzero first factor:
\[
T_{m-1}\otimes r=\ell\otimes T_{m-1}.
\]
Iteration reduces to the one-factor identity
\(t\otimes r=\ell\otimes t\), which makes \(t,r,\ell\) proportional.
Back-substitution proves the claim.

**Proof of the lemma.** The graded algebra \(A\) is generated by
\(V=A_1\).  Choose bases of \(A_5,A_6\), and let
\[
f_k:V^{\otimes k}\longrightarrow\mathbb F
\qquad (k=5,6)
\]
be the scalar \(k\)-linear forms obtained by multiplying \(k\)
degree-one elements.  These forms are nonzero because the corresponding
power layers are nonzero and are spanned by degree-one words.
Moreover \(A_iA_j=A_{i+j}\) for every ordered split, so each left or
right multiplication map used below is onto and hence nonzero.

Right and left multiplication through the one-dimensional layer \(A_5\)
are represented by nonzero \(s,t\in V^*\).  Associativity gives
\[
f_6=f_5\otimes s=t\otimes f_5
\]
as an identity of six-tensors.  The tensor fact makes
\(f_5\) a pure fifth power of \(t\) and makes \(s\) proportional to
\(t\).  Therefore
\[
f_5=c\,t^{\otimes5},\qquad f_6=d\,t^{\otimes6}. \tag{3}
\]
Thus the sixfold multiplication form is symmetric.  In particular, for
all \(x,y\in A_1\),
\[
x^3y^3
=f_6(x,x,x,y,y,y)
=f_6(y,y,y,x,x,x)
=y^3x^3
\quad\text{in }A_6.                             \tag{4}
\]

Finally take arbitrary lifts \(a,b\in J\), with degree-one parts
\(x=a+J^2\) and \(y=b+J^2\).  The image of
\(a^3b^3-b^3a^3\in J^6\) in \(A_6=J^6/J^7\) is exactly the difference
in (4), so it vanishes.  Since \(J^7=0\), the original commutator is
zero.  This covers arbitrary elements, including roots with higher
filtration components. \(\square\)

The profile \((2,2,2,1,1,1)\) satisfies the lemma.  Thus it is excluded
without raw-cube closure, finite-field arguments, or the formerly encoded
circle-product root equation.

## Consequences for the trust boundary

The first two lemmas exclude every algebra of dimension at most eight, so
the unconditional algebra-group search boundary first improves from
\[
\dim J\ge8
\quad\text{to}\quad
\dim J\ge9.
\]

In dimension nine, the elementary profile reductions and the three lemmas
now exclude all 29 profiles by hand.  Therefore the stronger boundary
\[
\dim J\ge10
\]
is also human-checkable.  The four archived SMT certificates are
independent regression checks and no longer premises of the
algebra-dimension theorem.

## Localization audit for the old solver result

Before the hand proof was identified, named assertions reduced the
\((3,1,1,1,1,1,1)\) model from 500 associativity coordinates and 21
ordered surjections to a deletion-minimal subset of one solver core:

- 14 associativity coordinates;
- 3 layer-surjection assertions;
- 1 noncommuting-cube witness.

The three layer maps are
\[
 A_1A_3\to A_4,\qquad A_2A_4\to A_6,\qquad A_3A_3\to A_6.
\]
The associativity coordinates are concentrated in degrees
\[
 (1,1,1)\to3,\quad (1,1,2)\to4,\quad
 (1,2,1)\to4,\quad (2,1,3)\to6,\quad
 (3,1,3)\to7.
\]
This concentration is what led to Lemma 1.  The exact deletion and
ablation audit is reproducible with:

```bash
python3 humanize_dim9_iteration_zero.py
```

The full model is UNSAT; removing all associativity or the witness makes
it SAT.  The full associativity system remains UNSAT without explicit
surjectivity assertions, but the much smaller archived core uses the
three maps displayed above.  The hand proof uses only the general
power-filtration surjectivity \(A_iA_j=A_{i+j}\), not this computational
ablation.

For \((2,2,2,1,1,1)\), grouped deletion gives an even more important
localization: no circle-product root equation, cube-map bijectivity
constraint, filtered higher component, or explicit layer-surjectivity
assertion is needed.  Leading associated-graded associativity and the
noncommuting-basis-cubes assertion are already inconsistent.  Lemma 3 is
the coordinate-free translation of precisely this reduced dependency.
