# An all-quotients no-go theorem for exponent-\(p\) wreath seeds

Date: 2026-07-29

## Theorem

Let \(p\) be an odd prime, let \(A\) be a nonabelian exponent-\(p\)
semi-extraspecial group, and put
\[
 U=A'=Z(A),\qquad W=A\wr C_p.
\]
Write
\[
 S=P_p(W),\qquad H=\langle S\rangle.
\]
For every normal subgroup \(N\trianglelefteq W\), if
\[
 P_p(W/N)
\]
is a subgroup, then
\[
 \Delta U\leq N.                                   \tag{1}
\]
Consequently the \(p\)-power-value subgroup in \(W/N\) is abelian.

In particular, no quotient of this natural exponent-\(p\) wreath
construction can solve the odd-prime KOU problem: every quotient that
repairs closure also kills the entire commutator subgroup of the generated
power values.

The restriction to odd \(p\) is natural: every exponent-two group is
abelian, so the stated nonabelian seed class is empty at \(p=2\).

## Exact value-set geometry

Put
\[
 K=U^p\leq A^p,
\]
where the exponent denotes a Cartesian direct power.  Since \(A\) has
exponent \(p\), its raw \(p\)-power set is \(\{1\}\).  The general wreath
formula gives
\[
 S=\{1\}\cup\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal C(A)}C^p.        \tag{2}
\]

The central conjugacy classes are the singletons \(\{u\}\), \(u\in U\).
Every noncentral conjugacy class is a full coset \(aU\), because \(A\) is
semi-extraspecial and hence Camina.  Also
\[
 H=\Delta A\,K.                                    \tag{3}
\]
The elements of \(H\setminus K\) are exactly the tuples whose coordinates
lie independently in one fixed noncentral coset \(aU\).  Therefore (2)
becomes the exact disjoint description
\[
 \boxed{S=(H\setminus K)\cup\Delta U.}             \tag{4}
\]

Since \(K\) is central in the base and \((\Delta A)'=\Delta U\),
\[
 H'=\Delta U.                                      \tag{5}
\]

## Passing closure to \(H/(N\cap H)\)

Fix \(N\trianglelefteq W\) and put
\[
 L=N\cap H.
\]
Word values commute with epimorphisms, so
\[
 P_p(W/N)=SN/N.
\]
The image of \(S\) generates \(HN/N\cong H/L\).  Hence, if the image of
\(S\) is a subgroup, it must equal all of \(H/L\):
\[
 SL/L=H/L.                                         \tag{6}
\]
We split according to whether \(L\) lies in \(K\).

## Case 1: \(L\) contains an element outside \(K\)

Take \(h\in L\setminus K\).  By (3), write
\[
 h=\Delta a\,k,\qquad a\notin U,\quad k\in K.
\]
For every \(g\in A\), centrality of \(K\) inside the base gives
\[
 [h,\Delta g]=\Delta[a,g].                         \tag{7}
\]
The semi-extraspecial commutator map
\[
 A\longrightarrow U,\qquad g\longmapsto[a,g]
\]
is onto for every \(a\notin U\).  Thus the set on the right side of (7),
as \(g\) varies, is all of \(\Delta U\).

Because \(h\in L\leq N\) and \(N\trianglelefteq W\), every commutator in
(7) belongs to \(N\).  Therefore
\[
 \Delta U\leq N,
\]
proving (1) in the first case.  Notice that this argument requires only one
element of \(L\setminus K\).

## Case 2: \(L\leq K\)

Now (4) and \(L\leq K\) imply
\[
 (H\setminus K)L/L=(H/L)\setminus(K/L).
\]
The only values in (4) that can cover elements of \(K/L\) are the diagonal
ones.  Therefore the closure equality (6) forces
\[
 K/L=\Delta U\,L/L,
\]
or equivalently
\[
 K=L+\Delta U                                      \tag{8}
\]
in additive notation.

The subgroup \(L=N\cap H\) is invariant under the top \(C_p\)-action.
Regard
\[
 K=U^p\cong U\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}\mathbb F_p[C_p]
\]
as a regular permutation module, and write \(t\) for a generator of
\(C_p\).  In
\[
 \mathbb F_p[C_p]\cong\mathbb F_p[z]/(z^p),
 \qquad z=t-1,
\]
the diagonal fixed-point space is
\[
 \Delta U=z^{p-1}K,                                \tag{9}
\]
because
\[
 1+t+\cdots+t^{p-1}=(t-1)^{p-1}
\]
in characteristic \(p\).

Equation (8) says that \(K/L\) is generated by the image of the fixed-point
space \(\Delta U\).  Hence \(t\) acts trivially on \(K/L\), and
\[
 (t-1)K=zK\leq L.                                  \tag{10}
\]
Using (9),
\[
 \Delta U=z^{p-1}K\leq zK\leq L.
\]
Then (8) yields \(K=L\), and in particular
\[
 \Delta U\leq L\leq N.
\]
This proves (1) in the second case.

## Abelianity after every successful repair

If \(P_p(W/N)\) is a subgroup, it is the image \(HN/N\) of \(H\).  By (5),
its derived subgroup is
\[
 (HN/N)'=H'N/N=\Delta U\,N/N.
\]
The theorem gives \(\Delta U\leq N\), so this derived subgroup is trivial.
Therefore every quotient with closed \(p\)-power values has an abelian
power-value subgroup.

## Proof-risk audit

The proof uses four points at which a set/subgroup confusion would be
fatal; each is explicit above.

1. Formula (4) is an equality of raw sets, not merely generated subgroups.
   It follows from the exact conjugacy-class power formula.
2. Closure in the quotient gives (6) because the image of \(S\) already
   generates the image of \(H\).
3. In Case 1, the commutator set covers all of \(\Delta U\), not merely a
   subgroup that generates it; this is exactly the semi-extraspecial
   surjectivity condition.
4. In Case 2, no semisimplicity is assumed.  The module is the unipotent
   regular \(\mathbb F_p[C_p]\)-module.  The implication
   \(K=L+\Delta U\Rightarrow (t-1)K\leq L\) comes from trivial action on the
   quotient, after which \(\Delta U=(t-1)^{p-1}K\leq L\).

Thus the argument does not rely on an invalid complement to the fixed-point
space.

## Exact \(p=3\) all-quotients regression

The script `validate_exponent_p_all_quotients_p3.g` constructs
\[
 A=\operatorname{UT}_3(\mathbb F_3),\qquad
 W=A\wr C_3
\]
as a pc group and enumerates all \(101\) normal subgroups of \(W\).  For
each \(N\), it maps all \(219\) raw cubes into \(W/N\) and compares their
number with
\[
 |H/(H\cap N)|,
\]
the order of the subgroup they generate.  The exact certificate is

```text
EXP_P_ALL_QUOTIENTS_P3|wreath_order=59049|normal_subgroups=101|cube_values=219|cube_group_order=243|K_order=27|DeltaU_order=3|delta_killed=100|closed=98|closed_L_le_K=1|closed_L_outside_K=97|closed_nonabelian=0
DONE
```

Thus all \(98\) closed quotient images kill \(\Delta U\), including one
quotient in the \(L\leq K\) proof branch and \(97\) in the
\(L\not\leq K\) branch.  The calculation also prevents reading the theorem
as a converse: \(100\) normal subgroups kill \(\Delta U\), but only \(98\)
give closure.

## Publication boundary

This theorem is a strong no-go result for one broad construction and all of
its quotients.  It does not settle the odd-prime KOU problem for arbitrary
exponent-\(p^2\) groups.  A priority search for the same regular-module
argument in the power-value and wreath-product literature is required
before any novelty claim, and no journal-quartile outcome is guaranteed.
