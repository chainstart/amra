# The sharp \(2p\) power-commutator barrier in the metabelian case

Date: 2026-07-30

## Theorem

Let \(p\) be an odd prime and let \(G\) be a metabelian group of exponent
dividing \(p^2\).  If
\[
  \operatorname{cl}(G)<2p,
\]
then
\[
  [x^p,y^p]=1\qquad(x,y\in G).                    \tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, the verbal subgroup \(G^p=\langle g^p:g\in G\rangle\) is
abelian.  In particular, the raw \(p\)-power value set cannot be a
nonabelian subgroup.

The class bound is sharp for pairwise commutativity.  A two-string subgroup
of \(\operatorname{UT}_{2p+1}(\mathbb F_p)\) is metabelian, has class
\(2p\), and contains the two explicit noncommuting \(p\)-th powers described
below.

This is a theorem for the metabelian case.  The corresponding statement for
arbitrary exponent-\(p^2\) groups of class \(2p-1\) is not proved here.

## Module setup

Put \(A=G'\), written additively.  Since \(G\) is metabelian, \(A\) is a
right module over
\[
  R=\mathbb Z[G/A].
\]
Let \(\Delta\) be the augmentation ideal of \(R\).  If \(\bar g\) is the
image of \(g\) in \(G/A\), the operator \(\bar g-1\) on \(A\) is a
commutator with \(g\), up to the harmless choice of commutator convention.
Consequently
\[
  A\Delta^r\leq \gamma_{r+2}(G).                 \tag{2}
\]
The class hypothesis therefore gives
\[
  A\Delta^{2p-2}=0.                              \tag{3}
\]
The exponent hypothesis also gives \(p^2A=0\).

## The exponent relation that kills the dangerous term

For \(a\in A\) and \(g\in G\), metabelian collection gives
\[
  (ag)^{p^2}
   =g^{p^2}+a(1+\bar g+\cdots+\bar g^{p^2-1})
\]
in additive module notation.  Both the left side and \(g^{p^2}\) are
trivial, so
\[
  a\,\sigma_{p^2}(\bar g)=0,\qquad
  \sigma_n(T)=1+T+\cdots+T^{n-1}.                \tag{4}
\]
Write \(X=\bar g-1\).  The binomial expansion is
\[
 \sigma_{p^2}(1+X)
   =\sum_{k=0}^{p^2-1}\binom{p^2}{k+1}X^k.       \tag{5}
\]
Modulo \(A\Delta^{2p-2}\), only \(0\leq k\leq2p-3\) matter.  In that range,
\[
 v_p\!\left(\binom{p^2}{k+1}\right)
 =\begin{cases}
    1,&k=p-1,\\
    \geq2,&k\ne p-1.
  \end{cases}                                    \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, \(1\leq k+1\leq2p-2\), and \(p\) is the only multiple of \(p\) in
that interval.  All terms in (5) except \(k=p-1\) therefore vanish on \(A\)
because \(p^2A=0\).  Since
\(\binom{p^2}{p}=pu\) with \(p\nmid u\), (4) implies
\[
  p\,aX^{p-1}=0\qquad(a\in A).                   \tag{7}
\]
The integer \(u\) can be cancelled because it is a unit modulo \(p\) and
\(p^2A=0\).

Relation (7) remains valid after replacing \(a\) by \(aD\), for any
\(D\in R\).  In particular,
\[
 p\,aD X^{p-1}=0.                                \tag{8}
\]
No claim that \(pA\Delta^{p-1}=0\) for arbitrary mixed augmentation
products is needed.  The repeated one-variable form (8), applied after all
other commuting module operators, is exactly what is used below.

## Proof of the theorem

Fix \(x,y\in G\), put \(a=[x,y]\in A\), and set
\[
 X=\bar x-1,\qquad Y=\bar y-1.
\]
The ring \(R\) is commutative because \(G/A\) is abelian.  Standard
metabelian commutator collection says that, up to multiplication by an
invertible monomial in \(\bar x,\bar y\), the element
\([x^p,y^p]\) is represented in \(A\) by
\[
  a\,\sigma_p(\bar x)\sigma_p(\bar y).           \tag{9}
\]
Now
\[
 \sigma_p(1+X)
   =\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\binom p{i+1}X^i,            \tag{10}
\]
and analogously for \(Y\).  For \(i\leq p-2\), the coefficient in (10) is
divisible by \(p\), while the coefficient of \(X^{p-1}\) is one.

Expand the product in (9).

1. If \(i,j\leq p-2\), its coefficient is divisible by \(p^2\), so the term
   is zero on \(A\).
2. If \(i=p-1\) and \(j\leq p-2\), its coefficient is divisible by \(p\),
   and the term vanishes by (7), applied to \(aY^j\).  The case
   \(j=p-1,\ i\leq p-2\) is symmetric.
3. The sole remaining term is \(aX^{p-1}Y^{p-1}\), which lies in
   \(A\Delta^{2p-2}=0\) by (3).

Every term in (9) is zero, proving (1).

## Exact matrix boundary

For \(n=2p\), every element of
\(\operatorname{UT}_{n}(\mathbb F_p)\) has the form \(I+N\), and
\[
  (I+N)^p=I+N^p.
\]
For strictly upper-triangular \(A,B\) of size \(2p\), both \(A^pB^p\) and
\(B^pA^p\) vanish: a product lies at least \(2p\) superdiagonals above the
main diagonal.  Hence all \(p\)-th powers commute in
\(\operatorname{UT}_{2p}(\mathbb F_p)\), whose class is \(2p-1\).

In size \(2p+1\), let
\[
 A=\sum_{i=1}^{p}E_{i,i+1},\qquad
 B=\sum_{i=p+1}^{2p}E_{i,i+1}.
\]
Then
\[
 (I+A)^p=I+E_{1,p+1},\qquad
 (I+B)^p=I+E_{p+1,2p+1},
\]
and the two products differ by \(E_{1,2p+1}\).  Thus class \(2p\) admits
noncommuting \(p\)-th powers.

More precisely, let \(H=\langle I+A,I+B\rangle\).  The associative algebra
generated by \(A,B\) has a mixed ideal spanned by the \(A^iB^j\) with
\(i,j\geq1\).  Since \(BA=0\), the product of any two mixed monomials is
zero.  The derived subgroup of \(H\) lies in one plus this square-zero
ideal, so \(H\) is metabelian.  Repeated commutation produces the nonzero
top monomial \(A^pB^p=E_{1,2p+1}\), so \(H\) has class \(2p\).  It has
exponent dividing \(p^2\) as a subgroup of the unitriangular group.  Hence
the metabelian theorem's strict class bound is genuinely sharp.

`validate_sharp_2p_matrix_boundary.g` checks both sides explicitly for
\(p=3\) and \(p=5\).  This matrix calculation is a regression, not the
proof of the metabelian theorem.

## Relation to the unrestricted problem

The module proof uses \(G''=1\) in two indispensable places:

- it makes \(G'\) a module over the commutative group ring
  \(\mathbb Z[G/G']\);
- it turns \([x^p,y^p]\) into the single bilinear norm product (9).

For a general group, the same argument in \(G/G''\) proves only
\[
  [G^p,G^p]\leq G''.                             \tag{11}
\]
It does not kill the surviving element inside \(G''\).  Thus (11) is a
genuine reduction, but not a proof of the unrestricted class-\(2p-1\)
claim.

The remaining universal target can be stated cleanly as the word inclusion
\[
 [x^p,y^p]\in
 \langle z^{p^2}:z\in F\rangle\,\gamma_{2p}(F)
                                                               \tag{12}
\]
in the free group \(F=\langle x,y\rangle\).  Establishing (12), or finding
a quotient in which it fails, is the precise next step.

## Literature and novelty status

The binomial collection mechanism goes back to P. Hall and T. E.
Easterfield:

- P. Hall, [“A contribution to the theory of groups of prime-power
  order”](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s2-36.1.29), *Proc. London Math.
  Soc.* (2) **36** (1934), 29--95;
- T. E. Easterfield, [“The orders of products and commutators in
  prime-power groups”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100016960),
  *Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc.* **36** (1940), 14--26;
- R. R. Struik, [“On nilpotent products of cyclic
  groups”](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1960-039-x), *Canad. J. Math.*
  **12** (1960), 447--462.

Khukhro constructed, under an explicit relation-ideal hypothesis, exponent
\(p^2\), class-\(2p-1\) groups with exceptional Hughes subgroups:
E. I. Khukhro, [“On a connection between Hughes' conjecture and relations
in finite groups of prime
exponent”](https://doi.org/10.1070/SM1983v044n02ABEH000963),
*Math. USSR-Sb.* **44** (1983), 227--237.  That result concerns the subgroup
generated by elements whose order is not \(p\); it does not state that the
set or subgroup of \(p\)-th powers is nonabelian.

The exact metabelian statement above was not located verbatim in the
sources checked on 2026-07-30.  The closest and most important priority
source is S. Bachmuth and H. Y. Mochizuki,
[“The class of the free metabelian group with exponent
\(p^2\)”](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160210407), *Comm. Pure Appl.
Math.* **21** (1968), 385--399.  Modern context is given by J. A. Barmak,
[“Invariants for metabelian groups of prime power exponent, colorings, and
stairs”](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008414X21000675), *Canad. J. Math.*
**75** (2023), 267--297.  Both use the same metabelian group-ring and
cyclotomic-ideal environment.

Failure to find the exact sentence is not a priority or novelty proof.  The
module theorem should be treated as a likely concise corollary of established
metabelian Burnside machinery until the 1968 paper and its citation chain
are audited line by line.
