# Algebra-group cube barriers at \(p=3\)

Date: 2026-07-30

## 1. Translation of the Wilson condition

Let \(J\) be a finite-dimensional nilpotent associative algebra over
\(\mathbb F_3\), and let \(G=1+J\).  In characteristic three,
\[
 (1+a)^3=1+a^3.                                  \tag{1}
\]
If \(J^9=0\), then \(\exp(G)\mid9\).  Therefore, if the raw cube set
\[
 H=P_3(G)=\{1+a^3:a\in J\}
\]
is a subgroup, then \(H^3=1\).  In this exponent range Wilson's genuine
non-powerfulness condition
\[
 H'\nleq H^3
\]
is equivalent to \(H\) being nonabelian.

Raw closure has the exact algebraic form
\[
 \forall a,b\in J\ \exists c\in J:\quad
 c^3=a^3+b^3+a^3b^3.                             \tag{2}
\]

## 2. Human dimension-nine lower bound

> **Proposition.** If \(J^9=0\), \(\dim_{\mathbb F_3}J\leq7\), and
> \(P_3(1+J)\) is a subgroup, then it is powerful.  Thus an algebra-group
> counterexample in the exponent-nine range must have
> \(\dim J\geq8\).

Suppose instead that the closed cube set is non-powerful.  By the preceding
translation it contains two noncommuting cubes \(1+a^3,1+b^3\).  Two units
\(1+u,1+v\) commute exactly when \(u,v\) commute, so
\[
 [J^3,J^3]\ne0,\qquad J^6\ne0.                   \tag{3}
\]
Also \(\dim J/J^2\geq2\).  Indeed, if \(J/J^2\) were one-dimensional,
successive approximation through the nilpotent filtration would express
every element as a polynomial without constant term in one lift; \(J\)
would be commutative.

Every nonzero term in the power filtration is strictly larger than its
successor.  Equation (3) therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dim J
 &=\dim J/J^2+\sum_{i=2}^{5}\dim J^i/J^{i+1}
   +\dim J^6\\
 &\geq2+4+1=7.                                   \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]
If equality held, then \(J^7=0\) and
\(\dim J^3/J^4=1\).  For \(u,v\in J^3\), write
\(v=\lambda u+w\) with \(w\in J^4\) when \(u\notin J^4\).  Then
\[
 [u,v]=[u,w]\in J^7=0,
\]
while if \(u\in J^4\) the same conclusion follows directly from
\([J^4,J^3]\leq J^7\).  In either case \(u\) and \(v\) commute, contrary
to (3).  Hence equality in (4) is impossible and
\(\dim J\geq8\).

This is a filtration-dimension obstruction, not a claimed lower bound on
the nilpotency class of the algebra group.

## 3. Exact dimension-eight profile reduction

The preceding human proof leaves dimension eight.  Put
\[
 d_i=\dim J^i/J^{i+1}.
\]
Under (3), \(d_1\ge2\) and \(d_i\ge1\) for \(2\le i\le6\).
There are exactly seven dimension-eight profiles:
\[
\begin{gathered}
(3,1,1,1,1,1),\ (2,2,1,1,1,1),\
(2,1,2,1,1,1),\\
(2,1,1,2,1,1),\ (2,1,1,1,2,1),\
(2,1,1,1,1,2),\\
(2,1,1,1,1,1,1).
\end{gathered}                                      \tag{5}
\]
In the five length-six profiles with \(d_3=1\), the dimension-seven
argument still gives \([J^3,J^3]\le J^7=0\).

The remaining length-six profile \((2,1,2,1,1,1)\) is impossible.  More
generally, in an associative graded algebra generated by \(V=A_1\), if
\(\dim A_2=1\), then \(\dim A_3\le1\).  To see this, choose a basis \(w\)
of \(A_2\), write
\[
 xy=\beta(x,y)w,\qquad xw=L(x),\qquad wx=R(x),
\]
and use associativity:
\[
 \beta(x,y)R(z)=\beta(y,z)L(x).                   \tag{6}
\]
Choose \(y\) outside both radicals of the nonzero bilinear form \(\beta\).
Equation (6) puts both \(L(V)\) and \(R(V)\) in one common line.  Since
\(A_3=L(V)+R(V)\), it cannot be two-dimensional.

Only the length-seven profile
\[
 (2,1,1,1,1,1,1)                                 \tag{7}
\]
remains.  Here a nonzero commutator in \(J^3\) would force the
one-dimensional degree-three and degree-four layers of
\(\operatorname{gr}J\) to fail to commute in degree seven.  This last case
has a small exact finite constraint system:

- two basis vectors in degree one and one in each degree \(2,\ldots,7\);
- 34 potentially nonzero \(\mathbb F_3\) structure constants;
- all 96 homogeneous associativity equations;
- all 21 ordered-split surjectivity constraints
  \(A_iA_j=A_{i+j}\).  These encode
  \(J^{i+j}=J^iJ^j\), rather than merely asking that each higher layer be
  generated in one unspecified way.

`search_dim8_algebra_profiles.py` emits this complete system in SMT-LIB.
The base profile is satisfiable, while adding
\[
 A_3A_4\ne A_4A_3
\]
is unsatisfiable.  This computation is now independently superseded by a
hand lemma.  If every cube lies in \(J^4\), then \(J^4J^4=0\).  Otherwise,
choose \(a^3\notin J^4\).  Since \(A_3A_3=A_6\), one has
\(a^6\notin J^7\), which forces \(a^4\notin J^5\).  Hence \(a^3,a^4\)
generate the one-dimensional layers \(A_3,A_4\), and the only possible
degree-seven cube commutator is a multiple of
\([a^3,a^4]=0\).  Thus the unconditional strengthening is
\[
 \dim J\ge9                                      \tag{8}
\]
for an exponent-nine algebra-group counterexample.  The finite Z3 audit is
retained only as an independent regression certificate.

The dimension-nine filtration has 29 profiles.  A further closure-aware
QF_BV audit removes the entire \(J^7=0\) branch.  Twenty of its 21 profiles
fall to the preceding degree and \(d_2=1\) arguments.  The last profile
\((2,2,2,1,1,1)\) is excluded by a necessary-condition system containing
the complete filtered associativity table, bijectivity of the projected
cube map, noncommuting basis cubes, and a cube root for their circle
product.  A later hand proof supersedes this system: one-dimensional
\(A_4,A_5,A_6\) and associativity force the fourfold, fivefold, and
sixfold multiplication tensors on \(A_1\) to be pure powers of one
linear form.  The sixfold form is therefore symmetric, giving
\(x^3y^3=y^3x^3\) in \(A_6\) and killing the filtered commutator in
\(J^7=0\).  This stronger argument needs no closure assumption.
The eight deeper profiles are then exhausted by the rank inequality
\(d_{i+j}\le d_i d_j\) and two hand cube-commutativity lemmas.  For the
two valid length-seven profiles, the preceding \(J^8=0\) lemma applies.
For the length-eight profile, either all cubes lie in \(J^4\), or one
element's powers form a filtration basis of \(J^2\), making it
commutative.  The old complete filtered models remain reproducible but
are no longer proof premises.
Thus the dimension-nine audit gives the intermediate bound
\[
 \dim J\ge10.                                     \tag{9}
\]
The full reduction and canonical solver hashes are in
`DIM9_ALGEBRA_PROFILE_STATUS.md`; the hand replacement is in
`DIM9_HUMAN_CUBE_COMMUTATIVITY.md`.  The solver files are independent
regression checks only.
Section 7 below applies the dimension-ten cyclic-tail lemmas and
strengthens (9) to \(\dim J\ge11\).

## 4. The 15-dimensional two-string algebra

Consider the algebra
\[
 J=\langle A,A^2,A^3,B,B^2,B^3,A^iB^j:
 1\leq i,j\leq3\rangle_{\mathbb F_3}
\]
with
\[
 A^4=B^4=BA=0
\]
and the evident truncated monomial multiplication.  It has dimension 15,
\(J^7=0\), and
\[
 J^6=\langle M_{33}\rangle,\qquad M_{ij}=A^iB^j.
\]
The cubes \(1+A^3\) and \(1+B^3\) do not commute because
\[
 A^3B^3=M_{33}\ne0,\qquad B^3A^3=0.
\]
Their product would require a solution of
\[
 c^3=A^3+B^3+M_{33}.                             \tag{10}
\]
No such solution exists.  If \(\alpha,\beta\) are the coefficients of
\(A,B\) in \(c\), then the pure coefficients of \(c^3\) are
\(\alpha^3A^3\) and \(\beta^3B^3\), while its degree-three mixed part is
\[
 \alpha\beta^2M_{12}+\alpha^2\beta M_{21}.        \tag{11}
\]
Equation (10) forces \(\alpha=\beta=1\), but then (11) is nonzero.

## 5. Every algebra quotient of the two-string model is excluded

The preceding obstruction survives every quotient that preserves
noncommuting cubes.

> **Theorem.** For every two-sided ideal \(I\trianglelefteq J\), the raw
> cube set of \(1+J/I\) is either not closed or, if closed, generates an
> abelian and hence powerful subgroup.  No algebra quotient of the
> two-string model satisfies Wilson's condition.

If \(M_{33}\in I\), then cubes commute modulo \(I\), because all cube
commutators lie in
\[
 [J^3,J^3]\leq J^6=\langle M_{33}\rangle.
\]

Now suppose \(M_{33}\notin I\).  If the product of \(1+A^3+I\) and
\(1+B^3+I\) were a cube, there would be \(c\in J\) such that
\[
 d=c^3-(A^3+B^3+M_{33})\in I.                   \tag{12}
\]
Right multiplication of (12) by \(B^3\) gives
\[
 (\alpha-1)M_{33}\in I,
\]
and left multiplication by \(A^3\) gives
\[
 (\beta-1)M_{33}\in I.
\]
Thus \(\alpha=\beta=1\).  Finally multiply (7) on the left by \(A^2\)
and on the right by \(B\).  All terms of degree at least four vanish
because \(J^7=0\), while (11) gives
\[
 A^2(M_{12}+M_{21})B=M_{33}.
\]
This puts \(M_{33}\) in \(I\), a contradiction.  Hence the two displayed
cubes retain their noncommutation but their product is not a cube.

## 7. Search consequences

The algebra route now has two rigorous pruning rules:

1. ignore exponent-nine algebras of dimension at most seven by the human
   filtration proof, and dimension eight by the exact graded constraint
   audit;
2. ignore every ideal quotient of the 15-dimensional two-string algebra.

Dimension ten is now closed by human arguments.  There are 92 in-scope
filtration profiles: length nine is excluded by the standing condition
\(J^9=0\).  The layer-rank inequality, the propagation rule
\(d_i=1\Rightarrow d_{i+1}\le1\), the consecutive-tail tensor lemma,
and the two cyclic-tail lemmas remove every profile.  In the last
length-seven case the filtered correction is retained explicitly:
\((tu)t=t(ut)\) forces the only coefficient relevant to
\([u,t^3]\) to agree on the two sides.  Exact counts and the historical
240-second checkpoint are in `DIM10_ALGEBRA_PROFILE_STATUS.md`.

Thus, under the standing \(J^9=0\) hypothesis, every algebra with two
noncommuting raw cubes has \(\dim J\ge11\).  This commutativity bound is
sharp:
`DIM11_SHARP_NONCOMMUTING_CUBE_WITNESS.md` gives an associative
dimension-eleven algebra of profile \((2,2,2,2,2,1)\) with a nonzero
cube commutator.  Its 171-element raw cube set is not closed, so the
next Wilson search must use closure essentially.

The closure-aware dimension-eleven pass now does so.  Of 246 profiles,
the quadratic-relation and tensor lemmas leave two, and the fibre-kernel
closure lemma excludes the sharp length-six profile.  The profile-level
remainder is
\[
(2,2,2,2,1,1,1).
\]
Its one-dimensional leading branch is excluded by the pure
degree-seven tensor, which gives \([J^3,J^4]=0\).  In the
two-dimensional branch, closure gives a cube bijection
\(A_2\to J^6\); the quadratic short-chain lemma produces
\(0\ne[b^2]\in A_2\) with \(b^6=0\), contradicting that bijection.
Thus, in the \(J^9=0\) algebra-group model, closed noncommuting raw
cubes require \(\dim_{\mathbb F_3}J\ge12\).  The complete argument and
independent finite certificates are in
`DIM11_CLOSURE_AWARE_STATUS.md`.
