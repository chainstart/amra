# Structural position of the closed power-value subgroup

Date: 2026-07-29

## Theorem

Let \(p\) be prime and let \(A\) be a group satisfying the proper positive
case of the prime-wreath criterion:
\[
  P_p(A)=A'<A,\qquad x^A=xA'\quad(x\notin A').
                                                        \tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
  B=A/A',\qquad W=A\wr C_p=A^p\rtimes\langle t\rangle,
  \qquad H=P_p(W).
\]
Then \(B\) is a nontrivial elementary abelian \(p\)-group.  Under the
coordinate quotient
\[
  \pi:A^p\longrightarrow B^p,
\]
one has
\[
  H=\pi^{-1}(\Delta B),                              \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
  W'=\pi^{-1}\!\left(
    \ker\left(\Sigma:B^p\to B\right)\right),\qquad
  \Sigma(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1})=\sum_i b_i.             \tag{3}
\]
Consequently
\[
  H\leq W',\qquad [W':H]=|B|^{p-2}.                 \tag{4}
\]

If \(A\) is a finite \(p\)-group, then
\[
  \Phi(W)=W',\qquad [\Phi(W):H]=|B|^{p-2}.           \tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
  p=2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=W'=\Phi(W),         \tag{6}
\]
whereas for odd \(p\), \(H<W'=\Phi(W)\).

There is also a canonical description of the whole quotient.  Put
\[
 R=\mathbb F_p[C_p]\cong\mathbb F_p[u]/(u^p),
 \qquad u=t-1.
\]
Then
\[
 W/H\cong
 \left(B\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}R/(u^{p-1})\right)\rtimes C_p,             \tag{7}
\]
where \(t\) acts as multiplication by \(1+u\).  In particular, \(W/H\)
has nilpotency class exactly \(p-1\), and
\[
 \gamma_k(W/H)
 \cong B\otimes u^{k-1}R/u^{p-1}R
 \quad(2\leq k\leq p).                              \tag{8}
\]
The final term in (8), for \(k=p\), is zero.

The equality with \(\Phi(W)\) in (5)--(6) is asserted only in the finite
\(p\)-group case.  Formulas (2)--(4) need no finiteness assumption once the
finite cardinal in (4) is interpreted only when \(B\) is finite.

## Proof

### 1. The quotient \(B\)

Since every \(p\)-th power belongs to \(A'\), the quotient \(B=A/A'\) has
exponent \(p\).  It is abelian by definition and nontrivial because
\(A'<A\).  Thus \(B\) is an elementary abelian \(p\)-group.

The exact prime-wreath closure theorem identifies the closed value set as
the fiber product
\[
 H
 =\{(a_0,\ldots,a_{p-1})\in A^p:
      a_0A'=\cdots=a_{p-1}A'\}.
\]
This is exactly the inverse image of the diagonal subspace \(\Delta B\),
proving (2).

### 2. The derived subgroup of the wreath product

Let
\[
  N=\pi^{-1}(\ker\Sigma)\leq A^p.
\]
We prove \(W'=N\).

First define
\[
  \rho:W\longrightarrow B\times C_p
\]
by
\[
  \rho((a_0,\ldots,a_{p-1})t^j)
  =\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}a_iA',\,t^j\right).
                                                        \tag{7}
\]
The cyclic shift by \(t\) does not change the coordinate sum, so \(\rho\)
is a homomorphism.  It is onto, its target is abelian, and
\[
  \ker\rho=N.
\]
Therefore
\[
  W'\leq N.                                           \tag{8}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, commutators inside the base generate
\((A')^p\), so
\[
  (A')^p\leq W'.                                      \tag{9}
\]
Modulo \((A')^p\), the commutators with \(t\) have the form
\[
  [(b_0,\ldots,b_{p-1}),t]
   =(b_1-b_0,b_2-b_1,\ldots,b_0-b_{p-1}),             \tag{10}
\]
up to the harmless choice of cyclic-shift orientation.  Every tuple in
(10) has coordinate sum zero.  Conversely, if
\((c_0,\ldots,c_{p-1})\in B^p\) has sum zero, choose
\[
  b_0=0,\quad b_1=c_0,\quad
  b_2=c_0+c_1,\quad\ldots,\quad
  b_{p-1}=c_0+\cdots+c_{p-2}.
\]
Then the cyclic difference tuple in (10) is
\((c_0,\ldots,c_{p-1})\), again after matching the shift convention.
Thus the images of base--top commutators fill \(\ker\Sigma\).  Together
with (9), this gives \(N\leq W'\), and hence proves (3).

Equivalently, (7) proves the useful abelianization formula
\[
  W/W'\cong B\times C_p.                              \tag{11}
\]

### 3. Comparing the two subspaces

For \(b\in B\),
\[
  \Sigma(b,\ldots,b)=pb=0,
\]
because \(B\) has exponent \(p\).  Hence
\[
  \Delta B\leq\ker\Sigma,
\]
and inverse images under \(\pi\) give \(H\leq W'\).

If \(B\) is finite, then
\[
 |\Delta B|=|B|,\qquad
 |\ker\Sigma|=|B|^{p-1}.
\]
Since \(H\) and \(W'\) have the same kernel \((A')^p\) under \(\pi\),
\[
 [W':H]
 =[\ker\Sigma:\Delta B]
 =|B|^{p-2},
\]
which proves (4).

### 4. The Frattini subgroup

Now suppose \(A\) is a finite \(p\)-group.  Then \(W\) is a finite
\(p\)-group, so
\[
  \Phi(W)=W^pW'.                                     \tag{12}
\]
Here \(W^p\) denotes the verbal subgroup generated by all \(p\)-th powers.
Because the raw value set \(H=P_p(W)\) is already a subgroup,
\[
  W^p=H.
\]
We proved \(H\leq W'\), so (12) yields
\[
  \Phi(W)=W'.
\]
Equation (5) now follows from (4).

When \(p=2\), the exponent in (4) is zero, so \(H=W'\); (5) gives
\(H=W'=\Phi(W)\).  When \(p\) is odd, \(p-2>0\) and \(B\ne1\), so the
index is greater than one.  This proves (6) and the strict odd-prime
statement.

### 5. The quotient module and its lower central series

Since \(H=\pi^{-1}(\Delta B)\),
\[
  A^p/H\cong B^p/\Delta B.
\]
The top generator acts by cyclic coordinate shift.  Identify \(B^p\) with
\(B\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}R\), where the regular module \(R\) records the
coordinate positions.  The diagonal subspace is
\[
 B\otimes\langle1+t+\cdots+t^{p-1}\rangle.
\]
In characteristic \(p\),
\[
 1+t+\cdots+t^{p-1}=(t-1)^{p-1}=u^{p-1}.
\]
This proves the semidirect-product description (7).

The base module in (7) is abelian.  Commuting one of its elements with
\(t\) multiplies it by \(u=t-1\).  Inductively,
\[
 \gamma_k(W/H)=B\otimes u^{k-1}R/u^{p-1}R
 \quad(2\leq k\leq p).
\]
If \(p\) is odd, then \(B\ne0\) makes the term for \(k=p-1\) nonzero,
whereas the term for \(k=p\) is zero.  If \(p=2\), (7) is a nontrivial
abelian group.  In both cases the nilpotency class is exactly \(p-1\).

## Interpretation

The power-value subgroup and the derived subgroup occupy two canonical
subspaces of the same quotient:
\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
 H/(A')^p &\cong& \Delta B,\\[2mm]
 W'/(A')^p&\cong& \ker\Sigma.
\end{array}
\]
The equality at \(p=2\) is therefore not accidental: in \(B^2\), the
diagonal and the augmentation kernel coincide because \(b+b=0\).
For odd \(p\), the augmentation kernel has \(p-2\) additional copies of
\(B\).  This precisely measures how far the closed \(p\)-power-value
subgroup lies below the derived and Frattini subgroups.

The quotient description sharpens this picture: after dividing by \(H\),
all seed-specific nonabelian structure disappears, leaving the same
uniserial cyclic-shift module for every seed with the same \(B\).
