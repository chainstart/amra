# Why the positive odd-prime wreath families do not answer Wilson's question

Date: 2026-07-30

## Result

Let \(p\) be odd, let \(A\) be a finite \(p\)-group, and put
\[
  W=A\wr C_p,\qquad H=P_p(W)=\{w^p:w\in W\}.
\]
Assume that \(H\) is a subgroup.  Then \(H\) is powerful.

More precisely, exactly one of the following occurs.

1. \(A\) is elementary abelian and \(H=\Delta A\) is abelian.
2. \(A\) is a nonabelian semi-extraspecial \(p\)-group with
   \(P_p(A)=A'\), and
   \[
     H' = H^p = P_p(H)=\Delta A'.                 \tag{1}
   \]

Thus the exact cyclic-wreath closure theorem produces many nonabelian
closed \(p\)-power value sets at odd primes, but none is a counterexample to
Wilson's question asking whether such a subgroup must be powerful.

Here \(H^p\) denotes the subgroup generated by the \(p\)-th powers in \(H\).
In (1) the raw value set \(P_p(H)\) is already a subgroup.

## Proof

The finite-seed classification in
`GENERAL_PRIME_FINITE_CLASSIFICATION.md` says that a finite \(p\)-group
seed with \(P_p(A\wr C_p)\) closed is either elementary abelian or
semi-extraspecial with \(P_p(A)=A'\).

The elementary abelian case gives the diagonal subgroup directly.  Now
assume that \(A\) is semi-extraspecial.  Then
\[
  A'=Z(A)=\Phi(A)
\]
is elementary abelian.  The wreath criterion identifies
\[
  H
  =\{(a_0,\ldots,a_{p-1}):a_0A'=\cdots=a_{p-1}A'\}
  =\Delta A\,(A')^p.                              \tag{2}
\]
Every element of \(H\) therefore has the form
\[
  (az_0,\ldots,az_{p-1}),\qquad z_i\in A'.
\]

Because \(A'\) is central, commutators of two such elements are diagonal:
\[
 [(az_i)_i,(bw_i)_i]=([a,b],\ldots,[a,b]).
\]
This proves \(H'\leq\Delta A'\).  Conversely, diagonal copies of elements
of \(A\) lie in \(H\), and their commutators generate \(\Delta A'\).
Consequently
\[
 H'=\Delta A'.                                    \tag{3}
\]

Since \(A'\) is central of exponent \(p\),
\[
 (az_i)^p=a^p
\]
in every coordinate.  Hence
\[
 P_p(H)=\Delta P_p(A)=\Delta A'.                  \tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) give (1).  For odd \(p\), a finite \(p\)-group is
powerful precisely when its derived subgroup is contained in its power
subgroup, so \(H\) is powerful.

## Search consequence

For any group \(G\), if \(P_p(G)\) is a subgroup \(H\), then
\[
 P_p(H)=P_{p^2}(G).
\]
Indeed, every \(h\in H\) is \(g^p\), and \(h^p=g^{p^2}\), with the converse
immediate.  Therefore the odd-prime Wilson target is the simultaneous
condition
\[
 P_p(G)\leq G,\qquad
 [P_p(G),P_p(G)]\nleq \langle P_{p^2}(G)\rangle.  \tag{5}
\]

When \(\exp(G)\mid p^2\), the right-hand subgroup in (5) is trivial.  In
that exponent range a closed \(P_p(G)\) is non-powerful exactly when it is
nonabelian.  Outside that range, noncommutativity by itself is not enough.

The two-string subgroup of
\(\operatorname{UT}_{2p+1}(\mathbb F_p)\) belongs to the exponent-\(p^2\)
range, so the ongoing unitriangular search may use noncommutativity as an
equivalent final test.  Any higher-exponent algebra-group search must
explicitly test (5).

## Novelty status

The proof is a short corollary of the exact wreath closure/classification
theorems in this package and standard powerful-group terminology.  It is
best presented as a necessary firewall around the odd-prime search, not as
a standalone priority claim.

Wilson's question is quoted explicitly as Question 4.7 in J. Williams,
["Quasi-powerful \(p\)-groups"](https://doi.org/10.1515/jgth-2020-0197),
*Journal of Group Theory* **24** (2021), 497--517.
