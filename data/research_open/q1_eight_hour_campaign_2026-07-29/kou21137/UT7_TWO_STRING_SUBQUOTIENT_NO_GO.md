# The two-string subgroup of \(\operatorname{UT}_7(\mathbb F_3)\): a complete section no-go

Date: 2026-07-30

## Wilson target

For a finite \(3\)-group \(G\), put
\[
 P_3(G)=\{g^3:g\in G\}.
\]
The odd-prime Wilson target is not merely that \(P_3(G)\) be a
nonabelian subgroup.  If \(H=P_3(G)\) is a subgroup, the exact target is
\[
 H'\nleq H^3=P_3(H)=P_9(G).                       \tag{1}
\]
In the exponent-nine range, \(H^3=1\), so (1) is equivalent to \(H\)
being nonabelian.

## The candidate

Let \(A=E_{12}+E_{23}+E_{34}\) and
\(B=E_{45}+E_{56}+E_{67}\), and set
\[
 X=1+A,\qquad Y=1+B,\qquad
 G=\langle X,Y\rangle\leq\operatorname{UT}_7(\mathbb F_3).
\]
Exact pc-group calculation gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{invariant}&\text{value}\\ \hline
|G|&3^{13}\\
\operatorname{cl}(G)&6\\
\exp(G)&9\\
|P_3(G)|&649\\
|\langle P_3(G)\rangle|&3^{10}\\
\operatorname{cl}(\langle P_3(G)\rangle)&2\\
\exp(\langle P_3(G)\rangle)&3.
\end{array}
\]
Moreover
\[
 z=[X^3,Y^3]\ne1,\qquad
 Z(G)=\langle z\rangle
 =\langle P_3(G)\rangle',\qquad |Z(G)|=3.         \tag{2}
\]
Thus the generated cube subgroup is non-powerful, but the raw cube set is
not closed:
\[
 649<3^{10}.
\]
The apparent near miss therefore does not answer Wilson's question.

## Every normal quotient is excluded

Let \(1\ne N\trianglelefteq G\).  A nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite
\(p\)-group meets its centre nontrivially.  Since (2) says that the centre
has order three,
\[
 z\in N.
\]
The subgroup generated by the cube values of \(G/N\) is the image of
\(\langle P_3(G)\rangle\).  Its derived subgroup is therefore trivial by
(2).  Consequently, whenever \(P_3(G/N)\) is closed, it is abelian and
hence powerful.  For \(N=1\), the raw cube set is not closed.  No normal
quotient of \(G\) satisfies (1).

The exact computation independently lists all 641 normal subgroups and
checks that each of the 640 nontrivial ones contains \(z\).

## Every proper subgroup is excluded

The two-generated \(3\)-group \(G\) has four maximal subgroups.  They cover
all proper subgroups.  For each maximal subgroup \(M\), exact enumeration
gives the same row:
\[
 |M|=3^{12},\qquad |P_3(M)|=163,\qquad
 |\langle P_3(M)\rangle|=3^5,
\]
and \(\langle P_3(M)\rangle\) is abelian (and powerful).  Hence for every
proper \(S<G\),
\[
 \langle P_3(S)\rangle\leq \langle P_3(M)\rangle
\]
for some maximal \(M\), so the cube-generated subgroup of \(S\) is
abelian.  This remains true after factoring \(S\).

Combining this observation with the normal-quotient argument gives the
computer-assisted conclusion:

> **Section no-go.** No section \(S/N\) of the two-string group \(G\) has
> a closed, non-powerful raw cube set.

This closes the full subgroup/quotient repair route for this candidate,
not just a bounded selection of quotients.

## Research consequence

The two-string construction remains the sharp class-six witness for
noncommuting cubes at \(p=3\), but neither it nor any of its sections can
repair raw closure.  Future unitriangular searches must change the ambient
relations before taking sections; further quotient enumeration inside this
same group is provably redundant.
