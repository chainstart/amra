# Odd-prime diagonal-quotient no-go for \(A\wr C_p\)

Date: 2026-07-29

## Targeted mechanism

Let \(p\) be an odd prime.  The proposed repair starts with a finite
\(p\)-group \(A\) of exponent \(p^2\), puts
\[
  U=P_p(A)=\{a^p:a\in A\},
\]
and considers \(W=A\wr C_p\).  If \(U\leq Z(A)\), the diagonal copy
\[
  \Delta U=\{(u,\ldots,u):u\in U\}
\]
is central in \(W\).  Quotienting by \(\Delta U\) kills all \(p^2\)-th
powers in \(W\), so the quotient has exponent \(p^2\).  The hope was to
choose \(U<A'\) so that noncommutativity survives in the \(p\)-th-power
subgroup.

This mechanism cannot work: under these hypotheses the \(p\)-th powers of
\(W\) are never closed, and quotienting by \(\Delta U\) cannot repair the
failure of closure.

In fact, there are two stronger structural statements.  Under the central
power-value hypothesis, closure has an exact Camina criterion.  Moreover, if
one starts in the positive exponent-\(p^2\) Camina case, *every* quotient
that lowers the wreath exponent from \(p^3\) to at most \(p^2\) makes the
\(p\)-th-power subgroup abelian.  Full proofs are also isolated in
`CENTRAL_POWER_WREATH_CRITERION.md`.

## 1. Exact \(p\)-th-power formula

Let \(\mathcal C(A)\) be the set of conjugacy classes of \(A\).  For any
finite group \(A\), with no exponent assumption,
\[
  P_p(A\wr C_p)
  =
  P_p(A)^p
  \;\cup\!
  \bigcup_{C\in\mathcal C(A)} C^p.                 \tag{1}
\]
Here superscripts on sets denote Cartesian powers.

Indeed, \(p\)-th powers of base elements give \(P_p(A)^p\).  Let \(t\)
cyclically permute the \(p\) base coordinates.  For every
\(1\leq k<p\), the \(p\)-th power of an element in \(A^pt^k\) has as its
coordinates the cyclic rotations of one ordered product of the base entries,
read with step \(k\).  Since \(k\) is invertible modulo \(p\), every
coordinate occurs exactly once.  The rotations are conjugate.

Conversely, fix \(x\in A\) and arbitrary \(g_1,\ldots,g_{p-1}\in A\).
Put \(g_0=1\).  Along the step-\(k\) coordinate order choose consecutive
factors
\[
  a_i=g_i^{-1}g_{i+1}\quad(0\leq i<p-1),
  \qquad
  a_{p-1}=g_{p-1}^{-1}x.
\]
Their product is \(x\), and the cyclic product starting at position \(i\)
is \(g_i^{-1}xg_i\).  Thus every ordered \(p\)-tuple in one conjugacy class
occurs, for every nontrivial top coset.  This also checks that different
\(t^k\)-cosets do not contribute different value sets.

Assume from now on that \(U=P_p(A)\) is a central subgroup.  Distinct
conjugacy-class Cartesian powers are disjoint, and
\[
  U^p\cap\bigcup_C C^p=\Delta U.
\]
Consequently, if \(s_C=|C|\),
\[
  |P_p(W)|
  =
  |U|^p+\sum_{C\in\mathcal C(A)}s_C^p-|U|.         \tag{2}
\]

## 2. The generated \(p\)-th-power subgroup

Put \(M=UA'\).  The subgroup generated by the \(p\)-th powers is
\[
  H:=\langle P_p(W)\rangle=\Delta A\,M^p.           \tag{3}
\]

To see the nontrivial inclusion, compare \((x,\ldots,x)\) with tuples in
\((x^A)^p\).  Because their entries can be chosen independently, their
coordinatewise quotients give every commutator generator in each coordinate,
hence generate \((A')^p\).  Base \(p\)-th powers generate \(U^p\).
The reverse inclusion follows because every conjugate of \(x\) lies in
\(xA'\).  Since \(\Delta A\cap M^p=\Delta M\), formula (3) gives
\[
  |H|=|A|\,|M|^{p-1}.                              \tag{4}
\]

Equations (2) and (4) reduce \(p\)-th-power closure to an exact
conjugacy-class moment identity.

## 3. Structural closure criterion

Coordinate-axis intersections in (1) and (3) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_p(W)\cap(\{1\}^{i}\times A\times\{1\}^{p-i-1})
   &=\{1\}^{i}\times U\times\{1\}^{p-i-1},\\
 H\cap(\{1\}^{i}\times A\times\{1\}^{p-i-1})
   &=\{1\}^{i}\times UA'\times\{1\}^{p-i-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If the value set is a subgroup, it equals \(H\), and therefore \(A'\le U\).
When \(U<A\), tuples
\((x,xu_1,\ldots,xu_{p-1})\in H\) for \(x\notin U\) force
\[
  x^A=xU.
\]
This also gives \(U\le A'\), hence
\[
\boxed{
 U<A:\quad
 P_p(A\wr C_p)\le A^p
 \iff U=A'\text{ and }x^A=xU\ (x\notin U).}        \tag{5}
\]
The omitted degenerate case is \(U=A\), when \(U^p=A^p\) already makes the
value set a subgroup.  For a nontrivial finite \(p\)-group, \(U<A\)
automatically.  The positive set in (5) is the fiber product of \(p\) copies
of \(A\) over \(A/U\).

In particular, if \(A\) has exponent \(p\), then \(U=1\), so closure is
possible only for abelian \(A\), and the power subgroup is diagonal and
abelian.  Since \(\exp(A\wr C_p)=p^2\), the unquotiented exponent-\(p\) seed
mechanism cannot yield the desired odd-prime KOU counterexample.

## 4. Quantitative moment obstruction when \(U<A'\)

Suppose \(A\) is a finite \(p\)-group and
\[
  U=P_p(A)\leq Z(A)\cap A',\qquad U<A'.
\]
Write \(u=|U|\), \(d=|A'|\), and \(z=|Z(A)|\).  Then \(M=A'\), and closure
would force
\[
  |A|d^{p-1}-\sum_C s_C^p=u^p-u.                  \tag{6}
\]

Every conjugacy class is contained in an \(A'\)-coset, so \(s_C\leq d\).
Therefore the left side of (6) is
\[
  \sum_C s_C(d^{p-1}-s_C^{p-1}).
\]
The \(z\) central elements give \(z\) singleton classes, hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
  |A|d^{p-1}-\sum_Cs_C^p
  &\geq z(d^{p-1}-1)\\
  &\geq u(d^{p-1}-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(U<A'\) inside a \(p\)-group, \(d\geq pu\), and so
\[
  u(d^{p-1}-1)
  \geq p^{p-1}u^p-u
  >u^p-u.
\]
This contradicts (6).  Thus
\[
\boxed{
 U=P_p(A)\leq Z(A)\cap A',\ U<A'
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 P_p(A\wr C_p)\text{ is not a subgroup}.}
\]

## 5. Why exponent-lowering quotients cannot preserve noncommutativity

The set in (1) is invariant under multiplication by \(\Delta U\):

- \(U^p\) is invariant because \(U\) is a subgroup;
- \(C^p\Delta u=(Cu)^p\), and \(Cu\) is another conjugacy class because
  \(u\) is central.

The action is free, and \(\Delta U\leq H\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
  |P_p(W/\Delta U)|&=|P_p(W)|/|U|,\\
  |\langle P_p(W/\Delta U)\rangle|&=|H|/|U|.
\end{aligned}
\]
It follows that
\[
  P_p(W/\Delta U)\text{ is closed}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  P_p(W)\text{ is closed}.                         \tag{7}
\]

If \(A\) has exponent \(p^2\), then every \(p^2\)-th power in \(W\) belongs
to \(\Delta U\).  A base element of order \(p^2\) retains order \(p^2\)
modulo \(\Delta U\), because its \(p\)-th power is nontrivial in only one
coordinate.  Thus the quotient has exponent exactly \(p^2\), but it cannot
turn a nonclosed \(p\)-th-power set into a subgroup.

If \(A\) has class two and \(U=A'\), then
\[
  H'=\Delta A'=\Delta U,
\]
so the \(p\)-th-power subgroup in \(W/\Delta U\) is abelian.  At \(p=3\)
this explains the extraspecial exponent-nine example: closure survives the
quotient, but all noncommutativity is killed.

There is a stronger conclusion for the whole positive wreath family.  Assume
\[
  \exp(A)=p^2,\qquad U=P_p(A)=A'\le Z(A),\qquad
  x^A=xU\quad(x\notin U).
\]
Then (5) gives
\[
  H=P_p(W)\cong A\times U^{p-1},\qquad H'=\Delta U,
\]
and \(\exp(W)=p^3\).  If \(N\trianglelefteq W\) and
\(\exp(W/N)\le p^2\), then for every \(u=a^p\in U\),
\[
  ((a,1,\ldots,1)t)^{p^2}=\Delta u\in N.
\]
Thus \(\Delta U\le N\).  Since word values commute with quotient maps,
\[
  P_p(W/N)=HN/N
\]
is a subgroup, but its derived subgroup is
\[
  H'N/N=1.
\]
Hence **every** quotient that reaches exponent at most \(p^2\), not merely
the diagonal quotient, has an abelian \(p\)-th-power subgroup.

## 6. Exhaustive \(p=3\) checks

`validate_odd_diagonal_quotient_no_go.g` evaluates the exact formulas for
every SmallGroups type at orders \(27,81,243,729\).  Among exponent-nine
seeds with a central subgroup of cube values, the wreath cube set is closed
only for
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
|A|&\text{SmallGroups IDs}\\\hline
27&4\\
81&\text{none}\\
243&66\\
729&471,472,473,474.
\end{array}
\]
Every closed hit has \(U=A'\), and the closed hits through order \(729\)
have class two.  No seed with \(U<A'\) survives.

The most promising near misses are
\(\operatorname{SmallGroup}(81,i)\), \(7\leq i\leq10\).  For each one,
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &|P_3|&|\langle P_3\rangle|\\\hline
A\wr C_3&4617&6561\\
(A\wr C_3)/\Delta U&1539&2187.
\end{array}
\]
The quotient has exponent \(9\), and its generated cube group is nonabelian
of exponent \(3\), hence not powerful, but the cube values are not closed.
The identical ratio \(4617/6561=1539/2187\) is (7) in numerical form.

For the smallest direct seed
\(A=\operatorname{UT}_3(\mathbb F_3)\), write \(Z=Z(A)\).  Formula (1)
sharpens the raw counts:
\[
  H=\Delta A\,Z^3,\qquad Z(H)=Z^3,\qquad H'=\Delta Z.
\]
The eight nonzero cosets of \(A/Z\) each contribute a full 27-element
coset of \(Z^3\), while the central sector contributes only \(\Delta Z\).
Thus
\[
  |P_3(W)|=8\cdot27+3=219,
\]
and the entire 24-element deficit lies in \(Z^3\setminus\Delta Z\).
As an \(\mathbb F_3C_3\)-module under cyclic coordinate permutation,
\(Z^3\) has the chain
\[
  0<\Delta Z<\ker(z_1+z_2+z_3)<Z^3.
\]
Multiplying the value set by these successive modules gives sizes
\(219,219,225,243\).  Hence only the full \(Z^3\) completes the values to
\(H\), and it contains \(H'=\Delta Z\).  Any quotient by a
\(C_3\)-invariant central submodule that repairs closure therefore kills the
noncommutativity.  This is a sharper module-theoretic barrier for the
smallest seed.

The theorem is valid for every odd prime; the finite scan is a \(p=3\)
regression, not evidence needed by the proof.  This no-go rules out the
specified diagonal-power quotient mechanism.  It does not settle the
odd-prime branch of KOU-21.137 for unrelated constructions or for quotients
by different normal subgroups.
