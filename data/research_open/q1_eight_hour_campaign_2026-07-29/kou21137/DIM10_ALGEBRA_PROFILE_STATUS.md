# Dimension-ten algebra-group profile status

Date: 2026-07-30

## Wilson condition in the algebra model

Let \(J\) be a nilpotent associative \(\mathbb F_3\)-algebra with
\(J^9=0\), and put \(G=1+J\).  For every \(a\in J\),
\[
 (1+a)^3=1+a^3,\qquad (1+a)^9=1+a^9=1.
\]
Suppose the raw cube set \(P_3(G)\) is closed and call this subgroup \(H\).
Every element of \(H\) is itself a raw cube \(g^3\), not merely a product
of raw cubes.  Hence
\[
 h^3=g^9=1\quad(h\in H),\qquad H^3=1,             \tag{1}
\]
where \(H^3\) is the **group** subgroup generated by cubes of elements of
\(H\).  It is not the algebra ideal \(J^9\); the latter is used only to
deduce \(g^9=1\).

For odd \(p\), powerfulness is \(H'\le H^p\).  Equation (1) therefore
shows that in this closed \(J^9=0\) algebra-group setting,
\[
 H\text{ non-powerful}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H'\ne1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists a,b\in J:\ a^3b^3\ne b^3a^3.            \tag{2}
\]
Before closure is known, the subgroup generated by raw cubes need not have
exponent three.  The CEGIS model uses only the safe direction: every Wilson
counterexample must supply the witness in (2).

## Exact profile enumeration

Let \(d_i=\dim J^i/J^{i+1}\).  Noncommuting cubes require
\(J^6\ne0\) and \(d_1\ge2\).  In dimension ten, positive-composition
enumeration within the \(J^9=0\) scope gives 92 profiles:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
\text{length}&6&7&8&9\\ \hline
\text{profiles}&56&28&8&0.
\end{array}
\]

The former ledger included the length-nine composition
\((2,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)\).  That would have
\(A_9=J^9/J^{10}\ne0\), contradicting the standing assumption \(J^9=0\).
The old count 93 was therefore a scope bug, not an additional candidate.

Eight human-checkable filters are applied.

1. Multiplication induces a surjection
   \[
   (J^i/J^{i+1})\otimes(J^j/J^{j+1})
   \twoheadrightarrow J^{i+j}/J^{i+j+1},
   \]
   so
   \[
   d_{i+j}\le d_i d_j.                            \tag{3}
   \]
   This leaves 29 profiles.
2. Associativity gives
   \[
   d_2=1\Longrightarrow d_3\le1,
   \]
   leaving 23 profiles.
3. If the profile has length six and \(d_3=1\), then
   \([J^3,J^3]\le J^7=0\).  This leaves 14 profiles.
4. A one-dimensional layer can never be followed by a larger layer:
   for every \(i\ge2\),
   \[
   d_i=1\Longrightarrow d_{i+1}\le1.             \tag{4}
   \]
   To prove (4), let \(V=A_1\), let
   \(0\ne f_i\in(V^*)^{\otimes i}\) be the scalar word tensor for the
   line \(A_i\), and put \(W=A_{i+1}\).  Left and right multiplication
   give surjections \(L,R:V\to W\), while associativity gives
   \[
   f_i\otimes R=L\otimes f_i.                    \tag{5}
   \]
   For every nonzero \(\lambda\in W^*\), applying \(\lambda\) to (5)
   and using the shift-tensor lemma makes \(f_i\) a pure power of
   \(\lambda\circ L\).  If \(\dim W>1\), choose independent
   \(\lambda,\mu\in W^*\).  The same nonzero pure tensor \(f_i\) would
   force \(\lambda\circ L\) and \(\mu\circ L\) proportional, contradicting
   injectivity of the dual \(L^*\) of the surjection \(L\).  Thus
   \(\dim W\le1\).
   This excludes
   \[
   (2,2,2,1,1,2),\quad(2,2,2,1,2,1),\quad
   (2,2,1,1,2,1,1),\quad(2,2,1,2,1,1,1),
   \]
   leaving ten profiles.
5. If the profile has length six and
   \[
   d_5=d_6=1,
   \]
   the consecutive-tail tensor lemma applies.  In
   \(A=\operatorname{gr}J\), let \(f_k\) be the \(k\)-fold multiplication
   tensor on \(A_1\).  Associativity gives
   \(f_6=f_5\otimes s=t\otimes f_5\).
   The elementary identity
   \[
   T\otimes r=\ell\otimes T
   \Longrightarrow T=c\ell^{\otimes m}
   \]
   makes \(f_6\) a symmetric pure sixth power.  Hence
   \(x^3y^3=y^3x^3\) in \(A_6\), and every filtered cube commutator lies
   in \(J^7=0\).  This excludes exactly
   \[
   (2,2,2,2,1,1),\quad (2,2,3,1,1,1),\quad
   (2,3,2,1,1,1),\quad
   (3,2,2,1,1,1),
   \]
   leaving six profiles.
6. Lemma 1 of `DIM9_HUMAN_CUBE_COMMUTATIVITY.md` applies when
   \(J^8=0\) and \(d_3=d_4=d_6=1\).  After the propagation filter, it
   removes
   \[
   (2,3,1,1,1,1,1),\quad
   (3,2,1,1,1,1,1),\quad
   (4,1,1,1,1,1,1),
   \]
   leaving three profiles.
7. The cyclic-\(J^3\) lemma below removes
   \[
   (2,2,2,1,1,1,1),
   \]
   leaving two profiles.
8. The cyclic-basis lemma below removes
   \[
   (2,2,1,1,1,1,1,1),\qquad
   (3,1,1,1,1,1,1,1).
   \]
   No profile remains.

`search_dim10_algebra_profiles.py` performs only this integer arithmetic
and prints every exclusion.

## The two final tail lemmas

We repeatedly use the shift-tensor fact proved in
`DIM9_HUMAN_CUBE_COMMUTATIVITY.md`: if a nonzero word tensor passes
between consecutive one-dimensional layers, it is a pure tensor power.
Consequently, if \(A_r,\ldots,A_s\) are lines, their word tensors have
the form
\[
 f_k=c_k\ell^{\otimes k}\qquad(r\le k\le s)       \tag{6}
\]
for one common nonzero \(\ell\in A_1^*\).

**Cyclic-\(J^3\) lemma.**  Suppose \(J^8=0\),
\[
\dim A_3=2,\qquad
\dim A_4=\dim A_5=\dim A_6=\dim A_7=1.
\]
Then \(J^3\) is commutative.

Choose \(v\in A_1\) with \(\ell(v)=1\), and choose a lift \(t\in J\).
Equation (6) shows that
\[
t^4,t^5,t^6,t^7
\]
have nonzero successive leading terms and form a filtration-adapted
basis of \(J^4\).

Let \(q_3:A_1^{\otimes3}\twoheadrightarrow A_3\) be the word map.
Right and left multiplication by \(v\), with target \(A_4\), define
linear forms \(R,L:A_3\to\mathbb F_3\).  For every
\(T\in A_1^{\otimes3}\), the pure fourth word tensor gives
\[
 R(q_3(T))=f_4(T,v)=c_4\ell^{\otimes3}(T)
 =f_4(v,T)=L(q_3(T)).                            \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(R=L=:\rho\), and \(\rho\ne0\).  Choose
\(u\in J^3\) whose image spans the one-dimensional kernel of \(\rho\).
Then the leading \(A_4\) components of \(ut\) and \(tu\) vanish, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
ut&=a t^5+b t^6+c t^7,\\
tu&=a't^5+b't^6+c't^7.
\end{aligned}                                    \tag{8}
\]
Associativity in the form
\[
(tu)t=t(ut)
\]
and the independence of the leading terms of \(t^6,t^7\) give
\(a=a'\) and \(b=b'\).  The uncontrolled difference in (8) is allowed
to remain in \(J^7\); multiplying twice more by \(t\) kills it.  More
explicitly,
\[
ut^3=(ut)t^2=a t^7=t^2(tu)=t^3u.                \tag{9}
\]
Also
\[
ut^4=(ut)t^3\in J^8=0,\qquad
t^4u=t^3(tu)\in J^8=0,                           \tag{10}
\]
and the same holds for \(t^5,t^6,t^7\).  Finally
\[
t^3,u,t^4,t^5,t^6,t^7
\]
is a filtration-adapted basis of \(J^3\).  Equations (9)--(10), the
fact that the \(t^k\) are powers of one element, and
\((J^4)^2\subseteq J^8=0\) show that every pair of basis elements
commutes.  Hence \(J^3\) is commutative.

This proof does **not** discard the possible filtered correction
\(A_3A_3\to A_7\).  Equation (8) retains it, and associativity shows
that the only uncontrolled coefficient is annihilated before it can
contribute to a product in \(J^3J^3\).

**Cyclic-basis lemma.**  Suppose \(J^9=0\) and
\[
\dim A_3=\dim A_4=\cdots=\dim A_8=1.
\]
The consecutive shift identities give
\[
f_k=c_k\ell^{\otimes k}\qquad(3\le k\le8).
\]
For a lift \(t\) of any \(v\) with \(\ell(v)\ne0\), the six elements
\[
t^3,t^4,\ldots,t^8
\]
have nonzero successive leading terms.  They form a
filtration-adapted basis of \(J^3\), and all are powers of \(t\).
Thus \(J^3\) is commutative.

Both lemmas are statements about the complete filtered algebra, not
only its associated graded multiplication.  Since every cube lies in
\(J^3\), they exclude the last three profiles without a closure
assumption or a solver certificate.

## Historical bounded SMT attempt

Before the tensor lemma was found, the smallest filtered table was
\((2,2,2,2,1,1)\).  `cegis_dim10_profile_222211.py` applied the same
strict model used in dimension nine:

- 176 filtered structure variables;
- all 320 associativity coordinates and 15 ordered layer surjections;
- 16 variables for two arbitrary cube-relevant roots;
- the genuine condition \(a^3b^3\ne b^3a^3\);
- a concrete checker prepared to enumerate all 6,561 inputs and add one
  symbolic cube root per missing product.

The exact checkpoint has 725 lines, 135,484 bytes, and SHA-256

```text
c4e82fc2e7e46b8a384add0f148c121ba0b37d49b1f6d4aa2d192eb54b2aa72c
```

The tensor lemma now excludes this timed-out profile and the other three
profiles listed above.  The bounded solver produced no candidate and is
not a premise of any exclusion.  The rigorous current boundary is:

> No dimension-ten profile survives the human filters.  Therefore any
> nilpotent associative \(\mathbb F_3\)-algebra \(J\) with \(J^9=0\)
> and two noncommuting raw cubes has
> \(\dim J\ge11\).

The historical timeout is retained only as a reproducibility record.
It no longer marks a mathematical search boundary.

## Sharpness boundary

The companion `DIM11_SHARP_NONCOMMUTING_CUBE_WITNESS.md` gives an
associative dimension-eleven algebra with profile
\((2,2,2,2,2,1)\) and two noncommuting raw cubes.  Its raw cube set is
not closed, so it is not a Wilson counterexample.  It does show that the
pure cube-commutativity bound \(\dim J\ge11\) is sharp and that any
further Wilson progress must use raw-cube closure rather than only
associativity and the power profile.  The closure lemma in
`DIM11_CLOSURE_AWARE_STATUS.md` now excludes this particular length-six
profile under the Wilson hypothesis, leaving two dimension-eleven
length-seven profiles unresolved.
