# Unit-rank uniformity and coordinate-volume audit

For one fixed number field \(K\), the estimate

\[
\#\{\varepsilon:h(\varepsilon)\le C\log n\}
=O_K((\log n)^r)
\]

is correct.  It is not uniform when \(K=K_n\) varies: the hidden constant
contains the geometry of the logarithmic unit lattice, while \(|A|=n\)
alone does not bound arithmetic height.

## Constant-free packing

Let \(\Lambda_n\) be the rank-\(r_n\) logarithmic lattice generated by the
relevant units.  Equip the logarithmic coordinate space with the supremum
norm, and let

\[
\lambda_n=\min\{\|v\|_\infty:v\in\Lambda_n\setminus\{0\}\}.
\]

Suppose all relevant parameters lie in a logarithmic cube of radius
\(H_n=C_n\log n\).  Packing sup-norm cubes of radius \(\lambda_n/2\)
gives

\[
|T_n|\le
\left(1+\frac{2C_n\log n}{\lambda_n}\right)^{r_n}.         \tag{1}
\]

An average gain \(n^{2/5}\) needs \(\Omega(n^{2/5})\) distinct curve
points, since one point contributes at most \(n^2\).  The parametrization
has bounded fibres, so

\[
r_n\ge
\frac{(2/5)\log n+O(1)}
{\log(1+2C_n\log n/\lambda_n)}.                            \tag{2}
\]

If

\[
\log C_n+\log(1/\lambda_n)=o(\log\log n),                 \tag{3}
\]

then

\[
r_n\ge(2/5-o(1))\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}.                \tag{4}
\]

This covers a fixed field with a uniformly polynomial-height container.
For varying fields, (4) requires (3); it is not automatic.

## Degree-dependent regime

Assume a full degree-\(d_n\) power-basis box, every coordinate active with
side at least two, and fitting the difference box implies logarithmic
height \(O(\log n)\).  Then \(d_n\le\log_2n\).

The standard degree-dependent height lower bound for a non-torsion
algebraic unit also gives
\(\lambda_n=\|\log|\sigma(\varepsilon)|\|_\infty
\ge d_n^{-1-o(1)}\).  The denominator of (2) is then
at most \((2+o(1))\log\log n\), giving only

\[
\boxed{
r_n\ge(1/5-o(1))\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}.
}                                                         \tag{5}
\]

Thus \(1/5\), not \(2/5\), is the safe coefficient in this model.  Without
height/container uniformity, even that coefficient is not justified.

## Coordinate volume

Since \(r_n\le d_n-1\), a proper full-coordinate binary box has at least
\(2^{r_n+1}\) points.  At
\(r_n=\Theta(\log n/\log\log n)\),

\[
2^{r_n}=n^{O(1/\log\log n)}=n^{o(1)}.                     \tag{6}
\]

The bare coordinate cost does not cancel a possible \(n^{2/5}\) word
count.  A no-go theorem needs a stronger link from independent
logarithmic directions to independent additive side growth.

## Strict conditional theorem

Assume logarithmic radius \(C_n\log n\), shortest lattice vector
\(\lambda_n\), and bounded fibres to curve points.  Target compatibility
implies (2).  Under (3), it implies (4).  Under the full power-basis degree
assumptions, the available uniform conclusion is (5).

No \(2/5\) coefficient follows from fixed-field Dirichlet counting alone
when the field, regulator, shortest unit, or additive-generator height
varies arbitrarily with \(n\).

The next missing result is a uniform height-versus-additive-container
inequality controlling \(C_n/\lambda_n\), or additive side volume, from
the number of independent unit directions.

The raw form of this statement is false: four multiplicatively independent
units in an explicit totally real quintic lie in a two-dimensional additive
space.  A viable replacement must use the inverse-symmetrized shifts
\(t+R/t,t-R/t\) and a bounded-index integral coordinate lattice.  See
`SYMMETRIZED_UNIT_CONTAINER_AUDIT.md`.

For that explicit polynomial family the inverse-symmetrized coefficient
question is now exact: one full coefficient minor equals

\[
4\cdot3069^{d-2}\prod_{m=1}^{d-3}m!
\]

in absolute value.  Whenever \(P_d(x)-1\) is irreducible of degree \(d\)
and a real root is selected, this proves full additive rank and forces
\(\exp(\Theta(d^2\log d))\) proper-box volume for the selected shifts.
Irreducibility and the required real-root property are currently verified
only for \(5\le d\le30\), so this is not yet an unconditional asymptotic
number-field family.  Along any unbounded sequence for which those
hypotheses hold, it still supplies only \(d-1=n^{o(1)}\) curve parameters
at that container size.
