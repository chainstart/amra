# Scalability audit for the translated-Latin SAT core

Date: 2026-07-30

## Outcome

`TRANSLATED_LATIN_SERVICE_COMPLEX.md` gives a genuine six-service real SAT
core.  This note tests two natural amplifications:

1. common translation copies on the same five-radius skeleton;
2. disjoint/direct layers of the five-radius skeleton.

Neither approaches the critical obstruction.

Common translation copies keep the full distance-cell count linear in the
number of copies.  Same-copy edges preserve the six visited cells and give
linearly many services.  All cross-copy edges give quadratically many
services and \(O(m)\) visited cells.  In both versions every cross edge
still has service degree one, and the construction has only
\(\Theta(m)\) points because the radius skeleton is fixed.

Separated full-cross-copy layers produce \(\Theta(L^3)\) services in the
balanced regime, short of the required \(L^{33/10}\) by \(L^{3/10}\).
Their natural global cell count is \(\Omega(L^3)\), exceeding the allowed
\(L^{27/10}\) by \(L^{3/10}\).  They also retain service degree one.

Thus the finite SAT core is not a geometric counterexample and these
direct amplifications do not change that conclusion.

The note also proves an exact palette-capacity lemma.  If service arcs are
coloured by their ordered signed pair \((\lambda,\mu)\), a synchronized
cycle is forced once the number of arcs exceeds the number of colours
times the relevant extremal capacity.  At \(\eta=1/30\), a synchronized
four- or six-cycle in the partner projection is forced only when the
shift-pair palette is below \(L^{19/30}\); in the hub projection the
threshold is \(L^{23/30}\).  No bound this small is presently available.

No unconditional exponent improvement is claimed.

## 1. Common translation copies

Start with the \(q=3,U=2\) SAT core.  Its five height sets contain three
points each, it has six services, twelve cross edges and six visited cross
cells.

Choose a translation set
\[
 A=\{0,T,2T,\ldots,(r-1)T\}
\]
with \(T\) avoiding the finitely many base-coordinate collisions.  Replace
every base height \(z\) in every radius class by
\[
 z+A.
\]
For each \(t\in A\), retain the translated copy of every base service,
using the same \(t\) at all four endpoints.

The resulting counts are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 m&=3r,\\
 S&=6r,\\
 E_{\rm cross}&=12r,\\
 \frac{2S}{E_{\rm cross}}&=1.                \tag{1}
\end{aligned}
\]
All signed cross shifts are unchanged, so the visited cross-cell set still
has size six.  Each visited cell now has \(r\) representations.

The full height-difference palette on the fixed skeleton remains linear.
For fixed base residues \(x,y\),
\[
 (x+t)-(y+t')=(x-y)+(t-t'),
\]
and \(A-A\) has \(2r-1\) values.  There are only constantly many base
residue and radius pairs, so
\[
 M_{\rm full}=O(r)=O(m).                     \tag{2}
\]

Here \(M_{\rm full}\) counts all axial product/value cells on this skeleton.
Restoring angular copies can only add distance values; the estimate is an
optimistic obstruction audit, not a complete three-dimensional distance
construction.

This is a legitimate real amplification, but it has only five radius
classes and \(\Theta(m)\) points.  More importantly, (1) shows that it
does not amplify compatibility degree.

### Full cross-copy replication

Retain every cross-copy version of each base Latin edge.  Translate the two
original diagonal pairs of a base service independently.  Every ordered
pair of translations \((s,t)\) gives one service whose opposite cross edge
uses the swapped pair \((t,s)\).  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 m&=3r,\\
 S&=6r^2,\\
 E_{\rm cross}&=12r^2,\\
 \frac{2S}{E_{\rm cross}}&=1.                \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
The signed shifts now range over translates of \(A-A\), so both the
visited and full cell counts remain \(O(r)\).

Choose the progression step \(T\) transcendental over the number field
generated by the finite SAT core.  If the first cross edge uses translation
indices \((i,j)\) and a proposed opposite edge uses \((k,l)\), subtracting
the base Gram equation gives
\[
 T^2\bigl((i-l)^2-(k-j)^2\bigr)
 +2T\bigl(X(i-l)-Y(k-j)\bigr)=0,             \tag{4}
\]
where \(X,Y\) are the base original vertical differences.  The original
radius blocks are distinct, so \(X\ne\pm Y\).  Transcendence forces
\[
 i=l,\qquad k=j.
\]
The swapped edge is therefore the unique compatible partner.  Even the
full cross-copy amplification has degree one.

### Why the fixed cell palette permits only same-copy partners

Take one base service and translate each of its two cross edges internally:
both endpoints of the first cross edge by \(s\), and both endpoints of the
second by \(t\).  Their signed shifts \(\lambda,\mu\) remain fixed, while
the partner displacement becomes
\[
 \theta+(s-t).
\]
The service-pair formula uniquely prescribes \(\theta\), so \(s=t\).
Hence the finite cell palette permits only the diagonal same-copy service.

The full cross-copy construction admits those new types, but generic
translations still give degree one.  Resonant translation sets could
create extra solutions to (4); obtaining enough of them is a new
additive-hyperbola problem and is not ruled out here.

## 2. Separated layers

Take \(k\) disjoint copies of the five-radius skeleton and use the full
cross-copy construction with \(r\) translations in each layer.  Separate
the geometric scales and layer offsets so cells from distinct radius blocks
do not collide.  The direct-layer counts are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \text{radius classes}&=5k,\\
 m&=3r,\\
 S&=6kr^2,\\
 E_{\rm cross}&=12kr^2,\\
 \frac{2S}{E_{\rm cross}}&=1.                \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]

Every pair of radius classes contains at least \(r\) distinct vertical
squared differences from the common translation progression.  Separation
of radial offsets therefore gives
\[
 M_{\rm full}
 \geq
 r\binom{5k}{2}
 =\Omega(k^2r).                              \tag{6}
\]

In the balanced scaling \(k\asymp r\asymp L\),
\[
 S=L^{3+o(1)},\qquad M_{\rm full}=L^{3+o(1)}. \tag{7}
\]
Compared with the campaign requirements,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S_{\rm required}&=L^{33/10-o(1)},\\
 M_{\rm allowed}&=L^{27/10+o(1)},
\end{aligned}
\]
the direct layers have service deficit \(L^{3/10}\) and cell excess
\(L^{3/10}\).

The lower bound (6) is a statement about the separated/direct layering
tested here.  A sophisticated product construction designed to collide
cross-layer cells could evade it; no such construction is known.

## 3. The coloured service projection

Consider either the partner- or hub-coordinate service projection.  After
selecting one representative of every parallel arc, let it be a finite
simple graph \(G\) with:

* \(N\) coordinate vertices;
* \(S\) service arcs;
* an edge colour
  \[
  \chi(\sigma)=(\lambda_\sigma,\mu_\sigma)
  \]
  from a palette of size \(K\).

Every even cycle within one colour has synchronized signed shifts.  The
cochain identity from the preceding round then gives
\[
 \sum_{\sigma\in C}(-1)^\sigma
 \bigl(C_{x_\sigma y_\sigma}
       -C_{u_\sigma v_\sigma}\bigr)=0.        \tag{8}
\]

Parallel service arcs omitted during simplification are already endpoint
reuse.  If two such arcs have different forced displacement labels, they
are forbidden; if their labels agree, they form a closed synchronized
two-cycle.  Thus simplifying separates, rather than hides, the first reuse
pattern.

## 4. Exact palette-capacity lemma

### Lemma 1 (first synchronized cycle)

If no colour class of \(G\) contains a cycle, then
\[
 S\leq K(N-1).                               \tag{9}
\]
Consequently \(S>K(N-1)\) forces a synchronized cycle and hence one radial
relation of the form (8).

### Proof

Every colour class is a forest and has at most \(N-1\) edges.  Sum over
the \(K\) colours. \(\square\)

### Lemma 2 (short synchronized cycle capacity)

There are absolute constants \(C_4,C_{46}\) such that:

1. if no colour class contains a \(C_4\), then
   \[
   S\leq C_4K N^{3/2};
                                                        \tag{10}
   \]
2. if no colour class contains a \(C_4\) or \(C_6\), then
   \[
   S\leq C_{46}K N^{4/3}.                   \tag{11}
   \]

The first statement is the elementary common-neighbour proof of the
\(C_4\) extremal bound, applied to every colour.  The second is the
standard even-cycle extremal bound.  Both concern the simplified graph;
parallel arcs have the separate interpretation above.

Thus:

* \(S\gg KN^{3/2}\) forces a synchronized four-cycle;
* \(S\gg KN^{4/3}\) forces a synchronized four- or six-cycle.

Every forced cycle is a countable radial low-rank event.  If
\({\cal C}_4(c)\) is the number of colour-\(c\) coordinate four-cycles,
then
\[
 \sum_c{\cal C}_4(c)
\]
is a lower bound for the number, with incidence multiplicity, of radial
gap quadruples satisfying
\[
 \Delta_1-\Delta_2+\Delta_3-\Delta_4=0.       \tag{12}
\]
This converts the conditional cochain identity into a precise radial
additive-energy statistic.

## 5. Critical palette thresholds

At \(\eta=1/30\),
\[
 S=L^{33/10-o(1)}.
\]
The partner projection has
\[
 N_N\leq L^2,
\]
and the hub projection has
\[
 N_H\leq UL=L^{19/10+o(1)}.
\]

Applying (7)--(9) gives:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{forced event}&\text{partner palette }K&
                     \text{hub palette }K\\ \hline
\text{some synchronized cycle}
 &K<L^{13/10-o(1)}&K<L^{7/5-o(1)}\\
\text{synchronized }C_4
 &K<L^{3/10-o(1)}&K<L^{9/20-o(1)}\\
\text{synchronized }C_4\text{ or }C_6
 &K<L^{19/30-o(1)}&K<L^{23/30-o(1)}.
\end{array}                                   \tag{13}
\]

The desired cross-edge compatibility degree is only \(L^{2/5}\).
That degree alone is below the \(N^{1/3}\) or \(N^{1/2}\) thresholds that
force a short cycle in one unrestricted colour class.  A palette bound or
additional multipartite expansion is essential.

No current consequence of \(M\leq L^{27/10}\) bounds the numerical
\((\lambda,\mu)\) palette by either \(L^{19/30}\) or \(L^{23/30}\).
The cell label forgets its sign and radius-block representation, and
different block pairs can produce distinct shift pairs.  Therefore (13)
is the first rigorous synchronized-cycle threshold, but it does not close
the endpoint.

## 6. Scalability verdict

The two tested amplifications fail for complementary reasons.

* Same-skeleton translations retain excellent cell compression,
  \(M=O(m)\), and can grow service count quadratically, but do not grow
  service degree or the number of radius classes.
* Direct layers grow the point count, but retain degree one, lose
  \(L^{3/10}\) in service mass and naturally spend \(L^{3/10}\) too much
  cell capacity.

The SAT core therefore remains a finite local warning, not a counterexample
to the original geometric statement.

The next construction attempt must choose a resonant translation set that
adds partners beyond the unique swapped edge in (4).  The next proof
attempt must bound the resulting shift-pair palette or show that many
palette colours share a common denominator/sign pattern, allowing the
radial additive-energy count (12) to be summed across colours.
