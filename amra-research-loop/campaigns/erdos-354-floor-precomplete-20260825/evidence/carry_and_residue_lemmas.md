# Carry and residue lemmas for Erdős #354

Put \(x_s=\lfloor2^s\alpha\rfloor\) and
\(y_s=\lfloor2^s\beta\rfloor\).

## Lemma 1: the interleaved union is a Sigma-sequence

For \(\theta>0\), write

\[
 a_{s+1}=2a_s+\epsilon_s,\qquad
 a_s=\lfloor2^s\theta\rfloor,\quad \epsilon_s\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Summing the recurrence gives the exact defect identity

\[
 a_{n+1}-\sum_{s=0}^{n}a_s
 =a_0+\sum_{s=0}^{n}\epsilon_s.
\]

Interleave \(x_0,y_0,x_1,y_1,\ldots\).  Before \(x_{n+1}\) is inserted, its
defect relative to all preceding terms is

\[
 x_0+\sum_{s=0}^{n}\epsilon_s^{(x)}-sum_{s=0}^{n}y_s.
\]

The positive part is at most \(x_0+n+1\), whereas the last sum grows
exponentially.  Hence this expression is bounded above over all \(n\).
Before \(y_{n+1}\), there is additionally the positive term \(x_{n+1}\) in
the preceding sum, so its defect is bounded above as well.  Thus the
interleaved union is a Sigma-sequence for every positive \(\alpha,\beta\).

This supplies the growth half of the standard Sigma-plus-precomplete
criterion.  It does not prove precompleteness.

## Lemma 2: every modulus is covered by a finite prefix

If \(\theta\) is not a dyadic rational, then for every integer \(q\ge2\), the
subset sums of a finite prefix of \((\lfloor2^s\theta\rfloor)_{s\ge0}\)
cover every residue class modulo \(q\).

Let \(R\subseteq\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) be the residues that occur infinitely
often.  If the subgroup generated by \(R\) were proper, some prime \(p\mid q\)
would divide every sufficiently late \(a_s\).  The recurrence then gives
\(\epsilon_s=a_{s+1}-2a_s\equiv0\pmod p\).  Since
\(\epsilon_s\in\{0,1\}\), all sufficiently late \(\epsilon_s\) would be zero,
making \(\theta\) dyadic rational, a contradiction.  Therefore \(R\)
generates the finite cyclic group.  Each generator in \(R\) occurs infinitely
often, so the finitely many nonnegative multiplicities needed to realise all
group elements can be supplied by distinct occurrences in one finite prefix.

If \(\alpha/\beta\) is irrational, at least one of \(\alpha,\beta\) is not
dyadic rational.  Hence the union in the public problem has complete finite
subset-sum coverage modulo every fixed \(q\).

## Exact remaining gap

Full modular coverage for each fixed \(q\) does not provide one integer
interval whose length grows past the next unused term.  The prefix may depend
arbitrarily badly on \(q\), and modular representatives can lie far apart.
The missing bridge is a quantitative lift from residue coverage to
precompleteness.  Neither lemma resolves the base-2 question, and the second
lemma uses the integral binary carry recurrence and therefore does not settle
the general \(\gamma\in(1,2)\) variant.

## Killed transfers

- Irrational ratio does not imply density of the doubling orbit: an
  irrational binary expansion with ones only at positions \(2^j\) has an
  orbit avoiding the binary cylinder `11`.
- A unit signed relation does not simply replicate under index shift: shifting
  doubles its value and adds a carry term.
- Floor/ceiling perturbations are one per selected term, so their accumulated
  subset-sum error is unbounded.
