# High-support centered correlation: multiplier pairing and block cumulants

This second-stage note works only on the high-conductor interface left by
`weighted_multiplicative_character_deepening.md`.  It tests two mechanisms
that retain special 451 structure: multiplication of the product multiset
by a small unit, and a conditional cluster expansion over prime-offset
blocks.  Both yield exact identities, but their natural separated handoffs
are rigorously obstructed.  The no-go statements below are limited to those
handoffs; they do not refute a direct signed estimate of the full-order
centered correlation.  Phase remains `survivor_deepening`, with `closes=[]`.

## 1. Centered functions and the product multiset

For a remaining prime `p=k+b`, put

\[
 d_p=b-1,\qquad
 A_p=-Q_0^{-1}\{1,2,\ldots,d_p\}\pmod p,
 \qquad \delta_p={d_p\over p-1},
\]

and

\[
 z_p(x)={\bf1}_{A_p}(x)-\delta_p.                  \tag{1}
\]

Fix

\[
 X=\exp(\gamma k/\log k)
\]

with `gamma>0`, and let `W` be the multiset of products

\[
 W=\{ut:1\le u,t\le X,\ (ut,P)=1\}.                \tag{2}
\]

It has `N=(1+o(1))X^2=exp(o(k))` elements counted with multiplicity, and
every represented value is at most `T=X^2=exp(o(k))`.  For a prime set `S`,
write

\[
 Z_S(x)=\prod_{p\in S}z_p(x),
 \qquad C_S(W)=\sum_{x\in W}Z_S(x).                \tag{3}
\]

The exact character inversion from the preceding note is

\[
 \left(\prod_{p\notin S}\delta_p\right)C_S(W)
 =\sum_{\operatorname{supp}\chi=S}c_\chi
      S_X^P(\chi)^2.                               \tag{4}
\]

Thus every statement below is about the same fixed product multiset, not a
translate average.

## 2. Exact local multiplier correlations

For a unit `g mod p`, define

\[
 R_p(g)={1\over p-1}\sum_{x\in(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^*}
             z_p(x)z_p(gx).
\]

The common-start interval gives the exact overlap formula

\[
 R_p(g)={|A_p\cap g^{-1}A_p|\over p-1}-\delta_p^2. \tag{5}
\]

This retains more than the cardinality `d_p`; the overlap sees the actual
dilate.

Because `2d_p<p` for every `p<2k`, one has

\[
 A_p\cap(-A_p)=\emptyset,
 \qquad
 {R_p(-1)\over\delta_p(1-\delta_p)}=-{d_p\over k}. \tag{6}

Thus `-1` is a genuine local sign reversal at every remaining prime.  On
the other hand, multiplication by `2` has no wrap and

\[
 |A_p\cap2^{-1}A_p|=\lfloor d_p/2\rfloor,           \tag{7}
\]

so

\[
 {R_p(2)\over\delta_p(1-\delta_p)}
 ={(p-1)\lfloor d_p/2\rfloor-d_p^2\over d_pk}.      \tag{8}
\]

For even `d_p`, (8) is exactly `(k-d_p)/(2k)>0`; for odd `d_p` its numerator
is `(d_p(k-d_p-1)-k)/2`.  In particular it is positive and asymptotic to
`1/2` throughout a narrow-offset range with `d_p` tending to infinity and
`d_p=o(k)`.  More generally, if a fixed positive integer `g` satisfies
`gd_p<p`, then the overlap in (5) is `floor(d_p/g)`, giving positive
normalized correlation `1/g+o(1)` when `d_p=o(k)`.

Consequently no fixed positive small unit is a coordinatewise sign reversal
on all offset blocks.  The only universal elementary reversal above is
`-1`, whose positive representative modulo

\[
 P=\prod_{k+A<p<2k}p
\]

is `P-1=exp((1+o(1))k)` and is outside the target budget.

There is a second, global obstruction to using a short list of small
multipliers as a spectral pairing family.

> **Fixed-generator quadratic resonance lemma.**  Let \(\mathcal P\) be
> any set of `m` odd primes, put \(P=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p\), and let
> \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\) be units modulo `P`.  If `s<m`, there is a quadratic
> Dirichlet character `chi mod P` such that
> \[
>  \chi(g_i)=1\quad(1\le i\le s),
>  \qquad |\operatorname{supp}\chi|\ge {m-s\over2}.       \tag{8a}
> \]

**Proof.**  For every `p` choose the local quadratic character `eta_p` and
identify the characters \(\prod_p\eta_p^{v_p}\) with
\(v\in\mathbf F_2^m\).  The equations \(\chi(g_i)=1\) are at most `s`
linear equations on `v`, so their common kernel `K` has dimension at least
`m-s`.  Let `I` be the coordinates on which some vector of `K` is nonzero.
Projection gives \(\dim K\le |I|\).  For a uniformly random vector of `K`,
each coordinate in `I` is `1` with probability `1/2`; hence the average
Hamming weight is \(|I|/2\ge(m-s)/2\).  Some vector has at least this
weight, proving (8a). `square`

Thus every averaging walk supported on the subgroup generated by
`g_1,...,g_s` has eigenvalue `1` on a character of conductor at least
\(k^{(m-s)/2}\) when all primes exceed `k`.  In the 451 set
`m=Theta(k/log k)`, so `s=o(m)` leaves a genuinely high-conductor exact
resonance.  Taking `s` comparable with `m` would require a near-full-rank
Legendre-symbol matrix for the chosen affordable integers; no such input is
provided here.  This lemma kills generator-only uniform spectral
contraction, not
a position-dependent multiplier chosen after seeing the current residue.
It is not an actual signed-error lower bound: the interval Fourier
coefficient of the resonant quadratic character can vanish, and no claim
that many of those local coefficients are nonzero has been proved.

## 3. A fixed-prefix transport obstruction

Even a multiplier with favorable complete-group covariance must act on the
short positive multiset.  The following elementary lemma quantifies the
missing handoff.

> **Positive-dilation variation lemma.**  Let `mu` be any probability
> measure supported on positive integers in `[1,T]`, let `g>=2` be an
> integer, and assume `gT<P`, so reduction modulo `P` causes no wrap.  If
> `g_*mu` is the push-forward under `x maps to gx`, then
> \[
> \|\mu-g_*\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
> \ge {\log g\over\log(gT)}.                        \tag{9}
> \]

**Proof.**  The function `phi(x)=log(x)/log(gT)` lies in `[0,1]` on the
union of the two supports.  Its expectations differ by exactly
`log(g)/log(gT)`.  The variational characterization of total variation
proves (9). `square`

For the normalized product-multiset weight in (2), `log T=O(k/log k)`.
Therefore every positive integer dilation has boundary discrepancy at least

\[
 \gg {\log k\over k},                               \tag{10}
\]

whereas the desired survivor density is

\[
 \delta=\prod_p\delta_p=\exp(-\Theta(k/\log k)).    \tag{11}
\]

The transport boundary in (10) is exponentially larger than (11).  A
coordinate pairing makes this even more visible: `(u,t) maps to (gu,t)`
stays in the rectangle only for `u<=X/g`, leaving a constant fraction of
pairs if the remainder is bounded absolutely.

This kills the following precise mechanism: infer complete-group
contraction from (5), pair `W` with `gW`, and pay the unmatched mass in
absolute value.  It does not kill a signed telescoping identity that also
proves cancellation on the dilation boundary.  Such an identity is not
currently available.

There is also no exact affordable invariance hidden here.  If a nonempty
finite set of positive representatives below `T` were mapped into itself by
`x maps to gx mod P`, with `g>=2` and `gT<P`, its maximum element would map
to the strictly larger integer `gx`, a contradiction.

## 4. Dyadic block cluster expansion

Partition the remaining primes by dyadic offset blocks

\[
 \mathcal B(B)=\{p=k+b:B<b\le2B\}.                 \tag{12}
\]

For one block, the exact expansion is

\[
 \prod_{p\in\mathcal B}(\delta_p+z_p(x))
 =\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal B}
   \left(\prod_{p\in\mathcal B\setminus S}\delta_p\right)Z_S(x).
                                                               \tag{13}
\]

A conditional cumulant scheme may estimate the proper supports in (13),
but it must retain the full-order remainder \(C_{\mathcal B}(W)\).  That remainder
has an exact block character kernel.

Put \(P_{\mathcal B}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal B}p\).  For any multiset `V` of `M`
units represented below `T`, orthogonality gives

\[
 \sum_{\operatorname{supp}\chi=\mathcal B}
 \left|\sum_{x\in V}\chi(x)\right|^2
 =\sum_{x,y\in V}\prod_{p\in\mathcal B}
 \begin{cases}
 p-2,&p\mid x-y,\\
 -1,&p\nmid x-y.
 \end{cases}                                       \tag{14}
\]

The identical-index contribution is

\[
 M\prod_{p\in\mathcal B}(p-2).                    \tag{15}
\]

For distinct represented values, the magnitude of the product in (14) is
at most the product of the block primes dividing `x-y`, hence at most `T`.
Thus

> **Block diagonal lemma.**  If
> \[
> \prod_{p\in\mathcal B}(p-2)>2MT,
> \]
> then
> \[
> \sum_{\operatorname{supp}\chi=\mathcal B}
> \left|\sum_{x\in V}\chi(x)\right|^2
> \ge {M\over2}\prod_{p\in\mathcal B}(p-2).        \tag{16}
> \]

Equal values from different multiset indices add positive copies of (15);
all unequal pairs have total absolute contribution at most `M^2T`, proving
(16).

## 5. A macroscopic block defeats the energy handoff

Take the bulk dyadic block

\[
 \mathcal B_* =\mathcal B(k/2)=\{p:3k/2<p<2k\}.   \tag{17}
\]

The prime number theorem gives

\[
 |\mathcal B_*|=\Theta(k/\log k),\qquad
 \log P_{\mathcal B_*}=(1/2+o(1))k.                \tag{18}
\]

For the original `W`, or for any submultiset left after conditioning on all
earlier blocks, one has `M<=N=exp(O(k/log k))` and `T=exp(O(k/log k))`.
Therefore (16) applies with an exponential margin whenever `M>0`.

The exact coefficient square mass of the block-full slice is

\[
 \prod_{p\in\mathcal B_*}\delta_p(1-\delta_p)
 =\exp(-\Theta(k/\log k)),                          \tag{19}
\]

because both `delta_p` and `1-delta_p` are bounded away from zero and one
on (17).  Consequently, even after every proper-support or earlier-block
term has been treated, the right-hand side produced by separated
Cauchy--Schwarz on the full-order block remainder, divided by its
density-scale main term, is at least

\[
 {1\over\sqrt{2M}}
 \prod_{p\in\mathcal B_*}
 \sqrt{{(p-2)(1-\delta_p)\over\delta_p}}
 =\exp((1/4+o(1))k).                              \tag{20}
\]

Here the equality uses \(\log P_{\mathcal B_*}=(1/2+o(1))k\), while
`log M=o(k)` and all density factors contribute only
`exp(O(k/log k))`.  Conditioning can only decrease
`M`, which makes the diagonal-to-main ratio worse.

This proves a delimited block-cumulant no-go: product equality/difference
energy plus a separate coefficient norm cannot absorb the full-order
remainder of even one macroscopic dyadic offset block.  It does not refute
direct signed cancellation between the coefficient vector and the kernel,
nor cancellation between different full-order block remainders.

## 6. Surviving statement after both tests

Mechanism A proves exact local contraction for special multipliers, but its
fixed-generator version has exact high-conductor quadratic resonances, and
its positive-prefix transport boundary is much larger than the target
density.
Mechanism B may organize proper supports, but its full-order remainder
cannot be handed to equality/difference energy and separated `L^2`.

The remaining assertion is therefore concrete: for the actual product
multiset (2), prove signed cancellation in the high-conductor sum of (4),
including the full-order remainder of a macroscopic block, without replacing
either side by an absolute norm and without transporting `W` by a positive
dilation at absolute boundary cost.  This is a strict narrowing of the
mechanism class, but it remains open and does not change the public upper
bound.
