# Representation search

Five representation families separate the arithmetic scaling, adaptive query
transcript, randomized mixture, density boundary, and excluded global scope.

The geometric family exposes a genuine threshold.  Starting edge primes near
`N^(1/2-epsilon)` leaves only `epsilon K` exponent units after multiplying
two incident primes.  A path with `s` vertices on the smaller shore needs a
balancing decrement of order `K/s`; choosing `s` of order `epsilon K`
therefore requires `epsilon^2 K` to diverge.  Starting above `sqrt(N)` is
impossible for this path host because an internal rough product already
exceeds `N`.

The transcript family is strictly broader than a static cylinder: primes
queried after nonzero answers may be arbitrary and seed-dependent.  Only the
finite sequence reached after repeated zero valuations is shared by all
integers coprime to its primes.

The randomized family conditions on one shared seed.  A transversal must hit
every bad relation, so the relation witnessing failure may depend on that
seed.  This makes the zero-leaf deletion event a necessary condition for
success and converts its deterministic population cost into an expectation
bound.

The density and scope comparators prevent overstatement.  Dense zero
transcripts, observation of the padding prime, per-integer programs, fresh
unshared query randomness, and inter-vertex alteration do not retain the
common positive-density leaf used by the proof.
