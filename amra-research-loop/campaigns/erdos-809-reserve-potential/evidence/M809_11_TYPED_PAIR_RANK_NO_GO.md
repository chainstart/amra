## 1. The only overlap-safe A rank

For every repeated colour `gamma` in a B-opposite configuration, let

\[
a_\gamma=x_\gamma y_\gamma\in\overline E(G[A])
\]

be its forced owned diagonal from
`M809_11_GRAPH_REALIZABILITY.md`.  For a colour set `T`, define

\[
\rho_A(T)=|\{a_\gamma:\gamma\in T\}|.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This is a coverage rank.  It is monotone and submodular: each missing A
edge `a` contributes the indicator that at least one colour in `T` owns
`a`, and this Boolean coverage indicator is submodular.  Thus (1.1) counts
an A edge once even when it is generated by several coherent stars.

Similarly, if `K(e_gamma)` is the canonical B reserve of the colour's base
pair, the overlap-safe B rank is

\[
\rho_B(T)=\left|\bigcup_{\gamma\in T}K(e_\gamma)\right|.
\tag{1.2}
\]

This is also a monotone submodular coverage rank.  It consists of actual
missing B edges, so it may directly certify `D_B<=M_B` through Hall or the
global union test.

The sound object is the **typed pair**

\[
(\rho_A,\rho_B),
\tag{1.3}
\]

not the scalar sum `rho_A+rho_B`.  The two coordinates live in disjoint
missing-edge budgets.

## 2. Minimal cross-star overlap certificate

Two different coherent stars can own the same missing A diagonal.  The
smallest endpoint model uses two repeated colours:

\[
\gamma:(b_1x_1,c_1y_1),
\qquad
\delta:(b_2x_1,c_2y_1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Both stars own `x1y1`.  Same-colour edges are disjoint and the missing
inner pair is compatible with the induced-matching constraint.  Per-star
counting returns two owned atoms, while

\[
\rho_A(\{\gamma,\delta\})=1.
\]

On the same underlying graph, recolour the second repeated pair as

\[
\delta:(b_2x_2,c_2y_2).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Now the per-star scalar ledger is unchanged but `rho_A=2`.  The graph, its
edge count, and therefore the graph scalar `S_m` are identical in (2.1)
and (2.2).  This kills:

- summing one diagonal credit per coherent star;
- identifying an owned-diagonal incidence count with distinct A capacity;
- any canonical decomposition of the graph scalar `S_m` into precisely
  these colour-owned diagonals.

This endpoint model is a graph-realizable local incidence pattern and has
no C7.  It is not claimed to satisfy the complete hard BCM density and
`L_4(2)` contract.  Its role is the minimal overlap certificate.

## 3. Same A rank and S_m, different B reserve

There is also an exact hard-local pair at the full edge threshold.  Begin
with the fourteen-vertex graph from `M809_11_GRAPH_REALIZABILITY.md`:

```text
n=14, e=50, A=N[v], |B|=3
L4(2), minimum degree at least four, every C7 rainbow
D_B=3, rho_A=3
K(bc)={bc,cz}, so rho_B=M_B=2.
```

Form the second graph by deleting the B edge `bz` and restoring the
previously missing A edge `x1x2`.  This preserves `n`, `e`, `A`, `B`, all
three repeated colours, and all three owned diagonals.  Exact replay gives

```text
L4(2): pass
every C7 rainbow: pass
D_B=3, rho_A=3
K(bc)={bc,bz,cz}, so rho_B=M_B=3.
```

Because `n,e,|B|` are unchanged, the inherited graph scalar

\[
S_m=e-\binom{|B|}{2}-\Phi(n,e)
\]

is identical in both graphs.  Nevertheless the B reserve changes from
deficient to paid.  Thus neither `rho_A` nor `S_m`, separately or through
their unchanged scalar values, determines the B-reserve outcome.

Most importantly, the first graph is an exact certificate against treating
A and B atoms as one untyped capacity:

\[
D_B=3,\qquad \rho_A+\rho_B=5,
\qquad M_B=2<D_B.
\]

The inequality `D_B<=rho_A+rho_B` is true but cannot imply
`D_B<=M_B`; its three A units are not missing B edges.

These two graphs are hard-local witnesses, not public-problem
counterexamples.  Their colour count is high and no claim is made that the
complete outer-A hardness gate fails.

## 4. Why owned diagonals do not yet contribute to S_m

`S_m` is a global numerical slack.  It has no inherited atomization

\[
S_m=|R_S|
\]

with precommitted colour owner sets, and no map from A diagonals to such
atoms.  Consequently a putative rank

\[
\rho_B(T)+\min\{S_m,\rho_A(T)\}
\tag{4.1}
\]

may be a formally submodular number, but using it as charge capacity assumes
the missing conversion theorem.  Formula (4.1) does not construct distinct
B edges, does not decompose `S_m`, and does not prove the outer-A gate.

The exact next lemma would need one of the following forms:

1. **owned slack atomization:** construct a set of `S_m` actual or
   combinatorial slack atoms with colour owner sets, then use its coverage
   rank with `rho_B`;
2. **A-to-B conversion:** from a family of distinct owned A diagonals and
   their common rectangles, construct distinct new B-reserve edges;
3. **outer-gate conversion:** prove directly that the submodular A rank
   forces `R_A<=S_m` after all overlaps are removed.

Without one of these the pair rank (1.3) is descriptive only.  The inherited
rectangle-to-budget theorem is the relevant nonlinear global tool, but it
does not currently provide a colourwise or circuitwise conversion map.
