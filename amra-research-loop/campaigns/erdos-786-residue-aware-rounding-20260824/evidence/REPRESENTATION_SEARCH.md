# Representation search and three-family comparison

Twelve representations in five families were admitted.  The three required
families are deliberately not merged.

## 1. Arithmetic alteration/dependency compression

`R786R-01` records the exact intersection graph of bad events;
`R786R-02` repairs a whole connected prime-incidence polymer; `R786R-03`
tries to charge the repair to shared fractional mass.  RR.1 eliminates the
easy comparators: event support is unbounded even at active degree two, and a
forest component can itself be one minimal bad event.  The family survives
only in a full-component, arithmetic form.

## 2. Largest-prime ownership with cofactor residue

`R786R-04` stores the exact rational residue, `R786R-05` turns owners and
charges into a flow problem, and `R786R-06` tests whether divisor structure
splits the state.  RR.2 shows why residue is both necessary and dangerous:
one balanced top-prime pair transfers a ratio with arbitrary numerator and
denominator prime support.  A surviving owner theorem must amortize this
state; it cannot bound it by the top fibre size.

## 3. Recursive residue-token potential/coherent stabilization

`R786R-07` gives the exact transition, `R786R-08` separates global from local
complexity, and `R786R-09` seeks a telescoping multiscale potential.  RR.1
shows that token count, active degree, treewidth, and cycle rank do not by
themselves decrease.  `R786R-10` and `R786R-11` add the reserve/revision
state required for an infinite construction; RR.2 is their future-extension
test.  `R786R-12` supplies primal counterfamilies to all three routes.

No representation reopens a hard threshold, unaltered sampling,
residue-free peeling, fixed prime cylinder, or bounded-cutoff optimizer.
