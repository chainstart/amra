# Target selection: integral rounding after the log-defect interface

## Exact finite target

For `N>=2`, let `H_N` be the hypergraph on `V_N={2,...,N}` whose edges are
supports `S union T` of disjoint Finsets satisfying

\[
\prod_{s\in S}s=\prod_{t\in T}t,\qquad |S|\ne|T|.
\]

The primary target is

\[
\tau(H_N)=o(N).                                      \tag{T1}
\]

This is exactly the finite distinct-factor density-one assertion after the
forced deletion of `1`.  The campaign never permits multiplicities within a
shore.

## Frozen predecessor interface

The promoted predecessor campaign proved and independently audited

\[
w_N(n)=\frac{\log(N/n)}{\log N},\qquad
\sum_{n=2}^Nw_N(n)=(1+o(1))\frac N{\log N},          \tag{T2}
\]

with `sum_(n in E) w_N(n)>=1` for every `E in H_N`.  This campaign treats
(T2) as read-only.  Its task is the missing arithmetic integrality step, not
another proof of fractional feasibility.

## Infinite coherence target

A secondary route may use the same rounding decisions at all cutoffs.  It
counts only if it constructs, for every `epsilon>0`, one admissible set with
an actual natural density greater than `1-epsilon`.  Unrelated finite
transversals, nestedness without a density estimate, or endpoint density do
not suffice.

## Success and failure boundary

A full proof of (T1), a coherent rule closing the infinite statement, or an
independently auditable all-parameter theorem sharply resolving a frozen
rounding mechanism can meet a closure-contract success condition.  Generic
set-cover approximation, a fixed-length theorem, a larger finite optimizer,
or a conditional ownership/coarea lemma is non-success.

No public search for the exact problem is permitted.  The predecessor
campaign is immutable.
