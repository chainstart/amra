# Representation search

Twelve representations across nine mathematical families were kept.  They
are grouped by information retained, not by vocabulary.

## Weighted/coarea views

`R786I-01` keeps the full defect profile.  It is immediately exposed to the
balanced-padding theorem: a method that rounds only by a lower-tail cutoff,
or by finitely many nested cutoffs, has forgotten the circuit incidence and
cannot work.  Coarea remains relevant only if coupled to a prime-scale rank
or ownership increment.

## Prime-incidence and algebraic views

`R786I-02`, `R786I-03`, and `R786I-08` keep the signed valuation equations.
On the squarefree active part, integers become hyperedges on primes and a bad
relation is a balanced-degree signed subhypergraph with unequal numbers of
hyperedges.  A single active-degree stratum is automatically safe, but one
stratum cannot be assumed dense and adjacent strata already support complete
bipartite circuits.  Global rank alone is also too coarse: the rank-one
powers `2^k` contain linearly many disjoint Schur-triple obstructions in the
exponent interval, whereas one high-rank connected graph circuit costs one
deletion.

## Recursive arithmetic views

`R786I-04`, `R786I-05`, and `R786I-12` are distinct levels of the rough-part
recursion.  Removing the `y`-smooth and repeated-large-prime exceptions gives
a clean squarefree incidence object, but largest-prime balance alone does not
cancel the lower-prime cofactors.  The factor-tree view explicitly carries
those residues as tokens; its first decisive test is whether the state/load
stays sublogarithmic.

## Probabilistic views

`R786I-06` retains dependency between failure events, while `R786I-07`
assigns a concrete clock owner to every circuit.  Independent per-edge
probability estimates are rejected.  Either representation must output a
global expected load `o(N)` or an exact resampling/dependency theorem.

## Coherent and algorithmic views

`R786I-09` is the only representation aimed directly at the infinite
natural-density interface.  Finite prime cylinders must stabilize while
controlling cross-cylinder relations.  `R786I-10` instead attaches an exact
certificate to every greedy rejection; it succeeds only with a proved
bounded-load charging map.  `R786I-11` supplies adversarial primal packings
against all proposed charges.

No representation is credited merely for restating `tau(H_N)=o(N)` or the
fractional LP.  Every mechanism below has an explicit all-parameter kill
test.
