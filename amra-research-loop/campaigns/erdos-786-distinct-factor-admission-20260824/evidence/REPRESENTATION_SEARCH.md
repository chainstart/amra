# Representation search

The portfolio contains twelve representations in seven mathematical
families.  Its four required independent entrances are explicit:

1. signed prime-exponent `0/1` circuits (`R786-01,R786-02`);
2. auxiliary-element amplification and incidence realization
   (`R786-03,R786-04`);
3. scale and bounded-dimensional obstructions (`R786-05,R786-07`);
4. exact SAT/ILP completeness (`R786-09,R786-10`).

Large-prime peeling, private-prime Hall deficiency, and the two density
gluing models provide three further families.  They are not cosmetic
variants: they retain respectively prime order, certificate ownership, and
the cross-block relations lost by fixed-N optimization.

## Common adversarial atlas

Every mechanism is tested first on four exact models.

* `{2,4}` distinguishes Finset relations from arbitrary integer kernel
  relations.
* The exact-one prime layer exposes the `1/e` ceiling of a single additive
  level set.
* `K_(s+1,s)` with distinct edge primes gives an arbitrarily long minimal
  unbalanced squarefree circuit and defeats bounded-length compression.
* The two admissible blocks `{2,3}` and `{6}` have a bad union, so finite
  optimization does not glue without a multiplicative marker theorem.

Finite exact optimization is used only as a falsifier and motif generator.
No optimum through a fixed `N` is counted as asymptotic progress.

## Information-retention table

* Circuit representations retain coefficient alphabet and minimal support.
* Incidence representations retain an explicit prime for every equality
  constraint and make shore imbalance visible.
* High-tail filtration retains the precise factor-length range settled by
  magnitude.
* Large-prime peeling retains ordered fibre multiplicity rather than a
  private-label fantasy.
* ILP retains every finite forbidden support; its missing datum is a uniform
  cover/rounding theorem.
* Profinite and block gluing retain genuine natural-density quantifiers and
  cross-block interactions.

The registry deliberately blocks the totally additive-function route: it
belongs to the repetitions-allowed variant and fails already on `{2,4}`.
